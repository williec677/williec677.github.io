Three very different watches dropped this Wednesday, and every one of them has a reason to exist. Stockholm's **Maen** is turning its sold out jumping hour collab into a regular production watch for under four figures in euros. Marseille dive specialist **Bohen** launched a cheaper sub brand called **H by Bohen**, and its first watch is a compact 530 m diver named for a world record. And in Glashütte, **Moritz Grossmann** marked two anniversaries with a 26 piece dress watch that has a school building carved into its dial. Specs come from Monochrome, aBlogtoWatch and the brands' own sites, all checked on November 11, 2026.

## Maen Grand Tonneau Jump Hour: the collab becomes a catalog watch

Back in 2025, Maen teamed up with content creator Nico Leonard on a limited Jump Hour. According to [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/maen-grand-tonneau-jump-hour-permanent-collection-accessible-automatic-jumping-hour-watch-review-price/), it went well enough that the design is now joining the permanent collection, with four new dials, a display caseback and a lower price. Maen's own homepage confirms the return: on sale December 10, 16:00 CET.

The display is the whole show. A big numeral at 12 o'clock snaps to the next hour the instant the hour changes, and the minutes ride a rotating disc in a curved window below it. No hands at all. It reads like a dashboard from a 1970s concept car, and that is a good thing.

| Case | 316L steel tonneau, 34 mm wide, 45 mm lug to lug, 9.7 mm thick, brushed and polished |

| Price | **About $800 USD (EUR 719.20 before EU VAT; EUR 899 with VAT)**, down from EUR 999 (about $1,112 USD) with VAT for the Nico Leonard edition |

One useful detail for US buyers: Maen's store lists each of the four new references at EUR 719.20 before Swedish VAT, which works out to about $800 USD. What you finally pay depends on your country's import duties and taxes, so check the checkout total. Monochrome also reports that Maen tests every movement in house for two weeks before assembly.

### My take

A real mechanical jumping hour in a slim 34 mm tonneau for this money is a genuine conversation starter. Yes, it runs a modified Chinese movement and not a Swiss one, and that is exactly how Maen gets this complication under a grand. Wear the Copper if you want people to ask about it, the Anthracite if you want it to look like it costs triple. ## H by Bohen Plongeur 530: a record holder's diver

Bohen is a Marseille brand founded by designer Blaise Dominique Giuliani, and [aBlogtoWatch](https://www.ablogtowatch.com/the-h-by-bohen-plongeur-530-pays-tribute-to-a-saturation-diving-legend/), in a post it labels as sponsored, reports that the Plongeur 530 is the first watch from a new, cheaper sub brand called H by Bohen. The name is a tribute to Patrick Raude, the COMEX saturation diver who aBlogtoWatch says was one of a three man team that reached 530 m in open water saturation diving in 1988, a record that still stands. Raude has worn and tested Bohen's pro divers since the start, which is why the water resistance is exactly 530 m.

| | H by Bohen Plongeur 530 |
|---|---|
| Case | 39 mm, 46.5 mm lug to lug, 12.6 mm thick, brushed lugs and polished mid case |
| Water resistance | 530 m, rose gold tone helium escape valve between the lugs |
| Bezel | 60 click ceramic |
| Options | Crown at 12 o'clock or 6 o'clock, Black/Gray or Vintage dial, four versions in total |
| Movement | Soprod P24 automatic |
| Bracelet | Steel with tool free removable links and a milled clasp with tool free fine adjustment, plus a black FKM strap in the box |
| Price | **About $1,668 USD (EUR 1,499 including all taxes)** launch offer, preorder now, deliveries scheduled for Christmas 2026 |

The price and delivery timing match [Bohen's own Plongeur 530 page](https://www.bohen-watches.com/en/plongeur530), which calls EUR 1,499 a launching offer, so don't assume it stays there. Giuliani told aBlogtoWatch he picked the Soprod P24 even though it costs Bohen more than a Sellita SW200, because its architecture lets the brand make the changes it needs for extreme pressure.

### My take

A 39 mm diver rated deeper than most 44 mm monsters, with a vertical crown that will never stab your wrist, is a smart package. The real flex is the bracelet: tool free links and on the fly adjustment are things plenty of watches at twice the price skip. ## Moritz Grossmann Benu 1878: 26 pieces for the school that started it all

Karl Moritz Grossmann was born in 1826, and the revived brand was relaunched on November 11, 2008, so today is a double birthday. [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/moritz-grossmann-celebrates-its-founders-200th-birthday-and-the-18th-anniversary-of-the-brands-revival-with-the-benu-1878-introducing-price/) reports the Benu 1878 is the eighth anniversary piece dedicated to him. The name points to 1878, the year Grossmann founded the German School of Watchmaking in Glashütte, and the dial shows the school's main building in three dimensional relief engraving on German silver.

The rest is pure Saxon dress watch. A 41 mm by 11.35 mm polished steel case, a railway minute track, small seconds at 6 and hand made lance hands heat annealed to a brown violet color. Through the sapphire back you get the manual wind in house **caliber 100.1**: 198 parts, 38 jewels, 2.5 Hz, 42 hours of reserve, an untreated German silver 2/3 plate, gold chatons and hand engraved floral cocks. It also keeps the brand's pusher based winding system, with a movement restart pusher at 4 o'clock. It comes on brown hand stitched kudu leather with a steel buckle.

**Limited to 26 pieces at EUR 38,500 excluding tax, about $42,839 USD.** Moritz Grossmann did not publish a US price. Twenty six pieces is a tiny run, so if this one speaks to you, talk to a dealer now.
