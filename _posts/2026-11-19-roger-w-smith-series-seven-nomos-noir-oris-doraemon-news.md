Some watch days are about value, some are about fun, and today has both, but the headline is one of the most respected watchmakers alive celebrating 25 years in business. Roger W. Smith, the Isle of Man watchmaker who learned under George Daniels, just unveiled the Series Seven, a tourbillon annual calendar that ties his whole career together. Then we come back down to earth with Nomos putting three of its smallest classics in black and gold, and Oris giving the Big Crown Pointer Date a Doraemon makeover. Let's get into it.

## Roger W. Smith Series Seven

**Announced:** November 19, 2026 ([Roger W. Smith](https://rwsmithwatches.com/news-collection/roger-w-smith-announces-the-series-seven), [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/the-roger-w-smith-series-7-annual-calendar-tourbillon), [SJX](https://watchesbysjx.com/2026/11/roger-smith-series-seven-tourbillon.html))

### A 25th birthday watch with deep roots

Roger W. Smith says the Series Seven was unveiled at the 25th Anniversary Owners' Dinner at the V&A in London, and the design is a love letter to two watches. The first is his own Pocket Watch No. 2, the offset dial piece that, per the brand, led George Daniels to tell him in 1997, "Congratulations, you are now a watchmaker." The second is the Daniels Millennium wristwatch, which Smith first saw on Daniels' wrist when he joined the workshop the following year. Hodinkee points out that Smith's own Millennium, the only one double signed by Smith and Daniels, sold for over $2.4 million at Sotheby's in 2023, and the No. 2 pocket watch sold at Phillips New York in 2023 for $4.9 million. That is the lineage this watch is speaking to.

### What it does

On the dial you get hours, minutes and seconds on an offset main dial, the date on a sub dial at six o'clock, and a numerical month display beneath twelve. The big change is the seconds: this is the first Roger Smith watch with a center seconds hand. The brand says every previous movement used subsidiary seconds, and making the switch demanded a completely different movement architecture.

The calendar is an annual calendar, so it handles 30 and 31 day months by itself and only needs a correction at the end of February. SJX explains that Smith built it the traditional way a perpetual calendar is built, just without leap year tracking, and notes that the month can be corrected independently with a pin pusher in the case band. SJX also flags a quirk worth knowing: per its reading, turning the time backwards past midnight looks like it will advance the date rather than do nothing.

### The movement

Roger W. Smith describes an entirely new calibre, conceived during lockdown, that is smaller and slimmer than his four Foundation era tourbillons. It has twin barrels and a one minute tourbillon incorporating the coaxial escapement, the low friction escapement Daniels invented. SJX reports it uses Daniels' slim line version of the coaxial rather than Smith's own single wheel design, and describes a free sprung balance adjusted with inertia blocks. The brand did not list technical figures in its announcement, but Hodinkee and SJX both report manual winding, 21,600 vph (3 Hz) and 48 hours of power reserve, with the two barrels there to deliver steadier torque rather than a longer reserve.

The finishing is classic Smith: English graining, a fully black polished tourbillon carriage, and on the prototype, a third wheel bridge engraved "MMXXVI Twenty Five Years of RWS."

### Case, metals and availability

The case is 40 mm across and 14.6 mm thick per Hodinkee and SJX. That is chunky for a dress watch, and SJX frames it as a feature of the sturdy British approach. The launch prototype is platinum with a silver engine turned dial. Hodinkee reports the first two prototypes are platinum and rose gold and that the watch can be made in any precious metal and customized on request, while SJX lists red gold or platinum, so confirm metal options with the workshop. Water resistance has not been published.

| Spec | Roger W. Smith Series Seven |
|---|---|
| Case | 40 mm x 14.6 mm, precious metal (launch prototype in platinum) |
| Dial | Silver engine turned on the prototype, offset hours and minutes, center seconds, date at six, numerical month beneath twelve |
| Movement | New calibre, manual winding, twin barrels, one minute tourbillon with coaxial escapement, 3 Hz, 48 h (per Hodinkee and SJX) |
| Complications | Annual calendar, tourbillon |
| Water resistance | Not published |
| Limited edition | No, but very small production |
| Price | **On request** |

**On the price:** Roger W. Smith has not published a price, and Hodinkee and SJX both list it as on request. SJX mentions a rumoured seven figure price tag. That is a rumor reported by SJX, not a figure confirmed by the brand, so treat it as such. Hodinkee says its understanding is that the workshop will make only about two a year, and the brand itself says only a small number will ever be made.

### My take

This is the watch Roger Smith fans have been waiting for. It is not just a tourbillon, it is a career summary: the No. 2 pocket watch layout, the Millennium dial, the coaxial escapement, and a brand new movement with his first ever center seconds. Will most of us ever touch one? No. But this is why independent watchmaking matters, and it is a perfect follow up to a big month for independents, from the [GPHG 2026 winners]({% post_url 2026-11-07-gphg-2026-winners-rexhep-rexhepi-aiguille-dor-news %}) to yesterday's [Algorithme du Temps wandering seconds prototype]({% post_url 2026-11-18-merci-super-algorithme-du-temps-reveries-des-secondes-news %}). My only wish list item: a simpler tourbillon only version, which Hodinkee also hints this movement could make possible down the road.

## Also new: Nomos Noir series

**Announced:** November 19, 2026 ([Nomos](https://nomos-glashuette.com/en-us/store), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/nomos-noir-series-tangente-ludwig-tertra-hand-wound-steel-or-gold-review-price/))

![Nomos Tangente 33 noir in stainless steel with black dial, white Arabic numerals, gold hands and small seconds at six on an anthracite velour strap]({{ "/assets/img/watches/nomos-tangente-33-noir.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Nomos Glashütte (Tangente 33 noir)*

Nomos calls it the little black dress idea applied to watches, and honestly it works. The Noir series takes the smallest versions of three icons, the Tangente 33, Ludwig 33 and Tetra 27, and gives them black galvanized dials with white printing and gold hands, in either steel or 18k yellow gold. That makes six references in the permanent collection.

* **Tangente 33 noir:** the Bauhaus one, 32.8 mm x 6.6 mm with Arabic numerals.
* **Ludwig 33 noir:** softer and more classic, same 32.8 mm x 6.6 mm with Roman numerals.
* **Tetra 27 noir:** the square, 27.5 mm x 27.5 mm and just 6.4 mm thick, with Arabic numerals.

All three use the in house hand wound DUW 4001 with the Nomos Swing System, a 53 hour power reserve and stop seconds, plus a small seconds at six. Per Monochrome, every model has a sapphire crystal and 30 m of water resistance, the steel versions come with a closed or sapphire caseback and ride on an anthracite velour strap, and the gold versions always get a sapphire back and a black Horween Genuine Shell Cordovan strap.

**US prices (Nomos US store, listing pages):**

|---|---|---|
| Tangente 33 noir | from $2,210 USD (ref. 152 / 152.SB) | $12,500 USD (ref. 106) |
| Ludwig 33 noir | $2,170 USD (ref. 226 / 227) | $12,500 USD (ref. 220) |
| Tetra 27 noir | $2,460 USD (ref. 416 / 416.GB) | $12,700 USD (ref. 420) |

**Price note:** those are the figures on the Nomos US store listing pages this morning. The individual Tangente 33 noir product page showed $2,500 USD for the configuration we viewed, and the store lists a 7 week delivery time on it, so confirm the exact price for your caseback choice at checkout.

**My take:** small watches are having a moment, and a 6.4 mm thick black and gold Tetra is about as elegant as a sub $2,500 USD manual wind gets. These are small, so try one on if you are used to 40 mm sports watches. ## Also new: Oris Big Crown Pointer Date Doraemon Limited Edition

**Announced:** November 19, 2026 ([Oris](https://www.oris.ch/en-US/product/watch/big-crown/new-big-crown-0/01-754-7798-4085-Set), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/oris-doraemon-limited-edition-big-crown-pointer-date-manga-inspired-video-review-price/))

![Oris Big Crown Pointer Date Doraemon Limited Edition with light blue brushed dial, red minute track, yellow tipped pointer date hand and seconds hand on a stainless steel bracelet]({{ "/assets/img/watches/oris-big-crown-pointer-date-doraemon.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Oris (ref. 01 754 7798 4085-Set)*

After Kermit, Miss Piggy and Gonzo, Oris is going to Japan. The new Doraemon Limited Edition, ref. 01 754 7798 4085-Set, turns the Big Crown Pointer Date into a tribute to the blue robot cat from the 22nd century. The touches are smart rather than cartoonish: a vertically brushed light blue dial for Doraemon's body, a red minute track for his collar, and a yellow seconds hand and pointer date tip inspired by his Hopter, the propeller gadget he uses to fly. The screwed caseback is engraved with Doraemon in flight, and the box includes a microfiber cloth inspired by his Time Kerchief.

Specs per Oris: 40 mm multi piece steel case, sapphire domed on both sides with inside anti reflective coating, 5 bar water resistance, 20 mm lug width, and the automatic Calibre 754-1 (Sellita SW200-1 base per Monochrome) at 4 Hz with 41 hours of power reserve, instantaneous pointer date and stop seconds. Monochrome lists it at 12 mm thick and 47.5 mm lug to lug. It comes on a steel bracelet with a folding clasp, and it is limited to 1,000 pieces.

**Price:** **$2,750 USD** on the Oris US site (CHF 2,250 in Switzerland). When we checked this morning, the Oris US online store showed the model as currently not available with a notify me option, while US retailer Mayors listed it at the same $2,750 USD, so your best bet may be an authorized dealer. The regular steel Big Crown Pointer Date sits at $2,400 USD on the same page, so the Doraemon premium is $350 USD.

**My take:** this is my favorite Oris character watch so far because it is the most wearable. Strip away the story and it is still a great light blue pointer date.
