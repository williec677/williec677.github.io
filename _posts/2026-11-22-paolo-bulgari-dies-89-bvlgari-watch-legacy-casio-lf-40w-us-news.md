The watch world lost one of the people who taught a jewelry house how to tell time. Paolo Bulgari, chairman of the Bvlgari Group since 1984 and grandson of the founder, died in Rome at 89, the company announced on Saturday, November 21. Most of the obituaries focus on the gemstones, the Hollywood red carpets and Elizabeth Taylor. Fair enough. But if you are a watch person, his years at the top cover the exact stretch when Bvlgari went from a Roman jeweler that sold a few watches to a serious watchmaker breaking world records. Here is what is confirmed, what he leaves behind on the wrist, and one fun Casio release to close out the weekend.

## Paolo Bulgari, 1937 to 2026

**Reported:** November 21, 2026 ([WWD](https://wwd.com/accessories-news/jewelry/paolo-bulgari-chairman-jewelry-roman-house-dies-rome-1239461393/), [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c8e39qk5vrpdo), [People](https://people.com/paolo-bulgari-dead-italian-luxury-designer-bvlgari-chairman-12270231))

What the reporting confirms:

* **Who he was:** born in Rome in 1937 (People, citing Bvlgari), the grandson of Sotirio Bulgari, the Greek silversmith born Sotirios Voulgaris who founded the business in Rome in 1884 (WWD and BBC).
* **His career:** he started working with his father Giorgio in 1960, specializing in precious stones. Bvlgari calls him one of the world's leading experts in gems. He became chairman in 1984 and, per WWD, kept that role through the LVMH takeover in 2011 and until his death.
* **The tributes:** Bvlgari called him "a true creative soul" and closed its statement with "Thank you, Mr. Paolo." LVMH chairman Bernard Arnault called him "a key architect of the brand's success," per WWD.
* **What is not known:** no cause of death has been disclosed. The BBC reports the funeral is set for Monday, November 23, at the Basilica of Santa Croce in Gerusalemme in Rome.

He also leaves a personal mark outside the business. In 2019 he and his wife set up the Paolo Bulgari Foundation to support young people with schooling and job training on the outskirts of Rome, per the BBC.

## His watch legacy, in four moments

WWD reports that under his leadership in the 1970s and 1980s, the house broadened beyond jewelry into watches, leather goods, accessories and fragrances. For collectors, that is the part of the story that still sits on wrists today.

**1975: the BULGARI ROMA gift.** According to Bvlgari's own heritage pages, the house sent its top 100 clients a limited edition gold digital watch engraved "BULGARI ROMA" on the bezel for Christmas 1975. No ad campaign. Just a gift that got people talking at the Via Condotti store.

**1977: Bvlgari Bvlgari.** That buzz led to the 1977 launch of the Bvlgari Bvlgari, with the double logo engraved right into the bezel. Bvlgari says the idea came from ancient Roman coins, where the emperor's portrait was ringed by inscriptions, and that the cylindrical case drew on the columns of Roman temples. Putting the brand name on the bezel as the design itself was a bold move in the 70s, and it became one of the most recognizable watch designs of its era. That is the watch in the photo up top.

**2000: buying real watchmaking.** Bvlgari bought the Gérald Genta and Daniel Roth watch businesses from Singapore's The Hour Glass in 2000, as reported by [The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB962220838695098198). That deal brought high complication know how in house, from the designer who drew the Royal Oak and the Nautilus, at a time when Paolo Bulgari was chairman.

**2014 onward: the Octo Finissimo.** The ultra thin Octo Finissimo arrived in 2014 and turned Bvlgari into the name to beat in thinness. [Haute Time](https://www.hautetime.com/bulgari-octo-finissimos-most-unusual-and-rare-limited-editions/) counts 10 world records in 11 years across tourbillons, a minute repeater, automatics, a chronograph GMT and a perpetual calendar.

**My take:** I am not going to pretend Paolo Bulgari personally sketched every watch on that list. The reporting credits him with the gems and the overall Bvlgari look, and credits his era with the push into watches. But the throughline is obvious: bold shapes, Roman references and a refusal to look like a Parisian or Genevan house. That attitude is why a Bvlgari Bvlgari from the 80s still looks confident today, and why the Octo reads as Roman architecture on the wrist. If you have ever wanted a vintage Bvlgari Bvlgari, now is a good moment to learn the references, because tributes like this tend to send collectors searching. Buy on condition and papers, not on the headline.

## Also this weekend: Casio LF-40W reaches the US

**Released in the US:** November 21, 2026 ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Casio-s-affordable-retro-LF-40W-digital-watches-with-gadget-style-display-launch-in-the-US.1456008.0.html), [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/21/casio-lf-40w-us-launch/), [Gizmochina](https://www.gizmochina.com/2026/11/22/casio-retro-inspired-lf40w-digital-watches-launched-in-the-us/))

![Three Casio LF-40W digital watches in black, blue and light gray resin on a mixing console]({{ "/assets/img/watches/casio-lf-40w.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Casio via Notebookcheck (LF-40W-1A, LF-40W-2A and LF-40W-8A)*

From Roman gold to a $43 resin digital. That is the range of this hobby, and I love it. When Casio launched the LF-40W in Japan earlier this month, we passed because there was no US price. Now there is. The tall display looks like an audio equalizer: time on top, then three rows of bars. The first row shows whether the alarm, hourly chime, button mute and daylight saving are on. The two rows below count seconds, one in 10 second steps and one in single seconds, so the lower half fills and empties every minute.

* **References:** LF-40W-1A (black), LF-40W-2A (blue) and LF-40W-8A (the light version; reports differ on whether to call it gray or beige)
* **Case:** resin, 37.8 x 33.7 x 8.6 mm, 23 g, resin crystal, basic water resistance with no depth rating
* **Strap:** bio based resin, fits 125 to 200 mm wrists
* **Functions:** world time for 48 cities, four city multi time, 1/100 second stopwatch, 24 hour countdown timer, five daily alarms, hourly signal, amber LED backlight, auto calendar to 2099
* **Power and accuracy:** CR1616 battery rated for about three years, ±30 seconds per month

**Price and availability:** **$42.95 USD** each on Casio's US store, available now, per Notebookcheck and The Gadgeteer.

**My take:** this is a fun, weird, cheap digital that looks like nothing else in the Casio catalog, and that is the whole point. It will not replace your F-91W, but it is a great gift for the music nerd in your life.
