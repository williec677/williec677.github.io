Friday's watch news stretches from a silent Omega drop to a French manufacture moonphase to a pure analog G-Shock landing in the US today. Omega put blue ceramic with titanium and brown ceramic with Sedna Gold Seamaster Diver 300M models on its site without a press circus. Yema opened a new dress collection with a glowing sculpted moon. And Casio's G-Shock 2500 Series, first announced in October, hits gshock.casio.com this morning. I checked every price and spec below against the brand or a named outlet on November 6, 2026.

## Omega Seamaster Diver 300M: blue ceramic and brown ceramic, no press release

Omega did not throw a launch party. The two new 43.5mm ceramic Seamasters simply appeared on the brand site and in the trade press, which is how Fratello and Gear Patrol broke the story ([Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/omega-silently-introduces-two-new-seamaster-diver-300m-models-in-blue-and-brown-ceramic/), [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/omega-secret-seamaster-diver-ceramic/)).

**Blue ceramic with Grade 5 titanium, ref. 210.92.44.20.03.001.** Per [Omega US](https://www.omegawatches.com/en-us/watch-omega-seamaster-diver-300m-co-axial-master-chronometer-43-5-mm-21092442003001), the case and laser engraved wave dial are blue ceramic, the diving scale on the unidirectional bezel is white enamel, and the skeletonized hands are titanium PVD with white Super-LumiNova. It sits on an integrated blue rubber strap with a Grade 5 titanium pin buckle. Weight is about 106g. US retail is $10,000. Fratello lists EUR 9,700 including VAT.

**Brown ceramic with 18K Sedna Gold, ref. 210.62.44.20.13.001.** Per [Omega US](https://www.omegawatches.com/en-us/watch-omega-seamaster-diver-300m-co-axial-master-chronometer-43-5-mm-21062442013001), brown ceramic covers the case and wave dial, the bezel scale is Ceragold, and the skeletonized hands are Sedna Gold with white lume. Dial markings mix white and Sedna Gold. The brown rubber strap closes with a brown ceramic pin buckle. Weight is about 122g. US retail is $15,500. Fratello lists EUR 15,000 including VAT. This sits next to the earlier black ceramic and Sedna Gold ref. 210.62.44.20.01.001, so think of it as a warmer color sibling rather than a clean sheet design.

Both share the same skeleton: 43.5mm diameter, 51.5mm lug to lug, 14.27mm thick, 21mm between the lugs, domed sapphire with AR on both sides, helium escape valve, screw in crown, 300m water resistance, and the Co-Axial Master Chronometer calibre 8806 (55 hour reserve, 15,000 gauss, silicon balance spring, METAS certified). That matches Omega's pages. Ignore the "8006" typo that slipped into Fratello's spec table. The body of that article and Omega both say 8806.

My take? The blue one is the talker. Full blue ceramic with titanium hardware is a clean, modern Seamaster look that the steel wave dials cannot match. The brown and Sedna Gold piece will split the room, but brown ceramic divers are rare and the No Time to Die era proved collectors will chase a warm Seamaster. Size is the honest caveat. At 43.5 x 14.27mm these are not the smaller, thinner 300M people keep asking Omega for. If you want one, buy from an AD or Omega so the five year warranty is clean, and use {% include aff.html network="ebay" url="https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw=omega+seamaster+diver+300m+ceramic&LH_Sold=1&LH_Complete=1" text="sold ceramic Seamaster 300M listings on eBay" %} to see what the older black and white ceramic pieces actually trade for before you trust any asking price.

## Yema Senans Grande Lune: French manufacture moonphase from EUR 2,470

Yema spent years proving it can build real tool watches and micro rotor movements in Morteau. The new Senans collection is the dress chapter, and the Grande Lune is the headline: the brand's first moonphase, in a slim round case, with a moon carved from solid Super-LumiNova ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/yema-senans-grand-lune-cmm-22-micro-rotor-new-dress-watch-collection-review-price/), [Time and Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/yema-senans-grande-lune-cmm-22-hands-on/)).

The case is 38.5mm across, 8.7mm thick (crystal not counted the same way in every write up), and 46.4mm lug to lug in 316L steel, with a stepped mirror polished bezel, brushed flanks, double domed sapphire, sapphire caseback, 50m water resistance, and 20mm lugs. The name nods to the Royal Saltworks of Arc-et-Senans near Yema's workshops. That semicircle shows up in the off center moon aperture at 7 o'clock.

The moon itself is a stationary, domed block of Grade X Super-LumiNova with crater detail. A thin rotating mechanical bilobe sweeps over it to show the phases, instead of a painted disc crawling under a window. The center dial gets a stamped barleycorn pattern, the chapter ring is circular brushed with luminous applied batons, and the hands and framing rings also carry lume. Three dial colors ship: black, blue, and golden (Time and Tide calls the warm one champagne).

Inside is calibre CMM.22, Yema's CMM.20 micro rotor base with the moonphase module. Movement height is 4.7mm, frequency 28,800 vph, 70 hour power reserve, and a charcoal electroplated finish with polished screws. Each dial color is limited to 200 pieces. Price is EUR 2,470 on leather or EUR 2,570 on mesh, per Monochrome and Time and Tide. No official US dollar figure was published in those write ups, so convert carefully and confirm on [yema.com](https://www.yema.com) before you buy.

My take? A manufacture micro rotor moonphase under EUR 2,600 with a glowing 3D moon is a strong value story for anyone who likes French indie watchmaking. The 7 o'clock aperture is unusual, and that is the point. If you want a dressier Yema instead of another Superman, this is the one to shortlist. ## G-Shock 2500 Series: all analog toughness, US sale today from $145

Casio announced the G-Shock 2500 Series on October 22 as the first purely analog models in the current G-Shock Basic lineup, no digital window ([Casio America PR](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/g-shock-toughness-goes-analog-the-new-2500-series-reimagines-the-watch-that-started-it-all-302916022.html)). Japan launch is November 7. The US web release is today, November 6, on gshock.casio.com, with the Soho store expected November 7 ([G-Central](https://www.g-central.com/g-shock-gac2500-and-gmc2500-watches-will-be-released-this-friday-11-6-in-the-u-s/)). Typical Casio US drop windows run about 10 AM to noon ET, so if you are reading this early, set the alert now.

Three tiers cover the lineup. Resin GAC-2500 models (black, green, white, yellow) are $145. Metal accent GMC-2500 models run $280 to $300. Full bracelet GMC-2500D models are $350. Case size is roughly 46.7mm tall by about 43.8 to 44.5mm wide and 12.7 to 12.9mm thick, so it wears nearer a DW-5600 than a GA-2100. Specs that matter: new analog module with small seconds at 9, Multi-Mission Date disc at 3 that also handles chronograph minutes and setting modes, full auto calendar through 2099, magnetic hand holding for shock resistance, second hand stop for up to five year battery life, LED light, 200m water resistance, and a 1 second stopwatch. Casio left the alarm out on purpose to keep the analog dial clean.

Japan preorders already cleared most colorways except white and yellow resin, per G-Central, so US stock may move fast on the metal pieces. If you already own a square G-Shock and want a strap swap playground, my [DW-5600 Strap Finder page](/strap-finder/casio-g-shock-dw-5600/) and {% include aff.html network="amazon" url="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=g-shock+dw5600+strap" text="DW-5600 straps on Amazon" %} stay useful for the classic case. The 2500 is a different shape, so buy the watch first, then measure.

My take? This is the G-Shock story of the week for anyone who wants toughness without a digital panel. At $145 the resin pieces are a no brainer try on. At $350 the bracelet versions are where the "quiet refinement" pitch either sticks or does not. Either way, today is the day to decide before the Japan style sellouts hit the US site.
