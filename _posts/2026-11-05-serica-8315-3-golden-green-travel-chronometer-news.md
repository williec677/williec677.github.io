French micro brand Serica just gave its Travel Chronometer a new suit, and the color is the whole story. Reference 8315-3 Golden Green pairs an olive green enamel dial with a two tone ceramic GMT bezel in "golden green" and ivory, keeps the same 39mm steel case and COSC certified Soprod C125 caller GMT, and lands at EUR 2,090 on the brand site. I checked every spec and price below against [Serica](https://serica-watches.com/en/products/8315-3-travel-chronometer) and named outlets on November 5, 2026.

## Why this colorway matters

The 8315 Travel Chronometer first landed in 2022 as Serica's function first GMT: no date, a clear dial, and a bidirectional 24 hour bezel built for travel rather than desk diving ([Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/serica-introduces-the-8315-3-golden-green-travel-chronometer/)). The Golden Green edition does not reinvent the architecture. It changes the palette.

The bezel is the hard part. Serica cuts the two tone insert from a single block of ceramic, with asymmetrical day and night sections so the numerals stay whole instead of getting sliced at the color break ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/the-new-serica-travel-chronometer-golden-green-ref-8315-3/), [Time and Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/serica-ref8315-03-golden-green-introducing/)). Brand copy calls the new shade "golden green" against ivory. In the metal it reads somewhere between soft khaki and coffee, which is why photos struggle with it ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/the-new-serica-travel-chronometer-golden-green-ref-8315-3/)). Serica itself says each new ceramic color takes months of trial and error ([Time and Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/serica-ref8315-03-golden-green-introducing/)).

The dial matches the bezel's warmth: olive green enamel with yellow gold accents around the markers and printing. Domed indices and the white lacquered broad arrow hands get cream toned Super-LumiNova C3, and the lollipop GMT hand keeps a burnt orange tip so you can find home time fast ([Serica](https://serica-watches.com/en/products/8315-3-travel-chronometer), [Time and Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/serica-ref8315-03-golden-green-introducing/)). Branding stays quiet. The dial text that stands out is COSC chronometer certification and 200 meters of water resistance, printed in gold.

## Specs from the brand

Per [Serica's product page](https://serica-watches.com/en/products/8315-3-travel-chronometer), the 316L steel case measures 39mm across, 12.30mm thick including the crystal, and 46.5mm lug to lug, with a 20mm lug width. Finishes mix brushed surfaces and polished accents on lyre lugs. A 2mm double domed sapphire crystal with inner anti reflective coating covers the dial, the crown is screw down at 8mm, the back is solid and screwed, and water resistance is 20 ATM (200 meters).

Inside is the Swiss automatic Soprod C125: 23 jewels, 28,800 vph, Côtes de Genève finishing, shock resistance, and COSC chronometer certification. Serica lists a 40 hour power reserve. Some press write ups say 42 hours ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/the-new-serica-travel-chronometer-golden-green-ref-8315-3/), [Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/serica-introduces-the-8315-3-golden-green-travel-chronometer/)); I am sticking with the brand's 40 hour figure. It is a caller style GMT, so the independent 24 hour hand sets the second zone while local hours and minutes stay put. That is simpler than a flyer GMT like Tudor's Black Bay GMT, and it is exactly how most travel callers work ([Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/serica-introduces-the-8315-3-golden-green-travel-chronometer/)).

It ships on Serica's Expedition bracelet: woven, folded, and machined 316L steel mesh with integrated vertically guilloché end links and a signed spring loaded clasp. One size covers wrists from 14.5cm to 22cm, with a maximum palm circumference of 25cm ([Serica](https://serica-watches.com/en/products/8315-3-travel-chronometer)).

## Price, variants, and availability

**Reference:** 8315-3 (Serica and Monochrome; Time and Tide writes 8315-03 for the same watch).

**Price:** EUR 2,090 on [serica-watches.com](https://serica-watches.com/en/products/8315-3-travel-chronometer). Monochrome reports USD 2,450 alongside that euro figure ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/the-new-serica-travel-chronometer-golden-green-ref-8315-3/)). Worn & Wound listed about $2,342, so treat the dollar number as an outlet conversion and buy against the brand's EUR 2,090 if you are ordering direct.

**Availability:** Permanent collection, immediate. Serica currently shows the 3 o'clock crown variant in stock and the 9 o'clock crown as notify me only. Shipping is listed at 5 to 10 days for in stock pieces, with a two year international warranty ([Serica](https://serica-watches.com/en/products/8315-3-travel-chronometer)).

My take? At roughly the price of a loaded Seiko Prospex or a mid tier Christopher Ward, you get COSC, 200 meters, a ceramic GMT bezel that took real R&D, and a bracelet that actually fits a wide range of wrists. The Golden Green look is opinionated, which is the point. If you want quiet black tool watch energy, look at the earlier 8315 colorways instead. If you want this shade, order before the ceramic batch sells through.
