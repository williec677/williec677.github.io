Wednesday morning brought a little of everything: an Italian oddball that keeps getting better, a young Geneva maison opening its first boutique with real stone dials, and a Japanese solar moonphase that finally has a US price. Let's go.

## Gagà Laboratorio Labormatic Arabia

**Announced:** November 25, 2026 ([Time+Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/gaga-laboratorio-labormatic-arabia-introducing/), [Time+Tide Shop](https://shop.timeandtidewatches.com/products/gaga-labormatic-arabia), [uhren-nerd](https://www.uhren-nerd.de/en/gaga-laboratorio-labormatic-arabia-limited-edition-watch/), [Watchonista](https://www.watchonista.com/editorial/novelties/gaga-laboratorio-labormatic-arabia-limited-edition/))

If you have never seen a Labormatic, here is the deal. The Italian brand moves the crown to 12 o'clock between the lugs, shows the hours on a rotating disc in a trapezoid window up top and reads the minutes with a single short central hand. It looks like nothing else, and it gives Gagà a canvas for wild color and material combos. The new **Labormatic Arabia** takes that canvas to the desert at night.

What's new:

* **Desert dial:** a gold colored dial base with a granular, sand like finish, split into four sectors of sparkling blue aventurine, each framed by a raised silver toned surround.
* **Eastern Arabic numerals:** the rotating hour disc uses dark blue Eastern Arabic numerals on gold.
* **Cabochons:** twelve domed blue cabochon indices mark every five minutes, and the open tip of the minute hand frames each one as it passes.
* **Case details:** a gold colored IP insert runs around the case and along the lugs, and the 12 o'clock crown gets a blue aventurine cabochon.
* **Calligraphy caseback:** the sapphire back carries the Gagà logo redrawn in Arabic calligraphy style, exclusive to this edition.

![Gagà Laboratorio Labormatic Arabia caseback showing the La Joux-Perret G100 automatic and calligraphy logo engraving]({{ "/assets/img/watches/gaga-laboratorio-labormatic-arabia-caseback.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Gagà Laboratorio via Time+Tide*

### Specs

* **Reference:** LMAR-001 (per uhren-nerd and Watchonista)
* **Case:** stainless steel with gold colored IP insert, 42 mm, 13.3 mm thick
* **Crystals:** sapphire front and back
* **Water resistance:** 50 m
* **Movement:** Swiss La Joux-Perret G100 automatic, 68 hours, custom rotor on a ceramic ball bearing
* **Strap:** sand colored woven fabric with tonal stitching, steel pin buckle
* **Edition:** 100 pieces

**Price and availability:** **$5,250 USD** on the Time+Tide Shop, which says it will carry the watch in the US from early December 2026, along with the Time+Tide Studio in New York City. The brand price is CHF 4,300 excluding tax (Time+Tide, uhren-nerd); Watchonista lists CHF 4,650, which lines up with that figure plus Swiss VAT. uhren-nerd says it can also be ordered on Gagà's own site. Heads up: Time+Tide is both the outlet that introduced the watch and its US retailer.

**My take:** the Labormatic is one of the few watches in this price zone that nobody mistakes for anything else, and this is the most refined version yet. Aventurine plus sand gold is a strong combo, and putting Eastern Arabic numerals on the hour disc is a smart, respectful touch instead of a gimmick. $5,250 USD for a 100 piece LJP powered oddity is serious money, but if you collect design first, this one earns its slot.

## Akhor AK-10 Boulder Opal and Chrysocolla boutique editions

**Announced:** November 25, 2026 ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/akhor-opens-its-first-geneva-boutique-and-celebrates-with-two-exclusive-stone-dial-editions/), [uhren-nerd](https://www.uhren-nerd.de/en/akhor-ak-10-boulder-opal-chrysocolla-stone-dial-watch/), [Akhor](https://akhor.ch))

![Akhor AK-10 boutique editions: steel with boulder opal dial and 4N/5N gold with chrysocolla dial, both on white alligator straps]({{ "/assets/img/watches/akhor-ak-10-boulder-opal-chrysocolla.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Akhor via Monochrome*

Akhor is a young Geneva brand founded by Anissa Bader just over a year ago, per Monochrome, built around a patented "floating dial." A solid upper disc carrying the central seconds hand appears to hover above a lower plate, with the indices on the lower level and only the tips of the hour and minute hands peeking out from underneath. The architecture was engineered by Clamax, Akhor's production partner. Now the brand has opened its first flagship boutique at 3 rue du Rhône in Geneva and is celebrating with two editions where that upper disc is cut from real stone.

* **AK-10-OPB:** steel case with a natural **boulder opal** upper disc from Queensland, Australia. The opal stays in its brown ironstone host rock, so you get flashes of blue, green and red across a dark background.
* **AK-10-AU-CHY:** 4N/5N gold case (a pink leaning toward red gold) with a **chrysocolla** upper disc, an intense cyan and turquoise copper mineral with dark inclusions that Monochrome compares to an aerial map of seas and coastlines.

Because every disc is cut from natural stone, every dial is different.

### Specs

* **Case:** cushion shape, 39 mm, 11 mm thick, sapphire front with internal AR, sapphire caseback, 30 m
* **Dial:** dual disc construction, stone upper disc, white sunray lower disc with applied nickel (steel) or 4N/5N gold (gold) markers, hands with luminous tips
* **Movement:** caliber AK10, manual winding, made for Akhor by Clamax, 31.3 mm x 3.9 mm, 117 components, 12 jewels, variable inertia balance, 4 Hz, 60 hours, hacking seconds
* **Certification:** Poinçon de Genève (Geneva Seal) plus chronometer certification, COSC and Observatoire Chronométrique de Genève per the brand
* **Strap:** white alligator, 20 to 18 mm, steel or gold folding clasp

**Price and availability:** **CHF 33,800 (about $40,352 USD)** in steel and **CHF 62,800 (about $74,973 USD)** in gold, conversions at the November 24 ECB rate. Sold only at the new Geneva boutique, per Monochrome. No US price, and no production limit has been stated.

**My take:** opal and chrysocolla are about as far from a safe stone dial as you can get, and that is the appeal. A floating stone disc over a Geneva Seal hand wound movement is a genuinely different flex at this level. For a stone dial at a very different price, look back at the [Nodus x Oracle Time Sector II Red Jasper]({% post_url 2026-11-20-nodus-oracle-time-sector-ii-red-jasper-alpina-galaga-citizen-attesa-hazy-news %}) from last week.

## Citizen Attesa Ever Since BY1000-69L reaches the US

**US listing:** reported November 25, 2026 ([Citizen US](https://www.citizenwatch.com/us/en/search?q=BY1000-69L), [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/After-Japan-Citizen-s-new-limited-edition-titanium-Attesa-moonphase-launches-in-the-US.1458784.0.html))

![Citizen Attesa Ever Since BY1000-69L with blue moonphase dial on a Super Titanium bracelet]({{ "/assets/img/watches/citizen-attesa-ever-since-by1000-69l.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Citizen*

Citizen's limited Attesa Ever Since first showed up in Japan about a month ago, per Notebookcheck, and it now has an official US listing. It is a deep blue moonphase dial with a subtle textured backdrop that Citizen US describes as "rolling mountains beneath a star-filled sky" (Notebookcheck notes the Japanese launch described a snow covered forest instead).

* **Reference:** BY1000-69L
* **Case and bracelet:** Super Titanium with Duratect Platinum coating, 41.5 mm; 10.8 mm thick per Notebookcheck
* **Crystal:** anti reflective sapphire
* **Water resistance:** 100 m
* **Movement:** Eco-Drive caliber H874, solar powered and radio controlled, with perpetual calendar, world time in 24 zones, day and date and moonphase
* **Accuracy and reserve:** plus or minus 15 seconds per month without radio signal and up to 2.5 years on a full charge in power saving mode, per Notebookcheck
* **Edition:** 2,400 pieces worldwide, unnumbered

**Price:** **$1,195 USD** on Citizen's US site. Notebookcheck points out that Japan sells it for ¥170,500 including tax, so the US price works out roughly 23% higher once Japan's consumption tax is backed out.

**My take:** this is the same H874 platform behind the [Attesa Death Star II]({% post_url 2026-11-14-citizen-attesa-death-star-ii-by1004-news %}), minus the Star Wars theme and $305 USD cheaper. Atomic sync, perpetual calendar and world time in titanium for $1,195 USD is a lot of watch, and it makes a sharp gift for the person who never wants to set a watch again.
