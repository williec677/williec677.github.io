While everybody else is carving turkey, the auction world is loading up for The New York Watch Auction: XV. Phillips says the sale, on December 5 and 6, 2026, is the highest value watch auction ever hosted in the United States, with a total low estimate in excess of **$25 million USD**. Haute Time put the full picture together today, and every number below is checked against Phillips' own press releases and lot pages where Phillips has published them.

This is not one big single owner collection either. Phillips' Paul Boutros and Isabella Proia say the sale "is not built around a single collection," which tells you how deep the high end market still is. Here's what matters.

## The sale at a glance

**Announced:** highlights October 15, 2026; full catalogue November 9, 2026 ([Phillips](https://www.phillips.com/press/release/phillips-celebrates-horological-excellence-with-the-release-of-the-full-catalogue-for-the-highest-value-watch-auction-ever-hosted-in-the-us)); full preview recap November 26 ([Haute Time](https://www.hautetime.com/phillips-new-york-watch-auction-xv-2026/))

* **Dates:** December 5 and 6, 2026, Phillips in Association with Bacs & Russo, New York
* **Size:** 196 lots per Haute Time; Phillips describes "close to 200" timepieces
* **Total low estimate:** in excess of $25 million USD, per Phillips
* **Public viewing:** opens December 2 per Phillips; Haute Time lists 432 Park Avenue, December 2 to 4, 10 AM to 7 PM ET Wednesday and Friday, 10 AM to 8 PM ET Thursday
* **Sessions (per Haute Time):** Session I, December 5 at 10 AM ET, lots 1 to 77; Session II, December 5 at 3 PM ET, TimeForArt, lots 78 to 103; Session III, December 6 at 10 AM ET, lots 104 to 196

One reality check: "highest value" is based on the published estimates. The hammer decides whether it becomes a record result. Estimates exclude the buyer's premium.

## Lot 123: Patek Philippe 2499 "Second Series" in pink gold

This is the crown jewel. The reference 2499 is the perpetual calendar chronograph with moon phase that sits at the very top of vintage Patek collecting, and this one is pink gold, which is the rare stuff. From the [Phillips lot page](https://www.phillips.com/detail/patek-philippe/239259):

* **Year:** 1953, with a Patek Philippe Extract from the Archives confirming production in 1953 and sale on September 27, 1958
* **Case:** 18k pink gold, 37.5 mm, on leather with an 18k pink gold Patek buckle
* **Movement:** manual wind calibre 13''', 23 jewels, stamped twice with the Geneva Seal
* **Rarity:** one of eight publicly identified second series 2499s in 18k pink gold, per Phillips, from "an important European collector"
* **Estimate:** in excess of **$3,000,000 USD**

Haute Time adds that its predecessor sits in the same sale: Lot 25, a yellow gold Patek Philippe reference 1518 from 1950, sold January 17, 1952, 35 mm, estimated at **$500,000 to $1,000,000 USD**. Phillips calls the 1518 the first perpetual calendar chronograph wristwatch made in series by any brand. Two generations of the most important complicated Patek ever, in one room. That is wild.

## Lot 26: Jerry Seinfeld's Vic Elford Daytona 6239

Now the one every car guy and Rolex guy is going to be talking about. On February 4, 1968, Vic Elford and his co drivers gave Porsche its first outright win in a 24 hour race at Daytona International Speedway. His prize was a stainless steel Rolex Cosmograph Daytona reference 6239, and there is a photo of him in his race suit holding the box open. Phillips calls it the first known, documented Rolex Daytona ever awarded to the winner of an automotive race. Think about that: this is the watch at the start of the whole Rolex and motorsport story.

Here's the provenance stack, per [Phillips](https://www.phillips.com/press/release/-phillips-to-offer-a-landmark-of-motorsport-history-vic-elfords-historic-rolex-daytona-from-the-collection-of-jerry-seinfeld) and its [November 24 feature](https://www.phillips.com/article/vic-elford-jerry-seinfeld-rolex-daytona-6239-phillips-new-york-watch-auction-xv-december-2026):

* **The car connection:** Seinfeld bought the winning Porsche 907 Langheck at auction in 2014, one of only eight built. He initially passed on the watch, then went back and bought it too
* **Paperwork:** a copy of a June 2005 Rolex U.S.A. service invoice in Elford's name with matching serial 1'475'604, a signed provenance letter from Elford dated November 2010, and a Rolex U.S.A. letter dated November 23, 2010 confirming authenticity and that a Daytona was presented to Elford in 1968
* **Restoration, disclosed:** Seinfeld commissioned a sympathetic restoration to its 1968 look. Per Phillips that meant color matching the hour and minute hands to the dial's lume plots, a period correct Tropic 21 domed crystal, a repair to one rivet of the original riveted Oyster bracelet, and a period correct presentation box. Phillips says the case, bezel, dial and bracelet are original
* **Estimate:** **$100,000 to $200,000 USD**

**My take:** that estimate looks conservative for a watch with this story and this name on the consignment. Trophy provenance plus a celebrity owner is exactly the combo that blows through estimates. And credit to Phillips for spelling out the restoration work up front. If you collect vintage, that kind of transparency is exactly what you want to see before you bid.

## Lot 134: Paul Newman Daytona 6241 "Champagne" in yellow gold

If the Elford watch is the history lesson, this is the eye candy. From the [Phillips lot page](https://www.phillips.com/detail/rolex/238010):

* **Year:** circa 1968; the 6241 was made only from about 1966 to 1969
* **Case:** 18k yellow gold, 37.5 mm, which Phillips says is the rarest of the gold variants, with French hallmarks on the caseback, Mk 1 bezel and 71 end links on an 18k riveted Oyster bracelet
* **Dial:** bi color "Paul Newman" champagne dial with large "T Swiss T" at 6, in the same serial range as other known examples
* **Movement:** manual wind calibre 722-1, 17 jewels
* **Estimate:** **$450,000 to $900,000 USD**

## The F.P. Journe wall

This might be the deepest group of early Journe ever put in one American sale. The headliner is Lot 10, Tourbillon Souverain "Souscription" No. 13, from the first 20 watches Journe sold by subscription in 1998 to fund the brand, delivered in 1999. Per [Phillips](https://www.phillips.com/detail/fp-journe/241899) it is platinum, 38 mm, calibre 1498 with 25 jewels and a remontoir d'égalité, with the original souscription contract signed by F.P. Journe, and the catalogue notes the subscription price was CHF 25,000 plus CHF 2,500 for platinum. The estimate is in excess of **$1,000,000 USD**.

Also in the group, per Phillips and Haute Time:

* **Lot 20:** second generation Tourbillon Souverain No. 36/99T in platinum, 38 mm, **$600,000 to $1,200,000 USD**; Haute Time says only 16 of this generation were made
* **Lot 33:** the only known pre souscription Chronomètre à Résonance in 18k pink gold, No. 061/00R, estimated in the same range per Haute Time
* **Lot 35:** one of five Tourbillon Souverain watches made for Sincere Fine Watches, pink gold with black mother of pearl dial, in excess of **$1,000,000 USD** per Haute Time
* **Octa Réserve de Marche prototype** No. Proto/00A, one of three known per Haute Time, in excess of **$500,000 USD**

## More big swings

Per Haute Time, with the lots confirmed in Phillips' releases:

* **Akrivia AK-02:** a unique stainless steel piece with two hand hammered dials, consigned by its original owner, **$500,000 to $1,000,000 USD**. Phillips says it is the first AK-02 ever offered at auction in the US
* **Philippe Dufour Simplicity:** early 18k white gold example, No. 60, circa 2001, **$300,000 to $600,000 USD**
* **Patek Philippe 5971/13P-010:** platinum, set with emeralds, **$600,000 to $1,200,000 USD**
* **Cartier Model A "King Farouk I" Mystery Clock:** commissioned in 1936, in excess of **$500,000 USD**, offered with no reserve

## TimeForArt: 26 one of one watches, no reserve

Session II is the third edition of TimeForArt with Swiss Institute: 26 unique watches donated by brands, all offered with no reserve, with 100% of proceeds, including Phillips' donated buyer's premium, going to the institute, per Phillips. It is led by a unique Simon Brette Chronomètre Artisans in platinum with a Grand Feu enamel dial by Anita Porchet, estimated at $60,000 to $120,000 USD per Haute Time. One more to watch for: SJX reports a one off red stromatolite version of the [Anoma A2]({% post_url 2026-11-26-anoma-a2-moebius-oval-watch-news %}) is in this session.
