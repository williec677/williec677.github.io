Some micro brands chase a new gimmick every drop. **Wren** went back to the watch that built its name and gave it the best dial it has made yet. The New York brand founded by Craig Karger has launched the **Diver One Titanium Glacier** at **$1,495 USD**, limited to **100 individually numbered pieces** in its first run. Every spec below comes from [Wren's own Glacier page](https://wrenwatches.com/products/diver-one-titanium-glacier), checked on November 15, 2026, with [Time+Tide's launch story](https://timeandtidewatches.com/wren-diver-one-titanium-glacier-introducing/) linked where I lean on it.

## The Glacier dial is the whole story

The Glacier joins the Snow and Dusk in Wren's titanium Diver One line, using the same case, bracelet and movement. What changes is the face. The center is a pale, icy blue that fades out to dark grey around the edge, and that grey flows straight into the matching dark grey ceramic bezel. Wren itself says this gradient "changes with the light more than any dial we have made," and in the official photos it really does shift from frosty to stormy depending on the angle.

There is no date window, which is the right call here. Nothing interrupts the fade, and the bright center pulls your eye right to the white hands and applied markers. If you remember the Diver One Abyss with its almost absurdly black Musou Black dial, the Glacier is the opposite mood: lighter, cooler, and a lot easier to wear every day.

### Why the titanium spec matters

Titanium is light, but plain titanium scratches easily, and that is the usual complaint. Wren puts a clear DLC coating on both the case and bracelet, and Time+Tide reports Wren's claim of roughly 800 Vickers surface hardness versus around 200 for untreated steel. Those are the brand's numbers, not an independent test, but the clear finish keeps the natural metal look instead of turning the watch black. At 41 mm, 12.4 mm thick and only 47 mm lug to lug, this is a diver that should sit flat on most wrists.

One small thing to flag on the strap: Wren's page says buyers get a choice of **blue or white FKM rubber**, while Time+Tide's review sample came with a **grey** strap. Check the option on Wren's site when you order.

### How to buy it

Wren's site lists it at $1,495 with **shipping starting November 21**, and when I checked on the morning of November 15 the page showed **only 14 left in stock**. Time+Tide also says it is available at the Time+Tide Studio in New York City. If you want one, I would not sit on it.

## My take on the Glacier

The honest knock is the movement. An ETA 2824 with 38 hours of reserve is proven and easy to service, but at $1,495 a lot of rivals now give you 60 hours or more, so a weekend off the wrist means resetting it. Everything else is strong value: Swiss made, grade 5 titanium case and bracelet with a coating, a fully lumed ceramic bezel, sapphire, 200 m, a tool free microadjust clasp and a second strap in the box. Most of all, that dial is genuinely beautiful. If you want one titanium diver that looks like nothing else at this price, this is it.

## Also worth knowing: Chopard Mille Miglia Classic Chronograph American Racing Stripes

You may have missed this one when it was announced earlier this month, and aBlogtoWatch just went hands on with it today. **Chopard** has made a US only edition of its Mille Miglia Classic Chronograph, the **American Racing Stripes, reference 168619-3021**, limited to **50 pieces** sold through Chopard and its authorized dealers in the United States. [Chopard's US page](https://www.chopard.com/en-us/watch/168619-3021.html) lists it at **$12,400 USD**.

The look comes from American racing liveries. A blue lacquered dial carries two white lacquered stripes running down the left side, and [Waqt](https://www.waqt.com/en/article/chopard-unveils-the-mille-miglia-classic-chronograph-american-racing-stripes) ties the theme to the blue and white cars of Dan Gurney's All American Racers in the 1960s.

| Strap | Blue rubber with Dunlop tire tread pattern, pin buckle |

[aBlogtoWatch's Ariel Adams](https://www.ablogtowatch.com/chopard-mille-miglia-classic-chronograph-american-racing-stripes-watch-hands-on/) reports the movement, which Chopard calls caliber A32211 internally, is built on an upgraded ETA 2892 base, and he points out the case simply reads "50" rather than carrying an individual number. My view: this is not a revolution, it is a well made, very wearable racing chronograph with a story American car people will love, and with only 50 pieces for the whole country, it will not hang around long.
