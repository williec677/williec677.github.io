Vacheron Constantin has been making watches since 1755, and only now, as [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/intro-vacheron-fiftysix) puts it, is the brand "finally going grey." This Tuesday morning the Geneva house dropped two new steel versions of the **Fiftysix**, its most accessible collection: the Fiftysix Self Winding and the Fiftysix Complete Calendar, both with a fresh two tone grey sector dial. It is a refresh, not a reinvention, but it is the kind of refresh that makes a watch you already liked suddenly look like the one to buy. I checked every reference, size and price below against Hodinkee, [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/vacheron-constantin-fiftysix-self-winding-and-fiftysix-complete-calendar-2026-updated-steel-case-two-tone-dials-introducing-price/) and [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/introducing-two-new-vacheron-constantin-fiftysix-watches-with-tuxedo-dials/), all published November 10, 2026. Then, at the far end of the price scale, MB&F gave its LM101 to enamel artist Gabriel Colliard and got back three tiny jungles in platinum.

## Vacheron Constantin Fiftysix 2026: the quick numbers

Both watches share the same steel shell, so here is how they line up.

| | Fiftysix Self Winding | Fiftysix Complete Calendar |
|---|---|---|
| Reference | 4600E/000A-H177 | 4000E/000A-H178 |
| Case | 40 mm steel, 48 mm lug to lug (Fratello), 9.6 mm thick | 40 mm steel, 48 mm lug to lug (Fratello), 11.6 mm thick |
| Water resistance | 30 m | 30 m |
| Movement | Caliber 1326, automatic, 4 Hz, 48 hours, 25 jewels | Caliber 2460 QCL/1, automatic, 4 Hz, 40 hours, 27 jewels, 308 parts, Hallmark of Geneva |
| Functions | Hours, minutes, central seconds, date | Hours, minutes, central seconds, day, month, pointer date, moon phase |
| Strap | Grey nubuck calfskin, ecru stitching, steel folding clasp | Same |
| Price | $14,300 / €14,200 / £12,600 / CHF 12,600 | $33,900 / €33,400 / £29,600 / CHF 29,500 |

Neither watch is a limited edition, and Hodinkee lists both as available now. These are retail prices from the brand's launch figures as reported by the three outlets, not secondary market numbers.

## The dial is the whole story

The Fiftysix has always borrowed from Vacheron's 1956 reference 6073, which Fratello notes is the brand's own cited inspiration, and that is where the Maltese cross shaped lugs come from. What changes now is the dial, and it is a good one. On the Self Winding, Monochrome describes a light grey opaline center that fades into a darker grey chapter ring, framed by a snailed light grey minute track at the edge. The date sits at 3 o'clock, cutting into a black and white railway track, and the applied Arabic numerals and long rectangular markers are 18k white gold. The hands and markers carry blue glowing Super-LumiNova.

Fratello calls it a tuxedo dial, and that is exactly how it reads in photos: crisp, layered, and cool toned to match the steel. Vacheron also reworked the finishing on the case, with vertically brushed lugs and flanks against a polished bezel and crown, and a box shaped sapphire crystal on top for a proper mid century look.

Is grey a safe choice? Sure. Hodinkee points out the Self Winding already came in blue and a single tone grey, and one Monochrome reader said he expected salmon. But the two tone treatment gives this dial far more depth than a flat grey, and it pairs naturally with that grey nubuck strap.

## The Complete Calendar is back in steel

The bigger news for collectors might be the second watch. Hodinkee reports that the steel Fiftysix Complete Calendar was briefly off the market, and now it returns with a reversed version of the same dial: a dark grey gradient center, a light grey sunburst chapter ring with white gold numerals, and a dark grey snailed date ring on the outside. Day and month sit in two windows below 12, a white gold arrow tipped hand points to the date around the edge, and the moon phase lives at 6.

Turn it over and you get Vacheron's own caliber 2460 QCL/1, with 308 parts and the Hallmark of Geneva, the strict finishing and quality seal tied to the city. It runs at 28,800 vph with a 40 hour reserve. That is a lot of traditional haute horlogerie for a watch that still only stands 11.6 mm tall.

## The Self Winding movement: an honest note

Here is the part the hype crowd skips. The Self Winding's caliber 1326 is not a Vacheron manufacture movement. Hodinkee says it is produced by Richemont's Manufacture Horlogère ValFleurier and then finished, assembled and regulated by Vacheron Constantin, and Monochrome adds that it is based on a Cartier ébauche. It is decorated with Côtes de Genève and spun by an openworked 22k pink gold rotor shaped like the Maltese cross, so it still looks the part through the display back. Just know what you are paying for.

## My take: which one would I buy?

If you want into Vacheron Constantin at retail, the Fiftysix Self Winding at $14,300 is still the front door, and this grey dial is the best looking way through it. Hodinkee confirms that price is unchanged from before. The Complete Calendar at $33,900 is the one I would actually lust after, because you get a true in house calendar movement with the Geneva Seal in a steel case that is easy to wear every day. The weak spot on both is 30 m of water resistance, so treat them as dress watches, not swimming partners.

## Also new: MB&F LM101 Gabriel Colliard, three enamel jungles

MB&F's Legacy Machine 101 is a strange canvas for an artist. The time sits at 2 o'clock, the power reserve at 6, and a big suspended balance wheel floats over the dial. Today MB&F revealed what Swiss based enameller Gabriel Colliard did with it, reported by [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/mbf-trilogy-lm101-by-gabriel-colliard-cloisonne-enamel-dials-introducing-price/), [SJX](https://watchesbysjx.com/2026/11/mbf-lm101-gabriel-colliard-cloisonne-enamel.html) and Hodinkee.

There are three cloisonné enamel dials, each built around one dominant color: red with two macaws, blue with a monkey, and green with a crocodile. Cloisonné means fine gold wires are bent by hand to outline the scene, and the little cells are filled with enamel and fired over and over to build up color. Monochrome reports MB&F thinned the dial plate to make room for the enamel layers and swapped the floating time and power reserve sub dials for longer hands. Colliard also enamels the ratchet wheel you see through the back.

The specs, per SJX and Monochrome:

* Case: 40 mm platinum, 16.55 mm tall, sapphire crystal, 30 m water resistance
* Movement: MB&F's in house LM101 caliber, hand wound, 18,000 vph (2.5 Hz), 60 hours of reserve, 14 mm suspended balance with a Straumann double hairspring
* Strap: brown alligator with a steel folding buckle and white gold cover
* Edition: 8 pieces in each color, 24 watches total
* Price: $131,000 / CHF 98,000 / €107,000, all before tax, at MB&F retailers and MAD Galleries

Here is the wild detail: Monochrome says Colliard completes about one enamel composition a month, so the full run of 24 will take more than two years. And as Colliard says, "the fire always has the final say," so no two dials will ever match. SJX argues the price is reasonable for the watchmaking plus the art, and I agree this is the rare collab where the artist truly changed the watch.
