The chronograph costs $75 more than the standard PRX at retail, and its complete service costs $70 more, not $200 more. One complete service on the Hamilton ($370) costs more than an entire Seiko 5 Sports Field automatic ($315). That is not a yearly bill. Many owners go years between full overhauls.

## What the True Cost Breakdown measures

The model is simple on purpose. You add the price you would actually pay to one official complete service bill. That sum is the cost of getting in and covering one full overhaul. What you later recoup depends on the price you can actually sell at, which I do not treat as a published market figure on this page.

A complete service is not every visit. Official lists also publish light and partial work, the brand examines the watch and recommends the tier, and some watches will never need a complete service while you own them. I use complete service so every watch is compared on the same, clearly defined expense.

## Hypothetical ownership examples: if it resold for 0, 10 or 20 percent less

These are clearly labeled hypothetical examples, not predictions of future market prices, and they use no resale estimates from a market data service. Each watch is bought at official US retail, gets one official complete service, and is then assumed to resell for 0, 10 or 20 percent less than that retail price.

At 0 percent, the ownership cost equals the official complete service bill, because the assumed sale price matches what you paid. At 10 or 20 percent, you add that share of the official retail price to the service bill.

| Watch | 0 percent | 10 percent | 20 percent |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Tissot PRX 40 mm quartz | 130 | 175 | 220 |
| Seiko 5 Sports Field automatic | 176 | 207.50 | 239 |
| Seiko 5 Sports Field GMT | 176 | 221 | 266 |
| Tissot PRX quartz chronograph | 200 | 252.50 | 305 |
| Hamilton Intra Matic Auto Chrono | 370 | 634.50 | 899 |

Under these official price assumptions, the standard PRX quartz stays lowest and the Hamilton stays highest at every level. The lesson is that your purchase price can dramatically change your ownership cost. Pay retail, and a later drop in value drives the result. If you can buy for less than official retail, run the same math with the price you would actually pay, because the service bill then matters more.

I am not publishing a ranking based on buying below official retail, because that would require market price estimates I am not using here.

## What I would take from the comparison

On official prices alone, a few points hold without any market estimates:

1. The PRX quartz chronograph costs $75 more to buy and $70 more to service than the standard PRX 40 mm quartz.
2. One official complete service on the Hamilton Intra Matic Auto Chrono ($370) costs more than the official retail price of the Seiko 5 Sports Field automatic ($315).
3. In the hypothetical 0, 10 and 20 percent examples above, the standard PRX quartz stays the lowest and the Hamilton stays the highest.

Those points describe this official price comparison, not which watch is best to wear. Function, size, and taste still decide the purchase.
