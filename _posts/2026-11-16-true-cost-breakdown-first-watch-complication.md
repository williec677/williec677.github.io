## What the True Cost Breakdown measures

The model is simple on purpose. You add the purchase price to one official complete service bill, then subtract an estimated resale value. The remainder is the illustrative ownership cost.

A complete service is not every visit. Official lists also publish light and partial work, the brand examines the watch and recommends the tier, and some watches will never need a complete service while you own them. I use complete service so every watch is compared on the same, clearly defined expense.

Resale figures are WatchCharts estimated private sale prices for the exact references in the video. They are estimates, not guaranteed proceeds, and they are not forecasts of a later sale.

## Scenario 1: buy at official retail

This scenario assumes you pay official US retail, pay one official complete service, and subtract today's estimated private sale price.

| Watch | Official retail | Complete service | Estimated resale | Reported sales, 1 year | Illustrative cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Seiko 5 Sports Field automatic SRPG29 | 315 | 176 | 202 | 125 | 289 |
| Tissot PRX quartz chronograph T1374171105100 | 525 | 200 | 428 | 6 | 297 |
| Seiko 5 Sports Field GMT SSK023 | 450 | 176 | 326 | 484 | 300 |
| Tissot PRX 40 mm quartz T1374101103100 | 450 | 130 | 209 | 421 | 371 |
| Hamilton Intra Matic Auto Chrono H38416111 | 2,645 | 370 | 1,474 | 71 | 1,541 |

The headline surprise is real under these assumptions: the standard PRX, which costs $75 less to buy than the chronograph, costs more to own in this scenario ($371 versus $297). Read that with a clear limit. The chronograph's estimated resale rests on only 6 reported sales in WatchCharts' one year volume, against 484 for the Field GMT over the same one year window, and WatchCharts market data for the chronograph only starts in September 2026. A six sale sample is thin, and it can change quickly when more sales appear.

Reported sales show market activity. They do not, by themselves, tell you how quickly or easily an individual watch will sell.

## Scenario 2: buy at today's estimated private sale

This second scenario starts below official retail. You buy at today's estimated private sale price, pay one official complete service, and subtract the same estimate. That last step is deliberate. It shows what remains after the first owner's depreciation, not a claim that you will sell later at today's number.

## Hypothetical resale sensitivity: 0, 10 and 20 percent

These are hypothetical examples, not predictions of future market prices. Each watch is bought at official retail, gets one official complete service, and sells for 0, 10 or 20 percent less than the purchase price.

| Watch | 0 percent | 10 percent | 20 percent |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Tissot PRX 40 mm quartz | 130 | 175 | 220 |
| Seiko 5 Sports Field automatic | 176 | 207.50 | 239 |
| Seiko 5 Sports Field GMT | 176 | 221 | 266 |
| Tissot PRX quartz chronograph | 200 | 252.50 | 305 |
| Hamilton Intra Matic Auto Chrono | 370 | 634.50 | 899 |

The standard PRX quartz stays lowest and the Hamilton stays highest at every level. The lesson: your purchase price can dramatically change your ownership cost. Pay retail, and the drop in value drives the result. Buy closer to the market price, and the service bill matters more.

## How to use the calculator with your own numbers

[Open the ownership calculator](/ownership-calculator/). Load one of the verified examples, or type your own figures:

1. The price you would actually pay, not a list price you will never face.
2. The service cost that matches your realistic plan. Official complete service is the conservative comparison used in the video. If you expect only a battery or a light visit, enter that number instead.
3. A resale value you can defend, preferably from recent sold data on the exact reference, not an asking price.
4. Optional selling fees, as a percentage of the sale plus any flat cost such as shipping.

The calculator shows the illustrative net ownership cost and the same hypothetical 0, 10 and 20 percent resale scenarios for your numbers. If the result is a gain, the model is telling you that estimated resale covers purchase and service under your inputs. That is still illustrative, not a promise.

## What I would take from the comparison

Under the scenario 1 assumptions:

- Lowest illustrative ownership cost: Seiko 5 Sports Field automatic SRPG29, at $289.
- Best balance of a useful complication, a lower price, and a much busier market: Seiko 5 Sports Field GMT SSK023, at $300, with 484 reported sales in one year.
- Most surprising comparison: Tissot PRX quartz chronograph, at $297, with the 6 sale limit stated at the reveal.
- Premium enthusiast pick: Hamilton Intra Matic Auto Chrono, at $1,541, if you want a mechanical chronograph and go in knowing the $370 complete service and the drop in value.

Those labels describe this comparison, not which watch is best to wear. Function, size, and taste still decide the purchase.
