A camera company just made one of the most interesting green dials of the year, and it did it with the same trick photographers chase every day: light and shadow. Leica's ZM 11 Green Spirit went on sale this morning, and it is not alone today. Armin Strom also dropped a 15 piece version of its twin balance Dual Time GMT with real meteorite on both dials. One is a daily wear sports watch, the other is a horological science project for space nerds. Let's get into both.

## Leica ZM 11 Green Spirit

**Announced and on sale:** November 17, 2026 ([Leica USA](https://leicacamerausa.com/leica-zm-11-green-spirit.html), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/leica-zm-11-green-spirit-watch-introducing-price/), [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/the-leica-zm-11-green-spirit/))

Quick background. Leica, the Wetzlar optics house that popularized the 35mm camera back in 1925, got serious about watches in 2018 with the L1 and L2 (later renamed ZM 1 and ZM 2), developed with Lehmann Präzision. Those leaned hard on camera cues. The ZM 11 arrived in 2023 and changed direction: a brushed, integrated bracelet sports watch built around a layered dial that plays with depth instead of copying a lens.

### The dial is the whole story

The Green Spirit's dial is made from two plates, each just 0.4 mm thick according to Fratello. The top plate carries horizontal slats, almost like a Venetian blind, sitting over a contrasting base layer. Tilt your wrist and the surface swings from a deep, saturated green to a cool industrial gray. Fratello says this is the first ZM 11 to get a gradient dial. Brushed applied indices with polished edges are filled with Grade A Super-LumiNova, the faceted baton hands are diamond cut, and Fratello points out a cool nerdy detail: the numerals in the 3 o'clock date window use the same typeface Leica engraves on its lenses.

### Case, bracelet and the red dot trick

The case is 41 mm wide, 13 mm thick and 45.3 mm lug to lug (per Fratello) in mostly brushed stainless steel, with a fluted screw down crown, a double domed sapphire crystal with anti reflective coating on both sides, a sapphire display back and 100 m of water resistance. Monochrome lists the case at 78 grams.

The fun part lives under the lugs. Leica's Easy Change System uses a little red dot pusher, inspired by the lens release on a Leica camera body, so you can swap the integrated bracelet for rubber or leather in seconds. Keeping the red dot hidden underneath keeps the dial side clean, which I think was the right call.

### Movement

Inside is the automatic Leica LA-3001, developed with Swiss movement specialist Chronode: 35 jewels, 4 Hz, about 60 hours of power reserve, and regulated to within +6/-4 seconds per day across five positions. Fratello notes the bridges get dark gray ruthenium plating with sandblasted and brushed surfaces rather than Geneva stripes.

## Armin Strom Dual Time GMT Resonance Manufacture Edition Meteorite

**Announced:** November 17, 2026 ([Armin Strom](https://arminstrom.com/watch-collections/dual-time-gmt-resonance-manufacture-edition-meteorite/), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/armin-strom-dual-time-gmt-resonance-manufacture-edition-meteorite-introducing-price/))

![Armin Strom Dual Time GMT Resonance Manufacture Edition Meteorite on the wrist, two meteorite dials with black chapter rings and rose gold colored hands]({{ "/assets/img/watches/armin-strom-dual-time-gmt-resonance-meteorite.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Armin Strom*

If you have never seen an Armin Strom Resonance watch, here is why people lose their minds over them. The movement has two completely independent balance wheels, and a patented resonance clutch links them so their oscillations sync up through the physics of resonance. In the Dual Time GMT, each balance drives its own dial with its own hours, minutes and day/night indicator, so you are reading two time zones off two separate regulating systems. The model first appeared in 2018 and got a full rethink in 2024 with a smaller case and the in house ARF22 caliber.

### What's new: space rock, twice

This edition keeps the 39 mm stainless steel case and puts natural meteorite on both dials. Because meteorite's crosshatched pattern comes from the stone itself, the two dials on a single watch will not match each other, and no two watches will match either. Black azurage chapter rings frame each dial, while the applied indices and faceted hands get a rose gold colored coating that warms up an otherwise grayscale watch. It comes on a matte grey alligator strap with a steel pin buckle.

### My take

A steel watch at CHF 105,000 sounds wild until you remember what is inside: two balances negotiating with each other through resonance, a movement under 5 mm thick, and finishing with hand bevelled edges, perlage and black polished screws. This is one of the few watches where a GMT complication is genuinely a mechanical flex and not just a fourth hand. The meteorite dials make the most sense on this watch of any edition so far, because the gray stone lets the movement's architecture do the shouting.
