The first one vanished in a day, so of course there is a sequel. **TAG Heuer** and Australian watch media house **Time+Tide** revealed the **Aquaracer Professional 300 x Time+Tide "Sundowner II"** today, and it fixes the one thing a lot of people said about the original: they wanted a mechanical movement. The solar engine is gone, a COSC certified automatic is in, and the case has grown into proper 300 m dive watch territory. Specs and prices below come from Time+Tide's launch article, Hodinkee and Fratello, all checked on November 11, 2026.

## Quick refresher: the first Sundowner

Two years ago Time+Tide and TAG Heuer did the Aquaracer Solargraph "Sundowner", a 40 mm grade 2 titanium watch built on the Aquaracer Professional 200 Solargraph, with rose gold toned details and a green NATO strap, inspired by the Australian outback. According to [Time+Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/tag-heuer-aquaracer-time-tide-sundowner-ii-hands-on/), all 250 pieces sold out within 24 hours. That is the kind of demand that makes a second chapter a no brainer.

## What's new on the Sundowner II

The theme carries over: dark sandblasted titanium, warm rose gold accents. Almost everything else moves up a level.

|---|---|
| References | WBP5184.FT6257 (rubber strap), WBP5185.BF0010 (titanium bracelet) |

| Price | **$5,100 USD** on rubber (£4,300, A$7,700), **$5,750 USD** on bracelet (£4,850, A$8,650) |

All prices are the official US dollar prices Time+Tide published, so no currency conversion was needed. This is a brand new release, so there are no sold prices on the secondary market yet.

One spec to flag: Hodinkee's spec box lists the watch as not chronometer certified, but Time+Tide and Fratello both say the TH31-01 here is COSC certified, and the dial in the official photos reads "Officially Certified Chronometer". I am going with the brand partner and the dial.

## The details that make it

**A real no date movement.** Time+Tide says the TH31-01 in this watch is a version exclusive to the Sundowner II, set up as a genuine no date caliber. That means no phantom crown position where a date would be set. It is a small thing, but if you have ever pulled a crown into a dead click on a "no date" watch, you know why it matters.

**The bezel.** The original had an all titanium bezel. The new one gets a grey ceramic insert with rose gold toned numerals, which looks cleaner and should hold up far better to daily knocks than bare metal.

**The dial.** The teak deck lines of the first watch are gone, replaced by the wave pattern from the regular mechanical Aquaracer, done in black. Add the octagonal applied markers and the clean, dateless layout and it is a seriously handsome dive dial.

**The size.** 42 mm sounds big, but titanium keeps the weight down, and 48 mm lug to lug with a 12 mm height is reasonable for a 300 m diver. Fratello's Daan de Groot says the larger case, mechanical movement and bracelet option make it "a great second chapter".

## My take

This is the right sequel. Solar was clever, but the people who spend over $5,000 on a collab mostly want something ticking inside, and TAG Heuer delivered a chronometer with 80 hours in a light titanium case. The rubber version is the smart buy: 450 pieces gives you a fair shot, and $5,100 for a numbered, COSC titanium diver with a story is a strong pitch. The bracelet version is the one everybody will want, and with only 50 made, I would not count on getting one unless you are first in line for Priority Access.

Is it pricey next to a regular Aquaracer? Fratello's readers already think so, and that is fair. You are paying for titanium, the no date chronometer and the limited run. If the first Sundowner taught us anything, it is that this collab has a crowd that moves fast.
