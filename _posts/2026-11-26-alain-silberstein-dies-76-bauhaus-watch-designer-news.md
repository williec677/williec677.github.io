Some designers you recognize by a logo. Alain Silberstein you could recognize from across a room, by three shapes and three colors. The French designer died early on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, at the age of 76. The news broke on Thanksgiving eve in the US, and it lands at a hard moment: he was less than a week away from revealing the comeback of his own brand.

## What is confirmed

**Reported:** November 25 and 26, 2026 ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/in-memory-of-alain-silberstein-1950-2026-the-singular-watch-designer/), [L'Est Républicain](https://www.estrepublicain.fr/economie/2026/11/26/disparition-d-alain-silberstein-genial-designer-horloger), [The Hour Markers](https://www.thehourmarkers.com/articles/alain-silberstein-obituary))

* **When:** he died on the morning of Wednesday, November 25, 2026, aged 76, per L'Est Républicain, the daily newspaper in Besançon, where he built his career.
* **Who broke it:** L'Est Républicain credits Grégory Pons, the French watch journalist, as the first to report it. Pons called him "the greatest French watch designer of the last half century" (our translation).
* **Born:** June 14, 1950, in Paris, per L'Est Républicain.
* **Not disclosed:** no cause of death has been published, and we have not seen funeral details. L'Est Républicain links to an online condolence page for anyone who wants to leave a message.

## From interiors to dials

Silberstein did not come up through a watchmaking school. He trained in design and worked as an interior architect, doing shops, restaurants and hotels. The Hour Markers says he and his wife Sylvie ran that practice together for nearly 15 years. In 1979 he moved to Besançon, the old capital of French watchmaking near the Swiss border, per Monochrome, and that is where the interest turned into a second career.

The Hour Markers reports the first spark was the arrival of Swatch in the 1980s, and that his early experiments were quartz. Mechanical watches won him over soon after. In 1987 he showed his first watches at the Basel fair. Monochrome names the debut model the Krono Bauhaus; The Hour Markers describes the Basel showing as three prototypes. Either way, 1987 is the year the brand counts from.

## The look everyone copied and nobody owned

The design language is the whole story. Both Monochrome and The Hour Markers point to the Bauhaus school and to painters like Kandinsky, Miró and Picasso, and Monochrome adds Calder and Mondrian. On the dial that meant a red triangle hour hand, a blue minute hand and a yellow serpentine seconds hand, with the same red, blue and yellow geometry in his logo. It looked like a toy at first glance and like a serious chronograph at the second.

It was never only about color, though. Highlights from the record:

* **1987: Krono Bauhaus.** The playful chronograph that introduced the signature hands and shapes, per Monochrome.
* **1990: his own company.** Alain Silberstein Créations was founded in Besançon with Sylvie, per Monochrome.
* **1993: Krono Saphir.** A case made almost entirely of sapphire crystal, down to colored sapphire pushers and crown, which Monochrome counts among the early uses of the material.
* **1997: Krono Tourbillon.** A tourbillon chronograph limited to 10 pieces, per The Hour Markers.
* **Movements:** Silberstein was a designer, not a movement maker. Monochrome notes his calibers were sourced from established suppliers, which let him dress up chronographs, tourbillons and perpetual calendars in his own visual language.

The Hour Markers also notes he built an especially loyal following among collectors in Japan.

## Closing, then a second act through collaborations

The company stopped operating in 2012, after roughly 30,000 watches by Monochrome's estimate; The Hour Markers attributes the closure to financial difficulties. What followed is the part younger collectors know best. He kept designing for other houses:

* **MB&F:** the HM2.2 Black Box in 2009 and a Legacy Machine 1 in his colors in 2016. If you followed our coverage of the [MB&F LM101 earlier this month]({% post_url 2026-11-10-vacheron-constantin-fiftysix-grey-two-tone-mbf-lm101-colliard-news %}), the Silberstein LM1 is the loud cousin of that family.
* **Louis Erard:** a run of collaborations starting in 2019, led by regulator dials with his colored hands, then Le Triptyque and Le Diptyque, per The Hour Markers. For many people these were the first Silberstein designs they could actually afford.
* **Others:** between them, Monochrome and The Hour Markers also list work with Bell & Ross, Ressence, Romain Jerome, Yema and MeisterSinger.

Monochrome also makes a point worth repeating: he was one of the people who helped bring independent mechanical watchmaking back after the quartz crisis, and today's indie makers often cite him as an influence.

## What happens to the relaunch

This is the painful part. His brand site, [alainsilberstein.ch](https://www.alainsilberstein.ch/), still says he is "composing the 2027 collection celebrating the 40th anniversary" of the brand, with timepieces "powered by innovative movements," and promises more on December 1, 2026. Monochrome reports the relaunch is still planned, with the concept and first subscription watches due December 1 and a full collection aimed at Watches and Wonders 2027.

The Hour Markers reports he was developing seven proprietary movements for the return. Treat that number as reported, not confirmed by the brand. The Hour Markers also says the project's future is now uncertain, while Monochrome says it is still going ahead. We will check the brand's December 1 announcement and update the story then.

**My take:** in a hobby that takes itself very seriously, Silberstein made watches that smiled. That is harder than it looks, and the fact that brands from MB&F to Louis Erard kept calling him decades after his debut says everything. It has been a heavy week for the industry: [Paolo Bulgari]({% post_url 2026-11-22-paolo-bulgari-dies-89-bvlgari-watch-legacy-casio-lf-40w-us-news %}) died just four days earlier, and the two men, in very different ways, both proved a watch can be a design object first.

## The bottom line

* **Alain Silberstein, 1950 to 2026:** Paris born designer, based in Besançon, died November 25 at 76. Cause not disclosed.
* **Legacy:** the Krono Bauhaus from 1987, the red, blue and yellow geometry, the sapphire cased Krono Saphir, a roughly 30,000 watch run under his own name to 2012, then MB&F and Louis Erard collaborations.
* **Next:** his relaunch reveal was scheduled for December 1, 2026; how it proceeds is not yet clear.
