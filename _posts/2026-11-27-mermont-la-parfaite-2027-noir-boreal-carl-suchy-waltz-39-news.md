Black Friday is supposed to be about doorbusters, but two of the most interesting dress watch stories of the week are the opposite of a deal. A tiny Geneva brand just unveiled three new dials for a solid platinum watch with one hand, and a revived Viennese name whose micro rotor carries the Habsburg crown gave its slimmest watch a new name and two moody new colors. No sales, no hype drops, just serious dress watches. Let's get into it.

## Mermont La Parfaite 2027 editions: Noir Boréal, Île d'Yeu and Éblouissante

**Revealed:** November 27, 2026 ([Monochrome hands on](https://monochrome-watches.com/hands-on-review-mermont-la-parfaite-new-dials-2027-independent-watchmaking-specs-price/), [Mermont](https://www.mermont.ch/))

![Mermont La Parfaite 2027 editions: Éblouissante with a platinum dial, Île d'Yeu with an ice blue dial and Noir Boréal with a black to purple dial]({{ "/assets/img/watches/mermont-la-parfaite-2027-trio.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Mermont (left to right: Éblouissante, Île d'Yeu, Noir Boréal)*

Mermont is a young independent founded by Sébastien Bey-Haut with his business partner Marie. Monochrome points out that neither is a watchmaker by trade, so the watches are finished, assembled and regulated in Geneva by independent watchmaker Nicolas Delaloye. The name comes from the French "MERveilleuse MONTre," marvelous watch, and the only model so far is La Parfaite, "the perfect one," a watch that tells time with a single sculpted hour hand. You read it approximately, on purpose. Mermont's pitch is that it lets you slow down.

The first La Parfaite was a 10 piece salmon Founders Edition, followed by a 28 piece blue dial, which Hodinkee reported in May at CHF 10,998 (about $13,160 USD at today's ECB rates). For 2027 both are replaced by three new editions, and they now appear on Mermont's own site as the 2027 collection:

* **Noir Boréal (28 pieces):** the headliner. Monochrome's Robin Nooy says it reads almost pure black head on, turns charcoal at an angle and jumps to deep purple with S shaped streaks in direct sun, thanks to the colimaçon (snail) brushing. His words: it "explodes when seen with one's own eyes."
* **Île d'Yeu (18 pieces):** an ice blue dial made with pigments mixed into Zapon varnish, the old school technique Mermont uses for its colors.
* **Éblouissante (8 pieces):** a solid platinum dial that shifts from silver to a soft champagne glow, with a heat blued hand and blued numerals instead of the polished hand and white print on the other two.

All three keep the Breguet style numerals, the angled minute track and the "secret" logo that only shows up as the light changes.

### Specs

* **Case:** 38 mm platinum, 9.2 mm thick, brushed and polished with sculpted lugs, tantalum caseback, sapphire crystal
* **Water resistance:** 30 m
* **Movement:** La Joux-Perret D101, hand wound, finition soignée (top grade finishing), 18 jewels, 21,600 vph, 50 hours, regulated by Nicolas Delaloye to within COSC limits per Monochrome
* **Display:** single "Solo" hour hand with a heart shaped opening
* **Strap:** 20 mm black or dark blue leather with rubber lining and a platinum pin buckle (per Monochrome)
* **Editions:** 8 Éblouissante, 18 Île d'Yeu, 28 Noir Boréal

**Price:** **CHF 12,498, about $14,955 USD**, for Noir Boréal and Île d'Yeu, and **CHF 14,998, about $17,946 USD**, for the platinum dial Éblouissante. Those are the Swiss franc prices reported by Monochrome and lug2lug; Mermont has not published a USD price, so the dollar figures are our conversion at the ECB reference rates of November 27, 2026 (EUR 1 = $1.0957 and CHF 0.9157, so CHF 1 = about $1.1966). With runs this small, check with Mermont directly for availability, taxes and shipping to the US.

**My take:** a full platinum case with a tantalum back for under $15,000 USD is a hard combination to find anywhere, and Noir Boréal is the one I would chase. The one hand concept will not be for everybody, but in a world of oversized complications this is refreshingly honest watchmaking. ## Carl Suchy & Söhne Waltz 39: the Vienna gets a new name, Nocturne and Deep Danube

**Announced:** November 27, 2026 ([Carl Suchy & Söhne](https://www.carlsuchy.com/collection/waltz-39), [Oracle of Time](https://oracleoftime.com/carl-suchy-sohne-waltz-39/))

![Carl Suchy & Söhne Waltz 39 Nocturne with an all black dial and Waltz 39 Deep Danube with an all blue dial]({{ "/assets/img/watches/carl-suchy-waltz-39-nocturne-deep-danube.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Carl Suchy & Söhne (Waltz 39 Nocturne, left, and Deep Danube)*

Carl Suchy & Söhne is the revived Viennese brand whose 2025 Vienna, designed by Eric Giroud, has been one of the quiet stunners of the slim dress watch world. As of this week it is the **Waltz 39**, a name Oracle of Time notes ties it to its bigger sibling, the Waltz N°1. The watch itself has not changed, but two new all one color dials join the lineup:

* **Nocturne:** all black, with the hands, indexes and minute track giving the contrast
* **Deep Danube:** all blue, a more formal take on the blue and white Blue Danube

They sit alongside the existing Blue Danube, Night and Day editions. The party piece is the rotating seconds disc at 6 o'clock, which Carl Suchy says completes the geometric dial pattern once each minute, so the design literally clicks into place as you watch it. On monochrome dials like these, Oracle of Time says the Giroud line work only reveals itself as the watch moves in the light.

### Specs (from Carl Suchy & Söhne)

* **Case:** 39 mm stainless steel, 7.9 mm thick, brushed and polished, domed sapphire crystal and sapphire caseback
* **Movement:** caliber CSS-V1 ultra thin automatic with a golden micro rotor engraved with the double griffins, Habsburg crown and double eagle, hand finished with Côtes de Vienne decoration, 48 hours
* **Functions:** hours, minutes, rotating seconds disc
* **Strap:** leather with quick release spring bars, the inner side stamped with the roof tile pattern of St. Stephen's Cathedral

**Price:** **EUR 18,900, about $20,709 USD**, on Carl Suchy's own site, unchanged from the Vienna per Oracle of Time. The dollar figure is our conversion at the ECB reference rate of November 27, 2026 (EUR 1 = $1.0957). In the US, authorized dealer Exquisite Timepieces lists both the Nocturne and Deep Danube at **$22,500 USD** (a dealer listing, not a brand published US price).

**My take:** under 8 mm with an automatic micro rotor and a dial that animates once a minute, this is a dress watch with real personality. Nocturne is the stealth pick for black tie season.
