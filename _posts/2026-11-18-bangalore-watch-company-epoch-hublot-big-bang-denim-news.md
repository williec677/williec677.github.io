A dress watch is the hardest kind of watch to get right, because there is nowhere to hide. No bezel, no chronograph, no dive rating to distract you, just a case, a dial and a pair of hands. Bangalore Watch Company built its name on sporty watches about Indian aviation, space and cricket, and today it went back to where it started with the Epoch, a hand wound dress watch with a party trick on the back. Then Hublot did the most Hublot thing possible and put genuine blue jeans on four Big Bangs. Let's get into it.

## Bangalore Watch Company Epoch

**Announced:** November 18, 2026 ([Bangalore Watch Company](https://bangalorewatchco.in/products/executive), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/bangalore-watch-company-epoch-hand-wound-dress-watch-from-india-introducing-price/))

Quick background if BWC is new to you. The brand was founded in 2018, and its very first watch was a dress piece called the Renaissance Automatic. Since then it has been all stories about modern India: pilots, astronauts, cricket legends, mountains. The Epoch is the return to the dress watch, and the brand frames it in a really nice way. Its point is that this watch has no particular story of its own, so there is room for yours.

### Two versions, one 39 mm case

The Epoch comes in two flavors:

* **Executive**, a stainless steel case with a champagne dial
* **Statesman**, a rose gold PVD coated steel case with a deep anthracite dial

Both use a 39 mm case that is 11.6 mm thick with a 47 mm lug to lug, which is right in the sweet spot for a modern dress watch. The brand calls it a "Zebra case" because the sides alternate brushed and polished finishing, and the lugs are kept compact so it should sit flat on the wrist. Up top is a flat sapphire crystal with anti reflective coating, the crown is a push pull design with the BWC logo, and water resistance is 50 m. That covers rain and hand washing, but this is not a swimming watch.

### That caseback

Here is the detail that makes the Epoch more than another clean dial. The back is a hinged officer style cover, like the lid on an old pocket watch. Close it and you get a solid back with hand finished perlage on the inside. Flip it open and there is a sapphire window showing off the movement. The inside of that cover can be engraved with a personal message, a birthday, an anniversary, whatever you want, and it stays hidden until you choose to open it. BWC notes on its product page that personalisation can add about five days to shipping.

This is the stuff that turns a watch into an heirloom. A gift watch with a date engraved on a hidden lid hits completely differently than one with a plain screw down back.

### The dial

BWC calls the finish a "sunrise" pattern: rays fan out from the snailed small seconds at 6 o'clock instead of from the center, so the light moves across the dial in a slightly unexpected way. The applied hour markers are shaped like little pyramids with four faces to catch light, paired with stylized Arabic numerals and a railway minute track around the edge. The hands are diamond cut and rhodium finished. There is also a small "Imagined in India" line near 6 o'clock. One honest note from me: the dial also carries "Since 2018" under the logo, which is a matter of taste. Some readers will love the heritage nod, others would rather keep the dial as clean as possible.

### Movement

Inside is the Swiss Sellita SW261-1, the hand wound, small seconds member of the SW200 family, running at 28,800 vph (4 Hz) with 23 jewels and stop seconds for setting the time to the second. It is decorated with Geneva stripes and blued screws, which you get to see every time you open that caseback. BWC says the movement is Swiss Made but regulated in Bangalore.

On power reserve, BWC's product page says 45 hours, while Monochrome lists a minimum of 42 hours. Both figures are close, but I am showing you both rather than picking one.

### Strap and lug width

Both watches ship on an Epsom pattern leather strap with a pin buckle matched to the case. BWC lists the lug width at **20 mm**, which is the most common size out there, so swapping straps is easy. ### My take

I like this one a lot. The dress watch space at around $3,000 USD is full of watches that look nice and say nothing, and the hinged caseback gives the Epoch a real reason to exist. The Statesman in particular, anthracite with rose gold tone, looks like a much more expensive watch in the press photos. The fair criticism is the movement: a Sellita SW261 is a reliable workhorse, but you are paying for design, finishing and that caseback, not an in house caliber.

## Hublot Big Bang Denim

**Announced:** November 18, 2026 ([Robb Report](https://robbreport.com/style/watch-collector/hublot-denim-big-bang-watch-collection-1238845708/), [Time+Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/hublot-big-bang-denim-editions-hands-on/))

![Four Hublot Big Bang Denim watches with blue denim dials and straps: Big Bang Original Titanium chronograph, Spirit of Big Bang with diamonds, Spirit of Big Bang chronograph, and Big Bang 33 mm with diamond bezel]({{ "/assets/img/watches/hublot-big-bang-denim-collection.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Hublot*

Hublot first put real denim on its watches back in 2013, and Robb Report notes that the 2013 pieces used Dsquared denim. Thirteen years later it is back with four new Big Bangs, and this time the denim theme goes past the dial and strap and into the metal itself.

### The cases are the story

Every case is finished by hand with several grades of abrasive paper to create a texture that looks like woven fabric. It is marked into the metal, not printed or applied, so each one comes out slightly different. Time+Tide spent time with two of them and singled out the case texture as the most interesting part of the release, especially on the big bezel of the 43 mm.

The dials use what Hublot calls "genuine blue jeans": real denim set at a 45 degree angle so the weave runs diagonally, then vacuum sealed in epoxy resin to lock in the color. The straps are denim on top with black rubber underneath and white stitching. Every piece also comes with a denim travel pouch.

### The four models

| Model | Case | Movement | US price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big Bang Original Titanium Denim (431.NXT.2710.NR.DENIM) | 43 mm x 13.2 mm titanium, 100 m | HUB1280 Unico automatic flyback chronograph, 72 h | **$24,000 USD** (CHF 19,400) |
| Spirit of Big Bang Denim | 42 mm titanium | HUB4700 automatic skeleton chronograph (El Primero derived) | **$27,700 USD** |
| Big Bang Steel Denim Diamonds (485.SXT.2710.NR.1204.DENIM) | 33 mm x 10.55 mm steel, 36 diamond bezel about 0.8 ct, 100 m | HUB1120 automatic (Sellita SW1000-1a base), 40 h | **$19,000 USD** (CHF 15,400) |
| Spirit of Big Bang Steel Diamonds Denim | 32 mm steel, diamond set | HUB1120 automatic | **$21,500 USD** |

**On the prices:** all four USD figures are Hublot's US prices as reported by Robb Report, and Time+Tide lists the same $24,000 and $19,000 USD for the two Big Bangs alongside the CHF prices shown. Reference numbers and full dimensions for the two Spirit of Big Bang models were not in either launch story, so I am not guessing at them. All four join the regular collection, not limited editions, and are available online and at Hublot boutiques.

### My take

Denim on a $24,000 USD watch is going to split people, and that is exactly why it works for Hublot. Love it or hate it, you will notice it from across the room. The hand finished woven texture on the case is the smart move here, because it turns a gimmick dial into a whole design idea. If I am buying one, it is the 43 mm Big Bang Original with the Unico flyback inside. That is real watchmaking hiding under the fun.
