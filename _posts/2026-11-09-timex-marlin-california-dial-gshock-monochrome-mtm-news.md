Two hundred watches, three hundred dollars, and one of the most fun dial layouts vintage collecting ever produced. Timex just dropped the Marlin California Dial Limited Edition, reference TW6A28700, and the first half of the run is already gone. Numbers 1 to 100 sold at the Windup Watch Fair in New York City. Numbers 101 to 200 went live today, November 9, 2026, on [timex.com](https://timex.com/products/marlin-california-dial-limited-edition-34mm-leather-strap-watch-tw6a28700) at $299, and that is the only place Timex says you can buy them. I pulled the price, edition size and specs straight from Timex's product page and launch post today, when it was still listed with an Add to Bag button.

**Verdict:** If you have ever wanted a California dial and did not want to pay vintage Rolex or Panerai money for the look, this is the cheapest legitimate way in from a major brand right now. A hand wound movement, a numbered caseback and a 34 mm case that wears like a real 1960s watch make it a genuine small run collectible for under $300. Just know what you are buying: domed acrylic, 30 m water resistance, and a movement Timex does not name on its own page.

## What a California dial actually is

The California dial splits the hour numerals in two. Roman numerals run across the top half, Arabic numerals fill the bottom half, and plain markers sit at the cardinal spots. On the TW6A28700, Timex puts Romans at 10, 11, 1 and 2, Arabics at 4, 5, 7 and 8, and stick markers at 3, 6 and 9, all in gold tone on a black dial. It is a layout that looks busy on paper and somehow reads instantly on the wrist, which is exactly why collectors chase it.

What makes this release smart is the canvas. The Marlin is Timex's 1960s dress watch, and its small round case with a tall domed crystal is the right shape for a vintage dial like this. A California dial on a 42 mm modern sports watch can feel like a costume. On a 34 mm Marlin it looks like something you found in your grandfather's dresser drawer.

## The specs Timex lists

Here is what Timex publishes for the Marlin California Dial Limited Edition:

* Case: polished stainless steel, 34 mm diameter
* Crystal: domed acrylic
* Dial: black California dial with gold tone Roman and Arabic numerals, stick markers at 3, 6 and 9, and a gold tone handset with lume in the hands
* Movement: mechanical, hand wound
* Water resistance: 30 m
* Strap: brown leather with quick release spring bars
* Weight: 33.2 g
* Edition: 200 pieces, each with its number engraved on the caseback
* Price: $299, numbers 101 to 200 sold exclusively on timex.com

Two details are worth flagging. Timex's spec sheet lists a case height of 13.5 mm, while the Windup Watch Shop listing for the fair allocation gives 10.5 mm thickness, and the difference likely comes down to whether the tall domed crystal is counted. Windup also lists a 41 mm lug to lug, an 18 mm lug width and a Seagull hand wound movement. Timex's own page simply says mechanical hand wound, so treat the Seagull detail as the retailer's information rather than Timex's.

## Why hand wound matters here

Most watches at this price run on quartz or an entry automatic. A hand wound movement changes how you live with the watch. You give the crown a few turns in the morning, you feel the mainspring tighten, and the watch is part of your routine instead of a gadget on your wrist. Timex leans into exactly that idea in its launch post, and for a vintage styled dress watch it is the right call. There is no rotor, so the case stays slim in the hand and the experience stays close to what a 1960s Marlin owner had.

The tradeoffs are honest ones for $299. Acrylic scratches more easily than sapphire, though it polishes out with a bit of compound. Thirty meters means hand washing and rain, not swimming. And a 34 mm case will look small to anyone used to a 40 mm diver. For a dress watch, that size is the point.

## Also new: four monochrome G-Shocks in Japan

Casio is rolling out a black and gray monochrome treatment across three classic G-Shock shapes, and all four models are now listed for Japan. The DW-5600MTM-8 square and the DW-5900MTM-8, with its three ring display that dates to 1992, are ¥15,950 each. The DW-6900MTM-8, the biggest of the group at 53.2 x 50 x 18.7 mm with metal front buttons, is ¥17,050, according to Notebookcheck. The standout is the DWE-5600MTM-8, which swaps the usual resin strap for a patchwork band of honed stainless steel links, urethane, fine resin and a translucent resin piece, plus a gray mesh print on the dial. Japanese G-Shock retailer Oomiya lists it as a November 2026 release at ¥28,050 including tax, with 200 m water resistance, Carbon Core Guard construction and a white Super Illuminator LED.

Global availability has not been confirmed by Casio yet, so treat any US date you see as unconfirmed until it shows up on Casio's US site. If you plan to swap the strap on the square, our [DW-5600 strap guide](https://mrtictoctalk.com/strap-finder/casio-g-shock-dw-5600/) explains why standard spring bar straps do not fit without an adapter.
