If you grew up in the 1980s or 1990s, the TAG Heuer Formula 1 was probably the first "real" watch you ever wanted. Now TAG Heuer has taken that exact energy and wrapped it in Red Bull team colors. To mark **ten years of partnership with Oracle Red Bull Racing**, which started in 2016, the brand has released the **Formula 1 Solargraph x Oracle Red Bull Racing, reference WBY111F.BA0042**, in a **38 mm** case, and [TAG Heuer's US site](https://www.tagheuer.com/us/en/timepieces/collections/tag-heuer-formula-1/38-mm-th50-00/WBY111F.BA0042.html) lists it at **$2,250 USD** and ready to ship. Every spec below is checked against that page on November 15, 2026, with [Time and Watches](https://www.timeandwatches.com/2026/11/tag-heuer-formula-1-solargraph-oracle-red-bull-racing-wby111f.html) filling in the details. Then we hit a seriously upgraded Vulcain diver and two very different Citizens.

## TAG Heuer Formula 1 Solargraph x Oracle Red Bull Racing

Start with the dial, because it is pure race team. The **blue sunray brushed** face takes its color straight from the Red Bull livery, a **red flange** carries the seconds and minutes scale, and the black lacquered indexes and hands are filled with Super LumiNova that looks white by day and glows green at night. Then TAG Heuer adds the fun part: a **yellow central seconds hand** that matches the yellow framed **date window at 3 o'clock**. Flip it over and the screw down caseback carries the Oracle Red Bull Racing logo, a checkered flag pattern and the TAG Heuer shield.

The bigger news for collectors is the size. Time and Watches points out that this release brings a **38 mm case** to this version of the Formula 1, echoing the proportions of the early models from the 1980s. At **10.2 mm thick** per TAG Heuer, this should wear the way the originals did: small, light and easy.

### Why Solargraph makes sense here

The TH50-00 runs entirely on light, natural or artificial. TAG Heuer says **a minute of direct sunlight powers a full day**, a full charge in under 40 hours of light gives **up to ten months** in total darkness, and the battery is rated for up to 15 years. For a watch that is meant to be thrown on and worn hard, that is exactly the right engine. No winding, no dead battery surprises.

### My take

Let's be honest about the price. $2,250 is a lot for quartz, even smart solar quartz, and the standard Formula 1 Solargraph models cost less. What you are paying for is the team connection, the 38 mm size and a color combo that pops like nothing else in TAG Heuer's catalog right now. If you are a Red Bull fan, or you just want the most fun, grab and go version of an icon, this is a home run. If you only care about value per dollar, a regular Formula 1 Solargraph gets you the same movement for less.

## Vulcain Skindiver Mark II: the skin diver that grew up

Vulcain made dive watches starting in 1960, and its Skindiver Nautique returned in 2023 as a faithful remake. The new **Skindiver Mark II** keeps that 1960s look and fixes almost every spec people complained about, according to [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/vulcain-skindiver-mark-ii-launch/) and [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/13/five-dials-on-a-skin-diver-that-finally-takes-depth-seriously/).

The case grows to **39 mm** (1 mm bigger than the Mark I) and **13.2 mm** thick, and water resistance jumps to **300 m**. The dial becomes a **sandwich dial**, with the triangle, dot and dash markers cut out of the top layer to show the lume underneath. The bezel gets a **fully lumed black ceramic insert**, and inside, the ETA 2824 is swapped for a **COSC certified Soprod M300** automatic, taking the power reserve from 38 to **60 hours**. You can see it through a sapphire caseback.

The Swiss franc prices are from The Gadgeteer, and the USD conversions use the ECB reference rate of November 13, 2026 (CHF 1 = about $1.1927). My view: at this money, a COSC chronometer with 60 hours, 300 m and a ceramic bezel in a 39 mm vintage case is a serious value play against the bigger names. If you liked the old Skindiver's looks but hated the 38 hour reserve, this is the one to wait for on November 24. Planning a strap swap? ## Citizen Promaster Land GMT BJ7150-50E: sapphire and solar for $595

On the affordable side, [Citizen's US site](https://citizenwatch.com/us/en/product/BJ7150-50E) now lists the **Promaster Land GMT BJ7150-50E** at **$595 USD**, and [Gizmochina](https://www.gizmochina.com/2026/11/15/citizen-promaster-land-gmt-watch-launched-specs-price/) reports it as a new US and UK release at GBP 399 in Britain. You get a **39.5 mm** steel case, a **fixed 24 hour bezel**, a black dial with red GMT accents, a date at 3 o'clock, an **anti reflective curved sapphire crystal**, **200 m** of water resistance and the **Eco-Drive Caliber B878** light powered movement, all on a steel bracelet with a push button fold over clasp. It even ships in a Promaster tackle box.

My take: a sapphire, solar, 200 m GMT under $600 is a very easy way into travel watches. Just know the bezel is fixed, so it tracks one second zone in 24 hour format rather than rotating for a third. If you want the full picture of GMTs at every budget, see the guide linked below.

## Also notable: Campanola gets Citizen's new Y520 automatic

This one is Japan only, but it matters. Citizen's luxury Campanola line announced the **Small Second Mechanical** on November 10, powered by the new **Caliber Y520**, a movement developed by Swiss maker La Joux-Perret and tailored for Campanola. [Citizen Japan](https://www.citizen.jp/news/2026/20261110.html) calls it Campanola's first new mechanical movement in 12 years. Two limited models go on sale in Japan on **December 3**: the **NZ4000-56E Amamitsuhoshi** (black lacquer with mother of pearl inlay, 100 pieces) and the **NZ4000-56N Kojo** (engraved white mother of pearl, 70 pieces), each at about **$5,156 USD (JPY 825,000 incl. tax)**. Both use a **40 mm** steel case, **13.9 mm** thick, with 60 hours of reserve and 5 bar water resistance. No US release has been announced.
