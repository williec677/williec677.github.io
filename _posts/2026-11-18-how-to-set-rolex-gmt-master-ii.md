The GMT Master II is one of the greatest travel watches ever made, and it is also the watch I get the most setup questions about. Which hand is home time? Why did the date just jump? Do I turn the bezel or the crown? Let me clear it up once and for all.

This guide covers the modern GMT Master II with calibre 3285, the movement Rolex introduced in 2018 and still uses today. The steps come straight from Rolex's own [GMT Master II user guide](https://media.rolex.com/rolexcom/media/user-guides/gmt-master-ii/rolex_gmt-master-ii_en-us.pdf), translated into plain English. If you own a GMT Master II from 1982 onward, the logic is the same, because the independent hour hand has been part of the GMT Master II since calibre 3085.

## Meet the four hands

Look at the dial and you will see four hands:

* **Hour hand** with the Mercedes style tip. This shows **local time**, wherever you are standing right now.
* **Minute hand.** Shared by everything.
* **Seconds hand.** Shared by everything.
* **24 hour hand,** the thin hand with the arrow or triangle tip, painted green on the 126710GRNR. It goes around the dial once a day and, read against the 24 hour bezel, shows **reference time**, which Rolex also calls home time.

The bezel turns in both directions and is graduated from 1 to 24. When the triangle on the bezel sits at 12 o'clock, Rolex calls that the neutral position. That is where the bezel lives while you set the watch.

## The crown positions

The crown on a GMT Master II has four positions, and getting them straight is 90 percent of the battle.

* **Position 0:** screwed down against the case. This is where it lives day to day, and it keeps the watch waterproof.
* **Position 1:** unscrewed, sitting against the case. Turn it clockwise to wind the movement.
* **Position 2:** pulled out to the first notch. This moves the local hour hand in one hour jumps, forward or backward, and the date changes with it.
* **Position 3:** pulled all the way out to the second notch. The seconds hand stops, and you set the 24 hour hand and the minute hand together.

Rolex is very clear on one rule: never unscrew the crown underwater.

## Step 1: wind it

If the watch has stopped, give it a few turns in position 1 before you set it. The calibre 3285 has a power reserve of approximately 70 hours, according to [Rolex's spec sheet for the 126710GRNR](https://www.rolex.com/en-us/watches/gmt-master-ii/m126710grnr-0004), so a stopped watch has usually been off the wrist for about three days.

## Step 2: set home time first (crown in position 3)

Order matters here. Rolex's user guide says to set the reference time first, because setting it also moves the regular hour hand.

1. Unscrew the crown and pull it all the way out to position 3. The seconds hand stops, so you can set the watch to the second.
2. Put the bezel in the neutral position, triangle at 12.
3. Turn the crown in either direction until the 24 hour hand points to your home hour on the bezel and the minute hand shows the right minutes.

Pick your home zone carefully. Most people use the city they live in. Pilots and frequent flyers sometimes use UTC, the modern successor to the Greenwich Mean Time that gave the watch its name.

## Step 3: set the date and local time (crown in position 2)

1. Push the crown in one notch to position 2.
2. Turn the crown in either direction. The hour hand jumps one hour at a time while the minutes, seconds, and 24 hour hand keep running without interruption.
3. Every time the hour hand passes midnight, the date changes. Going forward moves the date forward. Going backward moves it back. Rolex notes the date changes after two complete turns of the hour hand around the dial, which makes sense, since a day is 24 hours.
4. Keep jumping until the date and the local hour are both correct, then stop on the right hour.
5. Push the crown in and screw it down firmly to position 0.

The one trap here is noon versus midnight. Because the date is linked to the local hour hand, it is possible to set the right hour but on the wrong half of the day. If the date flips at lunchtime, you are 12 hours off. Jump the hour hand forward 12 more hours and you are fixed.

There is no separate quick set for the date on a GMT Master II. You set the date by moving the local hour hand. That is by design, and it means the date always matches local time.

## Real life: landing in a new time zone

This is where the GMT Master II earns its legend. Let us say you live in New York and fly to London, five hours ahead.

1. Unscrew the crown to position 1, then pull it to position 2.
2. Jump the hour hand forward five hours. If that takes you past midnight, the date moves forward too.
3. Push in and screw down.

That is it. The watch never stopped running, your minute hand is still precise, and the 24 hour hand on the bezel still shows New York time. When you fly home, jump the hour hand back five hours. Daylight saving time changes work the same way, one hour at a time.

## Bonus: a third time zone with the bezel

Want to track a third zone, like a colleague in Tokyo? Turn the bezel. Count the hour difference between your home zone and the third zone, then rotate the bezel that many hours. Now the 24 hour hand reads the third zone on the bezel, while the hour hand still shows local time.

The catch, as Rolex points out, is that while the bezel is turned, you can no longer read home time on it. Put the triangle back to 12 and home time returns. Three zones are trackable, two are visible at any moment.

## A quick history of why this works

The original GMT Master arrived in 1955. On that watch, the hour hand and the 24 hour hand were linked, so you used the bezel to read a second zone. According to [Rolex's GMT Master II brochure](https://newsroom-content.rolex.com/-/media/project/rolex/newsroom/rolex/rolex-newsroom-int/2024-04/gmt-master-ii/documents/brochures/03_gmt-master-ii_brochure_english.pdf), the big change came in 1982 with calibre 3085, which let the hour hand be set independently without stopping the watch. That movement gave birth to the GMT Master II. Calibre 3186 followed in 2005 with the blue Parachrom hairspring, and calibre 3285 arrived in 2018.

## Troubleshooting

* **The date changed at noon.** You are 12 hours off on the local hand. Jump forward 12 hours in position 2.
* **Home time is wrong but local time is right.** Pull to position 3, reset the 24 hour hand and minutes, then go back to position 2 and correct the local hour.
* **The 24 hour hand looks off by a few hours.** Check the bezel. If it is not in the neutral position, you are reading a third zone.
* **The watch stopped overnight.** Wind it in position 1. If it keeps stopping with regular wear, it may need service.

## Quick takeaways

* Set home time first: crown in position 3, bezel at neutral, 24 hour hand and minutes together.
* Then set date and local time: crown in position 2, hour hand jumps one hour at a time.
* The date follows the local hour hand. If it flips at noon, jump 12 hours.
* Turn the bezel to read a third zone, then return it to neutral for home time.
* Always screw the crown down, and never operate it underwater.
