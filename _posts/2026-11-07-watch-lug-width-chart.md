Here is the cheapest way to make a watch feel brand new: put it on a different strap. Here is the fastest way to waste money: buy that strap in the wrong size. One millimeter sounds like nothing until you see a gap at each end of the lugs, or you are wrestling a strap that bunches up and refuses to sit flat.

So I made you a cheat sheet. Twenty of the most popular watches people actually wear and actually strap up, each with its lug width pulled from my [Watch Strap Finder](/strap-finder/), where every size was checked against a brand page or a trusted watch database. ## The watch lug width chart

Lug width is the distance between the inside faces of the lugs, the gap your strap or bracelet slides into. It is always quoted in millimeters, and it is the number you shop by.

## The generation traps that burn buyers

The chart is the easy part. The expensive mistakes happen when two watches share a name and not a size. These are the ones I see trip people up over and over.

**Submariner 40 vs Submariner 41.** The Maxi case Sub that ran from 2010 to 2020 is 20mm. The 2020 redesign moved to 21mm. If you trade up from a 116610LN to a 126610LN, your old strap drawer does not come with you.

**Explorer 36 vs everything around it.** This one is sneaky. The 2021 Explorer 124270 is 19mm, while the older 36mm 114270 and 14270 are 20mm. Go bigger and it shifts again: the [Explorer 39](/strap-finder/rolex-explorer-39/) is 20mm and the [Explorer 40](/strap-finder/rolex-explorer-40/) is 21mm. Same family, three different answers.

**Sea Dweller sizes.** The 43mm [Sea Dweller 126600](/strap-finder/rolex-sea-dweller-126600/) is 22mm, but the older 40mm 116600 was 20mm. Know your reference before you click buy.

**Big case, small case.** Brands often change lug width with case size. The 42mm Hamilton Khaki Field Auto steps up to 22mm while the 38mm versions stay at 20mm. The midsize Seiko SKX013 is 20mm, so it will not share straps with a 22mm SKX007. Orient's larger 40.5mm Bambino uses a different width than the 38mm in the chart. Tudor's Pelagos 42 is 22mm, not the 21mm of the Pelagos 39.

Rule of thumb: the model name gets you close, the reference number gets you the right answer.

## How to measure lug width yourself

Watch not on the chart? Measure it. Takes two minutes.

1. **Grab a measuring tool with millimeter marks.** A cheap set of {% include aff.html network="amazon" url="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=digital+calipers" text="digital calipers" %} is the best option by a mile. A clear ruler works in a pinch.
2. **Measure the gap, not the lugs.** Place the jaws or ruler across the space between the inside faces of the two lugs, right where the strap enters. Ignore the outside width of the case.
3. **Read it in millimeters and round carefully.** Lug widths come in whole millimeters, and odd sizes like 19mm and 21mm are real. If you read something between two sizes, measure again rather than rounding to the size that is easier to shop.
4. **Double check with the old strap.** Measure the strap or bracelet end where it meets the case. It should match the gap.
5. **Confirm against a real spec.** Brand product pages and watch databases list it for most modern models. That is exactly how the finder was built.

If you want to swap straps at home, a decent {% include aff.html network="amazon" url="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=watch+spring+bar+tool" text="spring bar tool" %} is the one tool worth owning. Put a strip of tape on the lugs before you start, because one slip will leave a scratch you will see every day.

## Bracelet vs strap fit: what the number does not tell you

Lug width tells you a strap will fit between the lugs. It does not tell you how it will look or wear. A few things to keep in mind.

**Bracelets are fitted, straps are universal.** Factory bracelets use end links shaped to one case. A 20mm bracelet from one watch rarely sits flush on a different 20mm watch, even if the gap is identical. Straps are far more forgiving, which is why they are the fun part of this hobby.

**Do not cheat the odd sizes.** A 20mm strap on a 21mm watch leaves a visible gap and lets the strap slide sideways. A 22mm strap jammed into 21mm bunches and strains the spring bars. When a watch is 19mm or 21mm, shop that exact size. Plenty of budget straps only come in 20mm and 22mm, so the odd sizes take a little more hunting, and that is fine.

**Lug shape matters.** Rolex sports models put the lug holes close to the case, so curved end rubber or curved spring bars give you the cleanest line. The Seamaster 300M has long, curved lugs that hug curved end rubber well. The MoonSwatch has short bioceramic lugs and a light case, so thin NATO or hook and loop straps suit it better than thick leather.

**Some watches play by their own rules.** The Panerai Luminor 44 uses a tube and screw system on many cases, so check your lug hardware before you order. A few watches skip normal lug width entirely, which brings us to the bonus round.

## FAQ

### What is the most common watch lug width?

On this chart it is 20mm by a wide margin. Eleven of the twenty watches take it, including the Speedmaster Professional, the current GMT Master II, the Datejust 36 and the Black Bay 58.

### Can I use a 20mm strap on a 21mm watch?

You can physically fit it, but it will leave a gap and shift side to side. Buy the exact size for the best look and the most secure fit.

### Is lug width the same as strap width?

At the lugs, yes. Many straps then taper toward the buckle, so a strap listed for a 20mm watch may be narrower at the clasp end. Always shop by the lug end measurement.

### Where can I find the lug width for a watch not on this chart?

Check the [Watch Strap Finder](/strap-finder/) first. If your watch is not there yet, measure the gap between the lugs with calipers and confirm it against the brand's product page.
