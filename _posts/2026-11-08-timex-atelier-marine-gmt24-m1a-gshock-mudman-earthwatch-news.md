Timex just gave its Swiss made Atelier line the two colorways collectors have been waiting for. The Marine M1a now wears a polished blue enamel dial and a matching blue ceramic bezel, and the GMT24 M1a finally gets a red and blue 24 hour scale with a red GMT hand. Both are on sale now at Timex. Casio also launched this year's Love The Sea And The Earth Mudman in Japan, a shoebill inspired collaboration with Earthwatch Japan. I checked every size, price, and spec below against [Timex product pages](https://timex.com/blogs/the-timex-blog/timex-atelier-new-expressions-of-modern-watchmaking) and Japanese retailer listings on November 8, 2026.

## Timex Atelier Marine M1a: the blue diver from $950

The Marine M1a is the dive watch that launched Atelier in fall 2025, and the black originals have been tough to keep in stock. Blue was the obvious next move, and Timex executed it as a full color story rather than a timid accent. The polished blue enamel dial gets silver printing, applied indices filled with Swiss Luminova, and high polished silver hands that are filled with lume too. A unidirectional bezel carries a blue ceramic insert, so the dial and the bezel actually match.

The case is the same sculpted 41 mm stainless steel design with a skeletonized architecture and a plated steel middle case. Timex's November 5 announcement describes that middle case as black IP coated. A screw down crown backs up the 200 m water resistance. Crystal is double curved sapphire. Flip it over and an exhibition caseback shows the Swiss automatic.

Per the [TW6A20500 product page](https://timex.com/products/timex-atelier-marine-m1a-41mm-swiss-made-automatic-nbr-synthetic-rubber-strap-tw6a20500) and the [TW6A20400 bracelet page](https://timex.com/products/timex-atelier-marine-m1a-41mm-swiss-made-automatic-stainless-steel-bracelet-tw6a20400), the key numbers are:

* **Case:** 41 mm diameter, 48.11 mm lug to lug, 13 mm thick, brushed stainless steel
* **Lug width:** 20 mm
* **Crystal:** double curved sapphire
* **Water resistance:** 200 m
* **Dial:** blue enamel
* **Bezel:** unidirectional with a blue ceramic insert
* **Movement:** Catena S.A. caliber SA100, Swiss made automatic, 29 jewels, hacking seconds, rated accuracy of ±15 seconds per day
* **Power reserve:** approximately 38 hours on the product pages (the November 5 Timex blog post said 36 hours)
* **Frequency:** 28,800 vibrations per hour, per the [Timex announcement](https://timex.com/blogs/the-timex-blog/timex-atelier-new-expressions-of-modern-watchmaking)

Two versions are live and in stock on Timex US as of this check:

* [TW6A20500](https://timex.com/products/timex-atelier-marine-m1a-41mm-swiss-made-automatic-nbr-synthetic-rubber-strap-tw6a20500) on a matching blue NBR rubber strap with a deployant clasp, $950
* [TW6A20400](https://timex.com/products/timex-atelier-marine-m1a-41mm-swiss-made-automatic-stainless-steel-bracelet-tw6a20400) on a self adjustable stainless steel bracelet with a butterfly deployant clasp, $1,050

Both straps fit wrists from 150 to 205 mm. The steel bracelet uses removable links and, like the rest of the Atelier hardware, quick release spring bars. NBR is nitrile butadiene rubber, which Timex pitches as more resistant to oils, chemicals, and wear than ordinary rubber.

My take? A 41 mm, 13 mm, 200 m Swiss automatic with enamel, ceramic, and sapphire at $950 is a serious value shot from a brand most people still file under field watches and Ironmans. The 38 hour reserve is the honest weak spot, short for a modern automatic, so this wants wrist time. If you already like the Atelier design language, blue is the one that will get compliments. ## Timex Atelier GMT24 M1a: red and blue traveler energy from $1,350

The GMT24 M1a is the travel watch in the line, and this is the colorway people asked Timex for. [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/07/timex-atelier-gmt24-m1a-pepsi/) notes the first version launched in black with an orange GMT hand. The new one keeps the black enamel dial and adds a red and blue 24 hour scale under the glass, plus an eye catching red GMT hand. There is no rotating two tone bezel. The colors live on a fixed ring under the glass box sapphire, which keeps them safe from knocks and lets the whole scale take lume.

That red and blue pairing is the classic traveler GMT look, the same language collectors chase on a Rolex GMT Master II. If you want more options in that color family without jumping to five figures, the {% assign alt = site.posts | where: "slug", "affordable-rolex-gmt-alternatives" | first %}{% if alt %}[affordable Rolex GMT alternatives guide]({{ alt.url | absolute_url }}){% else %}affordable Rolex GMT alternatives guide{% endif %} is already live on the Journal.

Per the [TW6A20700 product page](https://timex.com/products/timex-atelier-gmt24-m1a-40mm-swiss-made-automatic-nbr-synthetic-rubber-strap-tw6a20700) and the [TW6A20600 bracelet page](https://timex.com/products/timex-atelier-gmt24-m1a-40mm-swiss-made-automatic-stainless-steel-bracelet-tw6a20600):

* **Case:** 40 mm diameter, 48.11 mm lug to lug, 12.7 mm thick, brushed stainless steel with a gunmetal IP coated middle case
* **Lug width:** 20 mm, quick release spring bars
* **Crystal:** glass box sapphire with triple anti reflective coating
* **Water resistance:** 100 m, with a screw down crown
* **Dial:** black enamel, date window beside 3 o'clock, Swiss Luminova
* **Movement:** Landeron caliber L24 GMT, Swiss made automatic, 28 jewels, 28,800 vibrations per hour, approximately 40 hour power reserve, hacking seconds, rated accuracy of ±15 seconds per day
* **Function:** independently useful 24 hour hand against the red and blue scale, plus the date

Two versions are live and in stock on Timex US as of this check:

* [TW6A20700](https://timex.com/products/timex-atelier-gmt24-m1a-40mm-swiss-made-automatic-nbr-synthetic-rubber-strap-tw6a20700) on a black NBR rubber strap with a deployant clasp, $1,350
* [TW6A20600](https://timex.com/products/timex-atelier-gmt24-m1a-40mm-swiss-made-automatic-stainless-steel-bracelet-tw6a20600) on a self adjustable stainless steel bracelet with a butterfly deployant clasp, $1,450

Same 150 to 205 mm wrist range as the Marine. The bracelet tapers and uses those tool free self adjustable links, which is the kind of everyday courtesy more brands at this price should copy.

My take? At 40 mm and 12.7 mm, this is the more wearable of the two new Ateliers, and the colors do the heavy lifting. A 40 hour reserve is still short, and 100 m is swimming rather than diving. What you are buying is Swiss GMT function, enamel, and that glass box sapphire at a price that undercuts most Swiss travel watches with the same vibe. ## Also new: G-Shock Mudman GW-9503KJ-2JR, the shoebill Earthwatch collab

Casio's annual Love The Sea And The Earth project has its 2026 watch, and the animal this year is the shoebill. The GW-9503KJ-2JR is a MASTER OF G Mudman built with Earthwatch Japan and colored after the tall African wetland bird, which the IUCN Red List classifies as Vulnerable. [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/07/casio-g-shock-mudman-gw-9503kj-shoebill-earthwatch/) and [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Casio-debuts-new-G-Shock-Mudman-in-collaboration-with-Earthwatch-Japan.1439480.0.html) both covered the drop over the weekend. [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/07/casio-g-shock-mudman-gw-9503kj-shoebill-earthwatch/) notes last year's Earthwatch Mudman was themed on the hippopotamus.

Japanese retailer listings, including [The Clock House](https://shop.theclockhouse.jp/products/gw-9503kj-2jr) and [oomiya](https://www.jw-oomiya.co.jp/products/gw-9503kj-2jr), put the design details and the hardware in one place. The main color is blue gray, taken from the shoebill's plumage, with a blue gray ion plated metal bezel meant to catch light like feathers. Gold on the dial ring and the front buttons, plus a light beige strap keeper, nods at that huge shoe shaped bill. Earthwatch Japan's logo sits on the keeper, the band is printed with "Change the World. yourself.", and the LED backlight reveals the organization's logo in the display. The caseback carries the Love The Sea And The Earth symbol, and the watch ships in a recycled paper box. A portion of sales supports Earthwatch Japan's work.

Under the colors, this is a full Mudman. Retailer spec sheets list:

* **Reference:** GW-9503KJ-2JR
* **Module:** 3553
* **Size and weight:** 56.7 mm by 52.7 mm by 14.8 mm, 88 g
* **Case and band:** carbon fiber reinforced resin and resin in biomass plastic, stainless steel bezel with blue gray IP, biomass plastic resin band
* **Crystal:** mineral glass
* **Water resistance:** 20 bar, 200 m
* **Construction:** shock resistant, mud resistant, Carbon Core Guard
* **Power and timekeeping:** Tough Solar and Multiband 6, covering Japan, North America, Europe, and China
* **Sensors:** compass, barometer, altimeter, and thermometer
* **Other:** duplex LCD, world time for 48 cities plus UTC, sunrise and sunset, 1/10 second stopwatch, 24 hour timer, five alarms, Super Illuminator LED
* **Runtime after a full charge, no further light:** about 6 months in normal use, or about 26 months in power saving
* **Accuracy without radio reception:** average monthly rate of ±15 seconds
* **Band fit:** 145 to 215 mm
* **Price:** ¥64,900 including tax
* **Availability:** Japan, with The Clock House listing an on sale date of November 13, 2026. [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/07/casio-g-shock-mudman-gw-9503kj-shoebill-earthwatch/) converts that retail price to about $410 and says preorders are open at Casio Japan. US availability is not confirmed.

My take? This is a collector G-Shock, not a must copy of the standard Mudman. You are paying for the shoebill palette, the metal bezel, and the charity collab, and you are buying it from Japan. If you want the same sensors, solar charging, and radio sync without the import hunt, the regular GW-9500 is the practical daily.
