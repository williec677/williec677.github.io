Black Friday morning, and the best watch news has nothing to do with a sale banner. Christie's just set up a serious December 4 auction in New York, the day before Phillips' big two day sale, which turns next week into the busiest week of the year for watch collectors in America. On the release side, a California engraver dropped a 20 piece watch that is cut entirely by hand, and Favre Leuba's CEO went on record about bringing back one of its most famous names. Here is what matters.

## Christie's Important Watches, New York, December 4

**Announced:** Christie's press releases of [October 22](https://press.christies.com/christies-presents-an-exceptionally-rare-patek-philippe-dual-time-ref-34521-as-a-highlight-of-december-luxury-week-in-new-york/) and [November 23](https://press.christies.com/christies-presents-important-watches-during-new-york-luxury-week/), 2026 ([sale page](https://www.christies.com/auction/auction-24504-nyr/overview)); highlights rounded up today by [Oracle of Time](https://oracleoftime.com/christies-new-york-watch-sale/)

The live sale runs at 10:00 AM ET on Friday, December 4, at Christie's Rockefeller Plaza galleries (20 Rockefeller Plaza), lots 1 to 130. Public viewing is December 2 and 3, 10:00 AM to 5:00 PM, per the sale page. Another 127 lots go into [Watches Online: The New York Edit](https://www.christies.com/auction/auction-24505-nyr), from December 2 to 11.

Christie's comes in hot. Its Rare Watches sale in Geneva on November 9 made **CHF 47,824,592 (US $58,703,706)**, which Christie's says is a 221% jump year on year with every lot sold. Christie's also says 19% of bidders were new to the house and Millennials and Gen Z made up 40% of registrants. That is the momentum New York is trying to ride.

### The headliner: Patek Philippe Dual Time 3452/1 by Louis Cottier

This is the kind of lot that makes specialists lose sleep. Per Christie's [lot page](https://www.christies.com/en/lot/lot-6619701) and [October 22 press release](https://press.christies.com/christies-presents-an-exceptionally-rare-patek-philippe-dual-time-ref-34521-as-a-highlight-of-december-luxury-week-in-new-york/):

* **What it is:** Patek Philippe Dual Time ref. 3452/1, designed by Louis Cottier, the watchmaker behind the world time system. Lot 26.
* **Rarity:** one of only seven examples made across the 3452 and 3452/1, and Christie's says each one differs in at least one design detail, so each is unique. Two of the seven are in the Patek Philippe Museum in Geneva.
* **Provenance:** sold new on October 30, 1961 (per the Patek Philippe Extract from the Archives cited by Christie's) and kept by the original owner's family ever since. Christie's calls it the last of the seven to surface and the first 3452 or 3452/1 on the market since 2006.
* **Case and dial:** 39 mm, 18k white and yellow gold, screw down caseback, white matte hobnail guilloché dial with two applied gold time zone chapters and a running aperture
* **Movement:** automatic caliber 27-460 modified by Cottier, so one movement runs both dials. They can run together or be set independently by pushing the button at 2 o'clock and turning the crown.
* **Bracelet:** integrated two tone gold bracelet by Jean Pierre Ecoffey, about 200 mm long
* **Estimate:** **$800,000 to $1,600,000 USD**

Why it matters: most vintage Patek dual time watches of the era are manual wind. This one is automatic, 39 mm (huge for 1961), and on a full gold bracelet, and you could previously only see one in a museum. If the 2026 auction run continues, that estimate may not hold it.

## Akmaev Time Adrift: 20 pieces, every wave cut by hand

**Announced:** November 27, 2026 ([SJX](https://watchesbysjx.com/2026/11/artur-akmaev-time-adrift.html), [Buying Time](https://www.buyingtime.news/artur-akmaevs-time-adrift-a-freehand-engraved-watch-inspired-by-the-sea/), [Akmaev](https://akmaev.com/))

Artur Akmaev is a California based engraver and watchmaker, in Santa Monica per SJX, who trained at the Carl Fabergé College of Arts and Crafts in Moscow, moved to the US in 2017, and engraves and finishes parts for other independents including David Walter and Josh Shapiro, per his site. His earlier pieces leaned big and loud (think 45 mm and pop culture themes). The Time Adrift is the opposite: small, personal and seriously good looking.

The solid silver dial is frosted and engraved freehand with a burin into a stormy sea, and a few numerals look like the tide knocked them out of place. SJX says the numerals from 12 to 6 are engraved and the rest are cut and applied one by one. Two versions: **Black Tide** and **Cobalt Blue Tide**, the blue one with lacquer filling the engraved numerals. The hands are solid white gold, engraved freehand and deliberately left unpolished so the cuts stay crisp. Because it is all freehand, no two dials will match.

### Specs (per Akmaev and SJX)

* **Case:** 316L stainless steel, 37 mm, 8.8 mm thick (7.4 mm without the domed sapphire crystal), 45.1 mm lug to lug, 19 mm lug width, 50 m
* **Movement:** La Joux Perret F200, hand wound, 3 Hz, 90 hour power reserve, 21 jewels, 2.7 mm thick; Akmaev engraves and refinishes the bridges and does final assembly and regulation himself
* **Functions:** hours and minutes
* **Strap:** leather with a steel pin buckle
* **Limited:** 20 pieces in total

**Price, and a gap you should know about:** SJX reports **US $12,000 excluding taxes**. Falk Watches, the US retailer listed on Akmaev's own site, [listed the Time Adrift on preorder](https://falkwatches.com/products/akmaev-time-adrift-pre-order) at **$9,800 USD** with estimated delivery in Q1 2027. That preorder page showed Sold Out on November 27. Akmaev's site does not publish a price, so confirm with the retailer before you commit.

**My take:** if you liked the hand engraved Holthinrichs dials [we covered on Tuesday]({% post_url 2026-11-24-holthinrichs-heritage-hand-engraved-timex-worn-wound-protocol-001-news %}), this is the same idea pushed further, with one person cutting the dial, the hands and the movement. That 19 mm lug width is a slightly odd size, so if you plan to swap straps, the [Watch Strap Finder](https://mrtictoctalk.com/strap-finder/) is a good place to start.

## Favre Leuba says a new Bivouac is on the way

**Source:** Favre Leuba CEO Patrik Hoffmann, interviewed by [Oracle of Time](https://oracleoftime.com/favre-leuba-ceo-patrik-hoffmann-on-the-bivouacs-return/) (published November 27, 2026)

The Bivouac is the Favre Leuba name collectors know: Oracle of Time describes it as the first watch with an altimeter and barometer. Asked directly if it is coming back, Hoffmann said: "It is going to be launched soon, and this is not a secret. A new Bivouac. I hope it will be the key product at Watches and Wonders 2027. It might be a few months later, perhaps, but we're trying."

**What is confirmed:** the CEO says a new Bivouac is in the works and hopes it will be the key product at Watches and Wonders 2027. **What is not:** no design, size, movement, price or firm date. Hoffmann also said the previous ownership's Bivouac was 18 mm thick and 48 mm wide, "unwearable" in his words, and he called the new one "very nice, and wearable." Treat this as an on the record teaser, not a launch.

**My take:** Favre Leuba has been on a roll with neo vintage pieces like the Harpoon and the [34 mm Deep Raider Ladies]({% post_url 2026-11-23-favre-leuba-deep-raider-ladies-34mm-news %}) we covered on Monday. A wearable mechanical altimeter watch would be a real flex in that mid price bracket. We will be watching Geneva.
