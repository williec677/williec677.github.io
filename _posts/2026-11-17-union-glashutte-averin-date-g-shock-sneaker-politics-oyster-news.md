Everybody wants an integrated bracelet sports watch, and almost everybody makes it round. Union Glashütte just went the other way. The Saxon brand took its square Averin, a case shape it has been running since 2008, stripped out the chronograph, shrank it way down, and bolted on a faceted steel bracelet. The result is the Averin Date, and at EUR 2,450 it lands right in the zone where a lot of you are shopping for that first serious German watch. Then, for something completely different, G-Shock and Louisiana sneaker shop Sneaker Politics turned the chunky DW6900 into a translucent purple oyster shell. Let's go.

## Union Glashütte Averin Date

**Announced:** November 17, 2026 ([Union Glashütte](https://www.union-glashuette.com/en_int/d0155071104100.html), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/union-glashutte-union-glashutte-square-integrated-steel-sports-watch-introducing-price/))

Quick context if the name is new to you. Union Glashütte is the more accessible sibling to Glashütte Original inside the Swatch Group, building mechanical watches in the same small Saxon town that gave us A. Lange & Söhne. Until today the Averin line was all about racing inspired chronographs, chunky ones too: Monochrome lists those at 41 mm by 41 mm and 15.45 mm thick. The collection got a full refresh in 2024, and this is the first time it steps away from the stopwatch.

### A much smaller, much slimmer square

This is the part that made me sit up. The Averin Date measures 37 mm by 37 mm with a 41.4 mm lug to lug. Monochrome puts the thickness at 9.85 mm, and the brand's own spec sheet rounds it to 10 mm. That is a huge drop from the chronograph and puts it in proper everyday wear territory. A square case wears bigger than its number because there is more metal in the corners, so 37 mm square on a short 41.4 mm lug to lug should sit like a 39 or 40 mm round watch on most wrists. That is my read, not a brand claim, so try one on if you can.

The edges are gently curved to soften the square, with alternating polished and satin brushed surfaces. Up top is a domed sapphire crystal with anti reflective coating on both sides that follows the case shape, and around back is a screwed sapphire caseback. Water resistance is 100 m, which is plenty for swimming and everyday life. The brand lists the weight at 140 grams on the bracelet.

### The bracelet makes the watch

The integrated stainless steel bracelet is the real headline here. Its faceted links pick up the angles and finishing of the case, so the whole thing reads as one object, which is exactly what you want from this style. It closes on a double folding clasp with twin push buttons, and the brand lists a quick change spring bar system. The lug width is just 12 mm because the bracelet flows straight out of the case, so do not plan on throwing a regular NATO on this one.

### Three dials

You get three references:

* **D015.507.11.041.00**, midnight blue with a textured, grained finish
* **D015.507.11.091.00**, aquamarine with the same textured finish
* **D015.507.11.031.00**, silver with a fine brushed finish

All three get applied baton indices, baton hands with Super LumiNova on the hour and minute hands, and a date window tucked into the dial at 6 o'clock. The aquamarine is the scene stealer for me. Turquoise dials on integrated steel have been the hottest look in watches for years, and a square case makes it feel fresh instead of like another copycat.

### Movement

Inside is the automatic Union Glashütte UNG-07.S1, based on the ETA 2892. It runs at 25,200 vibrations per hour (3.5 Hz) with 21 jewels, a silicon balance spring, stop seconds for precise setting and a power reserve of at least 60 hours. The brand gives the movement as 25.6 mm across and 3.85 mm tall, which goes a long way toward explaining that slim case. The silicon hairspring is the practical win: it shrugs off magnetism far better than a traditional spring, and the brand points to better stability against temperature changes and shocks too. You can see it all through the sapphire caseback.

Expect the real US number to depend on local dealers and taxes.

### My take

I love this move. The integrated bracelet category is crowded with round, octagonal and porthole shapes all chasing the same famous Genta designs, and here comes a German brand with a square that actually has its own history behind it. At 37 mm and under 10 mm, it is sized for real life, not for Instagram, and the 60 hour silicon movement means you can take it off Friday and pick it up Monday still running.

## G-Shock x Sneaker Politics DW6900SP26-6 "Oyster"

**Announced:** November 17, 2026 ([Sneaker Politics](https://sneakerpolitics.com/products/politics-x-casio-g-shock-6900-series-digital-watch-oyster-purple), [G-Central](https://www.g-central.com/louisianas-sneaker-politics-launches-first-g-shock-collaboration-for-its-20th-anniversary-dw6900sp26-6/), [Gizmochina](https://www.gizmochina.com/2026/11/17/casio-g-shock-sneaker-politics-dw6900sp26-6-lauched-specs-price/))

![G-Shock x Sneaker Politics DW6900SP26-6 in translucent purple resin resting among oyster shells, with Politics As Usual printed on the dial]({{ "/assets/img/watches/g-shock-sneaker-politics-dw6900sp26-6.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Sneaker Politics x G-Shock*

Sneaker Politics started in Lafayette, Louisiana in 2006, and for its 20th anniversary it went with its first ever G-Shock collaboration. The theme is pure Gulf Coast: oysters. Not just as food, either. Sneaker Politics says the project nods to how recycled oyster shells are used as a natural building material to help protect Louisiana's coastline from erosion. That is a way better story than most collab color swaps.

### What you get

The base is the classic DW6900, the three eye digital that has been a G-Shock staple for decades. Here it gets a semi transparent purple resin case and band meant to echo the inside of an oyster shell, iridescent blue and purple dial detailing, "Politics As Usual" on the face, a script logo on the strap and a 20th anniversary logo on the caseback. It ships in an oyster net box with a pearl pillow inside, which is a fun touch for collectors who keep the packaging.

Function wise it is standard 6900: shock resistant, 200 m water resistance, mineral glass, stopwatch, timer, alarms, full auto calendar, LED backlight and a flash alert. It runs on a CR2016 battery rated for about five years and measures 53.2 x 50 x 18.7 mm at 67 grams.

### How to get one

**Price:** $180 USD. **Release:** November 21, 2026 at 9:00 AM CST (10:00 AM ET) on sneakerpolitics.com. G-Central reports that online sales run through a random drawing with entries open now, and both G-Central and Gizmochina say the watch is listed on Casio's US site as coming soon. G-Central calls it a limited edition, but no edition size has been published, so I would not count on easy restocks.

### My take

At $180 USD this is a fun, wearable piece of streetwear culture with a real local story behind it, and translucent G-Shocks have a habit of getting snapped up by collectors. If you miss the draw, keep an eye on the secondary market rather than paying silly money on day one.
