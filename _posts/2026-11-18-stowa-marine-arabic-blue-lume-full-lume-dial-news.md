Lume shots are the most addictive thing in watch photography, and Stowa just made a watch where the lume shot is the whole dial. Not the hands, not a few dots on the markers. Every square millimeter of the dial. The German brand launched four new Marine Arabic Blue Lume models today, and they take the clean, classic Marine look and turn it into a little flashlight for your wrist. Here is everything you need to know.

## Stowa Marine Arabic Blue Lume

**Announced:** November 18, 2026 ([Stowa](https://www.stowa.de/marine-classic-40-arabic-blue-lume), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/stowa-marine-classic-and-marine-bronze-vintage-arabic-blue-lume-luminous-dial-introducing-price/))

### Where the idea comes from

Stowa says the inspiration is one of its own observation watches from 1942. Monochrome adds the detail: it was a big 57 mm deck instrument supplied to the German Navy, and instead of the usual white dial it had a dial completely coated in luminous material, with black numerals and markers sitting on top so it could be read in bad light. More than 80 years later, Stowa is putting that layout on the wrist using modern Super-LumiNova BGW9.

### The dial is the whole show

In daylight the dial reads as the cream white you already know from the Marine family, with black Arabic numerals, a railway minute track and heat blued hands. Turn the lights off and the entire surface glows light blue, while the numerals and hands stay dark, so you read the time as black on blue instead of glowing dots on black. It is the reverse of a normal lume dial, and that is exactly what makes it cool. There is no date window, so nothing breaks up that glowing surface.

### Four models, two metals, two sizes

* **Marine Classic 36 Arabic Blue Lume**, polished stainless steel
* **Marine Classic 40 Arabic Blue Lume**, polished stainless steel
* **Marine Bronze Vintage 36 Arabic Blue Lume**, CuSn8 bronze with Stowa's vintage finish
* **Marine Bronze Vintage 40 Arabic Blue Lume**, CuSn8 bronze with Stowa's vintage finish

All four use a sapphire crystal. The steel watches are rated to 10 ATM (100 m) and the bronze watches to 5 ATM (50 m). The 36 mm cases measure 44.6 mm lug to lug with an 18 mm strap width, and the 40 mm cases measure 48.6 mm lug to lug with a 20 mm strap width.

### Movement choices

This is classic Stowa: you build the watch you want. You can pick the automatic Sellita SW200 in Basic or Top grade, or the hand wound Sellita SW210 in Top grade, all running at 28,800 vph. Stowa's product pages do not list a power reserve, and Monochrome gives a range of 40 to 45 hours depending on the movement, so treat that as the ballpark.

The movement also changes the thickness. Stowa lists these heights for the automatic versions, and Monochrome lists the slimmer hand wound figures:

| Model | Automatic (Stowa) | Hand wound (Monochrome) |
|---|---|---|
| Marine Classic 36 | 11.3 mm | 11.1 mm |
| Marine Classic 40 | 10.6 mm | 9.6 mm |
| Marine Bronze Vintage 36 | 10.9 mm | 10.6 mm |
| Marine Bronze Vintage 40 | 10.2 mm | 9.0 mm |

### Straps and bracelets

The steel models can be ordered on brown or black leather, a polished Milanese mesh bracelet, or a steel bracelet mixing brushed and polished surfaces. The bronze models come on brown or black vintage style leather. With 18 mm lugs on the 36 mm and 20 mm lugs on the 40 mm, strap swaps are easy. ### My take

This is my favorite kind of release: simple idea, executed properly, priced like a real person can buy it. A full lume dial is a bit of a novelty in most hands, but on a Marine watch with a 1942 deck instrument behind it, it actually feels earned. The no date dial is the right call, and the choice between polished steel and aged bronze gives you two very different personalities. If I am buying one, it is the Marine Classic 40 with the hand wound SW210, because that 9.6 mm height (per Monochrome) on a 40 mm watch is going to wear beautifully. The fair criticism is that you are getting Sellita movements, not anything in house, and the bronze versions drop to 50 m of water resistance.
