Let me say the quiet part out loud. Most people who fall for the integrated bracelet look will never spend Royal Oak money, and they should not have to. The Tissot PRX Powermatic 80 is the reason that does not matter anymore. Flat tonneau case, bracelet that flows straight out of it like liquid steel, a dial that throws light around the room, and a Swiss automatic movement that runs for more than three days off the wrist. For the money, nothing else in the category gives you this much watch.

This Journal usually lives in the vintage Rolex world. Today we step out of it, because the PRX is the watch I point friends toward when they ask where to start. Every spec and price below is checked against a real source.

## Why the PRX Powermatic 80 hits so hard

Three things make this watch a monster seller.

1. **The look.** Integrated bracelet sports watches are the hottest design language in the hobby, and the PRX nails the proportions instead of copying anyone.
2. **The movement.** An 80 hour power reserve means you can take it off Friday night and it is still ticking Monday morning.
3. **The price.** Tissot lists the 40mm blue dial at $850 on Tissot.com as of November 2026, and the used market sits far below that. More on the numbers shortly.

Put those together and it feels like a cheat code.

## The 1978 backstory that makes it legit

This is not a design invented in a marketing meeting last week. According to the Swatch Group, Tissot's parent company, Tissot created a slim quartz model in 1978 with a wide transverse link bracelet that flowed straight out of a flat tonneau case. Hodinkee notes that the original debuted under the name Seastar. A few years later Tissot registered the PRX name. Per the Swatch Group, "PR" stands for Precise and Robust, and the Roman numeral X points to 10 bar, which is 100 meters of water resistance.

Tissot revived the design with a quartz PRX first, and it was an instant hit. Collectors immediately started begging for a mechanical version, and in 2021 Tissot delivered the Powermatic 80. SJX reported a launch price of US$650 in steel. That gap between launch price and today's retail is worth remembering when we get to the buying advice.

## The Powermatic 80 movement: a workhorse with a three day tank

Inside sits the Powermatic 80.111. Hodinkee and SJX both explain that it is built on ETA's C07.111, which descends from the legendary ETA 2824 family. ETA, like Tissot, belongs to the Swatch Group.

Here is what Swatch did to it:

* **Slower beat.** It runs at 21,600 vibrations per hour, or 3Hz, per Swatch Group, Hodinkee and SJX.
* **Nivachron hairspring.** Tissot says this titanium based alloy balance spring resists magnetic fields far better than a standard spring. Your laptop, phone and magnetic bag closures are less of a threat.
* **Built for automation.** Hodinkee points out that the movement is designed to be assembled with minimal human intervention and is laser regulated at the factory, so there is no traditional fine regulator.

WatchCharts lists the calibre with 23 jewels. Real talk: this is not fine watchmaking and nobody at Tissot pretends it is. It is a smart, tough, modern engine, and through the display back it looks the part. For a daily wearer, I would take three days of reserve over hand finishing at this price every single time.

## 40mm or 35mm: pick your fighter

This is the question I get most, so here is my opinion.

### Go 40mm if

* Your wrist is roughly average or larger and you want presence.
* You like the full sports watch swagger. The 40mm wears compact because the case length is only 39.5mm.

### Go 35mm if

* You have a smaller wrist, or you want something closer to the size of 1970s watches.
* You love a watch that disappears under a sleeve. At 104g it is noticeably lighter than the 138g of the 40mm.

The thickness is identical on both at 10.93mm, so the 35mm wears a touch chunkier relative to its width. Try both on if you can. If you cannot, my rule is simple: when in doubt, the 40mm is the one that turns heads.

## The waffle dial and the colors

The automatic PRX gets a textured waffle pattern dial that Tissot calls out on its 35mm page. Hodinkee notes that the quartz versions do not get this dial, and honestly, it is half the reason to pay up for the Powermatic. In sunlight the grid catches light from every angle.

At the 2021 launch, SJX listed the 40mm in blue, black, and a silver dial with a gold plated bezel. When the 35mm Powermatic arrived, Hodinkee reported blue, black, green, and white mother of pearl. Check Tissot.com for the current lineup before you decide. My pick is still the blue. It is the PRX in its purest form.

## The honest downsides

I love this watch. I am also not going to sell you a fantasy.

* **No screw down crown.** SJX notes the crown is not screwed down and the caseback is press fit. The watch is rated to 100m, but treat it as a swim and splash watch, not a dive tool.
* **No fine regulator.** Because of the factory laser regulation Hodinkee describes, a watchmaker cannot simply nudge the rate the old fashioned way.
* **It is everywhere.** You will see it on other wrists. If exclusivity matters to you, look elsewhere.
* **Polished surfaces scratch.** The polished bevels look incredible and pick up desk marks fast. Embrace it.

## Buying used without getting burned

Popular watches deserve the same discipline I preach for vintage.

1. **Match the reference.** The reference in the listing and paperwork should match the dial color and case size you see.
2. **Ask for a timing video or app reading.** A healthy Powermatic 80 should run steadily after a full wind.
3. **Check the bracelet.** Count the included links. Missing links cost real money to replace, and the integrated design means generic bracelets will not fit.
4. **Look at the quick release pins.** Make sure the bracelet releases and clicks back in cleanly.
5. **Box and papers matter less than condition** on a watch at this price, but a warranty card helps if the watch is still inside Tissot's 2 year warranty, listed on Tissot.com.

## FAQ

### Is the Tissot PRX Powermatic 80 worth it?

At used prices around $480 per Watch Price Tracker's sold data, it is one of the strongest values in Swiss watches. At the $850 MSRP, it is still a great watch, but buying used or at a discount is the smarter move.

### What movement is in the PRX Powermatic 80?

The Powermatic 80.111, based on ETA's C07.111 from the 2824 family, beating at 21,600 vibrations per hour with an 80 hour power reserve and a Nivachron balance spring.

### Is the PRX Powermatic 80 waterproof?

Tissot rates it to 100 meters, or 10 bar. The crown does not screw down, so swimming is fine but it is not a dive watch.

### Does the PRX Powermatic 80 hold its value?

Not the way a steel Rolex does. With retail at $850 and the used market median near $480, expect to lose roughly 40 to 50 percent if you buy new at full retail. That is exactly why used is the play.
