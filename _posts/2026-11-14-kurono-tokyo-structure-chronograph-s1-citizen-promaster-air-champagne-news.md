Hajime Asaoka is going full tool watch. **Kurono Tokyo**, the brand the AHCI independent runs alongside his own namesake pieces, has launched a brand new **Structure series**, and the first watch is the **"Structure" Chronograph S1** at **$3,690 USD** before VAT and duties. There is a catch, and it is a big one: Kurono says there will be **one single online order window, on Thursday, November 19, 2026 at 9:00 AM ET**, and the watch will not be repeated. Every spec below comes from [Kurono Tokyo's own S1 page](https://kuronotokyo.com/pages/chronograph-s1), checked on November 14, 2026, with outside reporting linked where I use it.

## Why the Chronograph S1 is different for Kurono

Until now Kurono watches have leaned classical: small cases, vintage proportions, a little modern twist. Asaoka says the S1 pushes "the purity of product design even further," and he sums the whole project up in two words: **"Functional Elegance."**

The star is the dial. It is built from four layers, and the indices are raised up toward the hands instead of bending the hand tips down to meet them, which is how earlier Kurono pieces handled it according to the brand's Japanese release on [Watch Media Online](https://watch-media-online.com/news/11985/). The payoff is a central chronograph hand that sits with almost zero parallax over the seconds track, so an elapsed time reading is exactly what you see. Asaoka also says the box sapphire crystal was shaped after analyzing refraction, so the minute track at the very edge of the dial stays readable even at an angle. The subdials are pushed out as large as the layout allows, and every counter hand is the same fluorescent orange as the chrono hand.

That 40 mm case is the biggest Kurono has gone in this style, and Asaoka calls it "slightly larger" on purpose to fit a proper tool chronograph. At 13.15 mm with the box crystal and 50.2 mm lug to lug, it should still wear like a real watch and not a hockey puck.

### How to actually buy one

Orders open online at **11:00 PM JST on November 19**, which is **9:00 AM ET** and 6:00 AM PT. Kurono says there will only be one order window, and it has not published a production number, just that the run is limited and will not come back. Deliveries are expected from **mid December 2026**. [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/13/you-get-one-window-to-buy-this-chronograph/) reports a limit of one watch per customer and recommends registering an account before the window opens, which is smart advice for any Kurono drop.

US buyers, read this twice: Kurono's page says US shipments are subject to the current **10% baseline tariff**, collected by the courier on delivery rather than at checkout, and orders cannot be cancelled. So budget roughly $370 on top of the $3,690 before any local charges. If you are in Tokyo or Shanghai, the S1 is on display at Kurono's Aoyama and Shanghai salons through November 19.

## My take on the S1

This is the most interesting Kurono in a while. A guy known for gorgeous hand finished dress pieces sat down and asked how a chronograph should work as a tool, and the answer is all about legibility, not decoration. The parallax fix is the kind of detail nobody puts on a spec sheet, and it is exactly why people line up for Asaoka.

Now the honest part. 3 atm is not much for something called a tool watch, the NE86PWT is a proven Japanese automatic chronograph movement rather than an Asaoka caliber, and with tariffs you are past $4,000 landed in the US. But compare that to what Swiss brands charge for a 40 mm automatic chronograph with this level of design thinking and it still looks like strong value. Kurono drops sell fast, so if the teal or salmon dial got you, have your account ready before 9 AM on Thursday.

## Also new: Citizen Promaster Air NK0040-58P in champagne

On the very affordable end, Citizen has added a **champagne dial Promaster Air Automatic, reference NK0040-58P**, now on sale on [Citizen's US site](https://www.citizenwatch.com/us/en/product/NK0040-58P.html) for **$895 USD**. [Gizmochina](https://www.gizmochina.com/2026/11/14/citizen-promaster-air-nk0040-58p-watch-launched-specs-price/) reports it launched in the US and UK, at £599 in the UK, joining the black and blue versions, with blue having arrived in September.

One small conflict to flag: Citizen's own copy calls the dial **champagne**, while the spec table on the same page lists it as **Ivory**. Expect a pale warm tone either way, with applied indices and red accents. A mechanical pilot's watch in Super Titanium with sapphire, a slide rule bezel and 200 m of water resistance for under $900 is a very easy recommendation, and the 60 hour 8310 means it survives a weekend off the wrist.
