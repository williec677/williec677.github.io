Ming built its name on bold, lume soaked sport and tool watches. So the last thing anyone expected this Monday morning was a slim, black and gold dress watch with no lume at all. That is exactly what the new **Ming 51.01 Halo** is, and the case material is the real headline: crystallised titanium with a mottled grain you can see from across the room. It is 37 mm, 8 mm thick, limited to 150 pieces, and it goes on order tomorrow. I checked the specs and price below against [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/ming-5101-halo), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/ming-51-01-halo-contemporaryt-thin-dress-watch-crystallised-titanium-case-introducing-price/), and [SJX](https://watchesbysjx.com/2026/11/ming-51-01-halo.html), all published November 9, 2026, and the Halo is already featured on [Ming's own site](https://ming.watch/). Two more fresh drops round this out: four new Sơn Mài Silver Leaf dials from French brand Awake, and a tiny run skeleton from English independent Garrick.

## Ming 51.01 Halo: the numbers

Ming numbers its families with four digits. Monochrome explains that 51 marks a brand new family and 01 means this is its first base model. Here is the spec sheet as the three outlets report it:

* Case: crystallised grade 5 titanium with a 5N rose gold tone coating (SJX calls it pink gold PVD)
* Size: 37 mm across, 8 mm thick, 43 mm lug to lug
* Crystal: box shaped sapphire, antireflective on both sides per SJX
* Water resistance: 50 m (SJX)
* Movement: La Joux-Perret for MING caliber 7380.M1, hand wound, 2.7 mm thick, 21,600 vph (3 Hz), 90 hour power reserve from two series coupled barrels
* Functions: hours and minutes only
* Strap: black goat leather by Jean Rousseau Paris on 20 mm curved quick release spring bars, with a crystallised titanium buckle
* Weight: under 40 g including strap and buckle, per SJX
* Edition and price: 150 pieces, about $13,282 USD (CHF 10,900) before tax
* Orders: open November 10, 2026 at 1:00 PM GMT (8:00 AM Eastern) at ming.watch and Ming's retail partners, per Monochrome

## What crystallised titanium actually is

This is not a coating trick or a printed pattern. Hodinkee and Monochrome both describe the same process: the titanium is heated in a vacuum chamber to about 1,200°C and cooled slowly, which grows the metal's natural crystal grains larger. An acid etch then makes those grains visible. SJX puts it well, saying the treatment does not create crystals so much as enlarge and expose the ones already in the metal, much like etching reveals the patterns inside a meteorite slice. Hodinkee compares the look to Damascus steel.

Ming then lays a warm rose gold tone coating over the top, so in photos the case reads almost like bronze or rose gold. Pick it up and the illusion breaks, because it is still titanium and the whole watch weighs under 40 g. SJX admits being wary of coatings on durability grounds, and that is a fair flag for anyone planning daily wear. Like any coating, it can show wear over years in a way solid gold never will.

## The dial: Ming without the glow

The signature Ming trick of engraving the hour markers on the underside of the sapphire crystal is still here. The difference is what fills them. Instead of lume, the grooves carry 5N rose gold colored lacquer, which keeps the black and gold look the same day and night. The dial itself is black lacquer with a polished center and a circular brushed outer ring, and the rose gold tone hands are lume free too. SJX notes the domed crystal throws reflections that hide that dial texture at a glance, so this is a watch you enjoy up close.

## The movement: thin, long running, and dressed up

The 7380 base is the same ultra thin platform used by La Joux-Perret's sister brand Arnold and Son, according to Monochrome. Ming's version gets openworked bridges with anthracite coating, a matte black mainplate, hand finished bevels, and Ming's spoke motif on the ratchet and barrel wheels. At 2.7 mm thick it is what lets the whole watch land at 8 mm, and SJX points out that much of that height is the domed crystal, so the case itself wears even slimmer. Ninety hours of reserve on a hand wound dress watch means you can take it off Friday and it is still running Monday morning.

## My take: is the Halo worth about $13,282 USD (CHF 10,900)?

Here is the honest part. Ming started life as a brand that sold watches for well under a thousand dollars, and SJX calls out that this price "defies the brand's budget roots." You can find the same La Joux-Perret 7380 family in cheaper micro brand watches. What you cannot find elsewhere is this case. A 37 mm crystallised titanium dress watch with Ming's floating crystal markers is a genuinely new look, and only 150 people get one.

Who should jump? Ming collectors, obviously, and anyone who has been waiting for a dress watch that does not look like every other black dial on a leather strap. Who should pass? Anyone who wants lume, real water resistance, or a solid gold case for the money. ## Also new: Awake Sơn Mài Silver Leaf gets four Vignette dials

French brand Awake added four dials to its Sơn Mài Silver Leaf line today, per [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/awake-son-mai-silver-leaf-collection) and [Time and Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/awake-son-mai-silver-leaf-new-dials-in-the-shop/). Java Green, Gentian Blue, and Indian Red join the permanent collection, while Purple Viola is limited to 200 pieces. Awake calls the new look "Vignette," a 1970s style gradient that runs dark at the edge and lighter toward the center.

The dials are made in a lacquer workshop in Hanoi. Hodinkee reports each one takes more than ten hours to build up and polish about twelve layers of pigmented lacquer over silver leaf, which caps output at roughly 100 dials a year for each permanent color. That handwork is the reason to buy one, and it also means no two dials look exactly alike.

The rest of the spec, per Hodinkee:

* Case: redesigned stainless steel, 38 mm across, 44.75 mm lug to lug, 11.5 mm thick, carrying Awake's new handwritten logo
* Water resistance: 100 m
* Movement: La Joux-Perret G101 automatic with a 68 hour reserve and a new tungsten rotor
* Lume: Awake's Chiaroscuro setup, sculpted steel caps over blocks of Super-LumiNova BGW9
* Strap: alcantara or nubuck lined with rubberized calfskin, made by Jean Rousseau and Bouveret in France, with quick release bars and a Fitwell M1 micro adjustable pin buckle
* Price: €2,640 including VAT (Hodinkee estimates about $2,950); deliveries start in late November

Time and Tide adds that its shop and its Discovery Studios in New York, London, and Melbourne are carrying the new dials. The reason I like this release: it is real Métiers d'Art dial work at a price that is still within reach of a serious enthusiast, and the 38 mm case update makes it easier to wear. ## Also new: Garrick S8, a hand made skeleton at five a year

At the other end of the scale, Norfolk based independent Garrick introduced the S8, reported by [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/garrick-s8-watch-skeletonized-39mm-english-indie-watchmaking-introducing-price/) and listed on [Garrick's own site](https://www.garrick.co.uk/). Monochrome says it answers collectors who liked Garrick's 42 mm S3 but wanted something smaller.

The S8 uses a 39 mm, 8 mm thick case in 904L stainless steel with polished or frosted finishes, sapphire front and back, and 100 m of water resistance. The new hand wound Garrick S8 caliber runs at 21,600 vph with about 60 hours of reserve, and it is heavily skeletonised so the movement is the dial. Hours and minutes sit on a chapter ring, with small seconds at 8 o'clock, and the engraved markers are filled with ink by hand under a microscope. One watchmaker finishes and assembles each watch over about eight weeks, and Garrick caps production at five watches a year, built to order with choices for case, finish, wheels, balance, and hands.

Prices start at GBP 59,500 in steel and GBP 73,000 in precious metal, both before VAT, per Monochrome. The S8 debuts at IAMWATCH in Singapore from November 12 to 15, 2026. This is collector territory, and with only five a year, the waiting list is the product.
