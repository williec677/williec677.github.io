Some watches look good in photos. The Albishorn Voisin Aérosport is the kind that makes you stop scrolling. Sébastien Chaulmontet's tiny Swiss brand built its name on "imaginary vintage" chronographs, and now it has gone somewhere completely new: a **38 mm Art Deco dress chronometer** with a dial that sinks into the case in layers. It went on sale on [Albishorn's website](https://albishorn-watches.ch/en/collections/earth-collection/voisin-aerosport-classic) on November 11, and I missed it in my first sweep, so here it is properly. Every spec below is checked against Albishorn's own product page on November 15, 2026, with [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/introducing-albishorn-voisin-aerosport) and [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/albishorn-voisin-aerosport-retro-futuristic-chronometer-dress-watch-sector-dial-review-price/) filling in the rest. Then we finish with a fun regional Seiko.

## Albishorn Voisin Aérosport: a watch for a man who never ordered one

The story is the hook. Gabriel Voisin was one of Europe's early aircraft builders, and after the First World War he turned to cars under the Avions Voisin name. One of his wildest designs was the 1930s **Voisin C28 Aérosport**. Albishorn's idea is simple and clever: what watch would Voisin have commissioned for himself back then? Not a tribute with a car printed on the dial, but something that just belongs in his world.

Who is behind it matters too. Hodinkee notes that Chaulmontet's day job is **Director of Innovation and Marketing at Sellita**, and until now Albishorn has been all chronographs, built around a modified Valjoux 7750: Monochrome lists the Maxigraph, Type 10, Thundergraph and Marinagraph. Hodinkee calls this the brand's second model, and it is the first time Albishorn has stepped away from tool watches.

### That dial

This is where the Voisin earns its price. Albishorn builds the dial from **three stacked elements**:

* a lower layer with a **180 degree sector running seconds** display at 4 o'clock, snailed for texture
* a **silver opaline** main dial on the Classic, or a grained light blue base on the Ice Blue
* a raised, **satin brushed hour and minute chapter ring** with polished edges and blue printing

Hodinkee explains the party trick: the seconds hand is **double ended**, with half of it always hidden like a moonphase disc, so the sector reads 0 to 30 and then 31 to 60. The subdial sits deep below the main dial with the hand floating above it. Blued steel hands finish the look. It reads clean at a glance and reveals more the closer you look, which is exactly how good Art Deco should work.

### Case and wearability

At **38 mm across, 9.6 mm thick including the sapphire (8.9 mm without), 45.2 mm lug to lug and 20 mm between the lugs**, this is proper modern dress watch sizing that should disappear under a cuff. The steel case is far from plain, though. Monochrome describes a wide two level bezel, circular grained on top and mirror polished on the inclined lower step, plus stepped lugs and a grained ring set into the caseband. Like every Albishorn so far, the **crown sits at 10 o'clock**. A box sapphire covers the dial, there is a sapphire caseback, and water resistance is **50 m**.

### The movement

Albishorn did not drop in an off the shelf calibre. The **ALB04 M** is a proprietary hand wound movement based on a **Manufacture AMT** ébauche, engineered so the hours and minutes and the sunken seconds run on separate planes. It is **COSC certified**, which Hodinkee points out is a real challenge with a display like this. Through the back you get Côtes de Genève, double snailed winding wheels, polished screw heads and gold toned engravings.

### My take

This is the watch that could take Albishorn from collector favorite to a name everybody knows. For about $4,115 USD you get an in house designed movement with chronometer certification, a genuinely original dial that nobody else is doing, and finishing that Hodinkee says punches above its price. Hodinkee is also clear that the 99 piece cap reflects how small the brand is rather than an attempt to manufacture hype. Small batches still sell out, though. If the Ice Blue speaks to you, I would not sit on it. The one thing to think about is that 10 o'clock crown, which is comfortable on the wrist but a little fiddly for daily hand winding.

## Seiko 5 Sports HDB019K1 Alishan: Taiwan gets its own SKX

On the other end of the price ladder, Seiko keeps rolling out regional limited editions. Seiko Taiwan has opened preorders for the **5 Sports HDB019K1 "Alishan,"** inspired by the Alishan mountain range in central Taiwan, according to [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Seiko-s-new-limited-edition-5-Sports-takes-inspiration-from-Taiwan-s-Alishan-mountains.1447238.0.html) and [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/seiko-5-sports-hdb019k1-alishan-taiwan/).

It uses the familiar SKX style **42.5 mm** steel case with a **forest green dial**, warm gold accents and a **black and sand brown bezel** meant to echo the region's wooded landscape. Inside is Seiko's **4R36** automatic with day and date at 3 o'clock, shown through a screw in exhibition caseback, with a steel bracelet and **100 m** of water resistance. The extras are a nice touch: an outdoor travel bag, an illustrated carabiner and a presentation box with artwork by Taiwanese illustrator Croter.

Price is **about $395 USD (NT$13,000)**, converted at about NT$32.9 to the dollar, with deliveries expected in early December. It is sold only in Taiwan through Seiko boutiques and the Seiko Taiwan online store, and none of the sources I checked list how many will be made.

My take: earthy colors on an SKX shape are a winning formula, and this is one of the better looking regional editions Seiko has done. US collectors will have to chase one on the secondary market, so expect a premium. If you just want the platform, the regular 5 Sports is easy to find, and the Strap Finder has a page for the [Seiko 5 Sports SRPD](https://mrtictoctalk.com/strap-finder/seiko-5-sports-srpd/).
