Here is a question that has started more friendly arguments at watch meetups than almost any other: Jubilee or Oyster? Same case, same dial, same movement, and somehow the bracelet completely changes the personality of a Rolex. One looks dressy and vintage. The other looks sporty and indestructible.

The good news is there is no wrong answer. The better news is that once you understand how the two are built, the right answer for you gets very clear. Let us get into it.

## A tale of two bracelets

### The Oyster: three links, late 1930s

According to [Rolex](https://www.rolex.com/watchmaking/features/bracelets/oyster), the Oyster bracelet was developed in the late 1930s. Its signature is the three piece link: three broad links across the bracelet. It is the bracelet you see on the current Submariner, Sea Dweller, and Explorer.

The look is flat, broad, and purposeful. It reads as a tool. On a sports watch, it simply looks right.

### The Jubilee: five links, 1945

The Jubilee came later and with a specific mission. [Rolex says](https://www.rolex.com/watchmaking/features/bracelets/jubilee-bracelet) it was specifically created for the Datejust, launched in 1945 to celebrate 40 years of craftsmanship, and that it took years of research to perfect. Its pattern is five links across: two larger satin finished outer links flanking three smaller polished center links.

All those smaller links do two things. They catch the light, so the Jubilee has more sparkle. And they let the bracelet drape around the wrist more fluidly. Rolex itself calls the Jubilee "supple and comfortable." Today it is no longer just a Datejust bracelet. Rolex notes it now equips other watches in the collection too.

| Introduced | Late 1930s | 1945, with the Datejust |
| Links across | Three | Five |
| Link design | Three broad links | Two satin outer links, three polished center links |
| Look | Sporty, tool watch | Dressy, vintage |
| Datejust 41 126300 blue dial US price | about $8,950 USD | about $9,150 USD |

Those prices come from rolex.com, checked November 13, 2026: the [Datejust 41 126300 on Oyster](https://www.rolex.com/en-us/watches/datejust/m126300-0001) and the [same watch on Jubilee](https://www.rolex.com/en-us/watches/datejust/m126300-0002). Same 41mm Oystersteel case, same bright blue dial, same calibre 3235 with approximately 70 hours of power reserve. The only difference is the bracelet, and it costs about $200 USD more for the Jubilee.

## Comfort: how they wear

This is where personal preference really takes over, but the construction tells you what to expect.

* **The Jubilee** has more, smaller links, so it conforms to the wrist with very few gaps. It feels light and almost liquid. If you have a flatter or smaller wrist, the Jubilee often sits beautifully.
* **The Oyster** has fewer, bigger links, so it feels more solid and planted. It holds its shape and sits like a single structure on the wrist. If you like to feel your watch, the Oyster delivers.

My advice: try both on the same day. You will know within five minutes.

## Clasps: where the details live

The bracelet is only half of the story. The clasp matters just as much for daily comfort, and Rolex pairs different clasps with different models.

* **Oysterclasp with Easylink.** On the Datejust 41 126300, both the Oyster and Jubilee versions use a folding Oysterclasp with the Easylink 5mm comfort extension link, according to Rolex's spec sheets. Easylink lets you add about 5mm of length when your wrist swells on a hot day.
* **Oysterlock with Easylink.** The Explorer 36 124270 and the GMT Master II 126710GRNR on Oyster use an Oysterlock safety clasp, which Rolex says prevents accidental opening, plus Easylink.
* **Oysterlock with Glidelock.** On the Submariner and Sea Dweller, Rolex's newsroom says the Glidelock system has 10 notches of about 2mm each, letting you adjust the bracelet by about 20mm without tools, enough to wear it over a diving suit up to 3mm thick.

So when you compare bracelets across models, compare clasps too. A Jubilee on one watch and a Jubilee on another may not feel identical at the wrist.

## Looks: which one suits which watch

Here is my rule of thumb.

* **Datejust:** Jubilee is the classic pairing. Rolex designed it for this watch, and it has worn it since 1945. The Oyster gives the Datejust a cleaner, more modern look, which is great if you want a daily watch that leans casual.
* **GMT Master II:** if you find one on either bracelet, both work. The Jubilee softens the sporty look, and the Oyster makes it a pure travel tool. If you are setting one up for a trip, my [guide to setting a GMT Master II](/how-to-set-rolex-gmt-master-ii/) walks through every step.
* **Submariner and Sea Dweller:** Oyster all day. The Glidelock clasp is one of the best things about these watches.

## Vintage bracelets: what to watch for

On vintage Rolex, both bracelets age, and the term you will hear is stretch. Over decades, the pins and link holes wear, and a bracelet develops gaps and sag. Because a Jubilee has more links and more pivot points, collectors often find that older Jubilees show this wear more visibly. A stretched bracelet is not a deal breaker, but it is a negotiating point, and a period correct bracelet in good condition adds real value to a vintage watch.

If you are shopping vintage, compare comps for the watch with and without its original bracelet. My guide to [finding real sold prices](/how-to-find-real-sold-prices-for-vintage-watches/) shows how to pull those numbers, and my [GMT Master II 16710 buyer's guide](/rolex-gmt-master-ii-16710-buyers-guide/) covers bracelets on that reference.

## Can you swap one for the other?

On many modern Rolex models, Rolex offers the same reference on both bracelets, like the Datejust 41 126300. Whether a specific bracelet fits a specific case depends on the end links and the lug width, so confirm the part with an authorized dealer or a Rolex service center before you buy a loose bracelet. The Datejust 41 has 21mm lugs, and my [Datejust 41 Strap Finder page](/strap-finder/rolex-datejust-41/) has ideas if you want to try a strap instead.

## My verdict

If the watch is a Datejust and you want the full classic Rolex experience, get the Jubilee. It was literally made for it, and the extra $200 USD at list is a small premium for such a visible change. If you want your watch to feel like a tool, or you are buying a dive watch, go Oyster and do not look back.
