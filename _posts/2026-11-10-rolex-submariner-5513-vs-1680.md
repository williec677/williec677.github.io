If you want a vintage Submariner you can actually wear, the conversation usually ends with two references: the 5513 and the 1680. Both sit in a 40mm case with an acrylic crystal and tritium lume, and both are rated to 200 meters. From across a room they look like siblings. Up close they are built for different people.

## The short version

| | 5513 | 1680 |
|---|---|---|
| Production (approx.) | 1962 to 1989 | late 1960s to about 1979 |
| Date | No | Yes, with cyclops |
| Movement | Cal 1530, later 1520 | Cal 1575 |
| Chronometer | No | Yes |
| Dial text | Two lines under the logo | Four lines including the chronometer text |
| Crystal | Acrylic | Acrylic with cyclops |

Sources for the dates and calibers: [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/a35195715/rolex-submariner/), [Bob's Watches](https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-submariner-1680), and the [Bob's Watches 1680 guide](https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-blog/watch-review/appeal-reference-submariner-1680.html).

## The 5513: the long running tool watch

The 5513 is the longest produced vintage Submariner, with a run of roughly 27 years. Rolex never had it certified as a chronometer, so the dial carries only two lines of text under the logo. Early watches used calibre 1530. Later ones used the 1520, a simpler movement in the same family with fewer adjustments ([Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/a35195715/rolex-submariner/)).

That long run is the 5513's biggest strength and its biggest trap. There are many dial variations, and each has its own following and its own fakes:

* **Gilt dials** from the early 1960s, with gold colored printing on a glossy black base.
* **Matte dials** with white printing from the late 1960s through the 1970s, and variants collectors nickname by small printing differences.
* **Glossy dials with white gold surrounds** around the markers from the 1980s, a look that previews the modern Submariner.

The no date dial is the reason people love this watch. The symmetry is perfect, and there is no cyclops interrupting the crystal. If you want the purest Submariner design, this is it.

## The 1680: the first Submariner Date

The 1680 introduced the date to the Submariner. Its calibre 1575 adds a date and runs at 19,800 vibrations per hour, and the dial says Superlative Chronometer Officially Certified. Expect to see "1570" stamped on the movement bridge. That is correct, because the 1575 is a 1570 with a date module, and it trips up new buyers every week ([Bob's Watches](https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-submariner-1680)).

The headline variant is the **red Sub**, where the word SUBMARINER is printed in red. Red text dials were produced from roughly 1969 to the mid 1970s, after which Rolex switched to all white printing. Collectors divide red dials into several "marks" by tiny printing details. The differences are real and documented, but they are also exactly what fakers study, so a red dial needs more scrutiny than any other part of the watch.

The white text 1680 is the sleeper. It has the same case, movement, and date as a red Sub without the red premium. If you want a vintage Sub Date to wear, start there.

## Things that matter on both

**Case condition.** Both references have drilled lugs, which makes strap changes easy and also shows polishing clearly. Sharp lug edges and a visible bevel on the top of the lugs are good signs. Rounded, soft lugs mean polishing. Lots of polishing lowers value more than most buyers expect.

**Bezel insert.** Aluminum inserts fade and chip. A faded "ghost" insert can add appeal, but inserts are easy to swap. Value the watch without assuming the insert is original.

**Lume.** Tritium should have aged into a consistent color across the dial and hands. A mismatch suggests replaced hands, which is common after service and worth a price adjustment.

**Service parts.** Many of these watches had their dials, hands, and crystals replaced at Rolex service. Genuine service parts are not fakes, but they move the watch from collector grade to wearer grade. We will cover that in a dedicated guide.

**Bracelets.** Folded link bracelets are period correct and lighter than the modern solid ones. Stretch is normal. Excessive stretch is a negotiating point.

## How they wear

On the wrist the two are closer than the spec sheet suggests. Same case, same lug width, same bezel action. The 1680's cyclops adds a small bump to the crystal profile, and the date window breaks the dial's symmetry at 3. Some people find that practical. Others find it busy. Try both on if you can, because this is a decision you will feel every time you look down.

The acrylic crystal on both is part of the charm. It is warm, slightly domed, and polishes out with a little paste. It also scratches easily, so expect light marks on any watch that has been worn.

## Questions to ask any seller

* When was it last serviced, and by whom? Do you have the invoice?
* Has Rolex or anyone else replaced the dial, hands, or bezel insert?
* Are the bracelet and clasp original to the watch, and what do the clasp codes say?
* Can I see the serial and reference between the lugs, and the movement?
* What is your return policy if a watchmaker finds a problem?

A seller who answers quickly and completely is worth paying a little more to.

## So which one?

Pick the **5513** if you want the cleaner dial, prefer a watch that feels like a tool, or want the widest choice of eras to match your budget. A later 1980s 5513 with a glossy dial is the most practical daily wearer of the bunch.

Pick the **1680** if you use the date, want a chronometer movement, or love the red Sub story. Buy a white dial if you want to wear it hard. Buy a red dial only with expert help and a solid return policy.

## Quick takeaways

* 5513: no date, non chronometer, cal 1530 then 1520, made for nearly three decades.
* 1680: first Sub Date, chronometer cal 1575, "1570" on the bridge is correct.
* Red text 1680 dials are the most faked part of this comparison. Get expert eyes on one.
* Check lug sharpness, lume consistency, and service parts on both.
* Price from dated sold comps that match dial variant and condition.
