Friday lunch break just got loud. A Los Angeles micro brand and a London watch magazine teamed up on a blood red stone dial that costs less than a weekend away, a Swiss brand built a pilot's watch around a 45 year old arcade shooter, and Citizen dropped two titanium Attesas with a bezel that shimmers like the inside of a seashell. Three very different watches, one common thread: nobody played it safe this week.

## Nodus x Oracle Time Sector II Sport Red Jasper

**Released:** November 20, 2026 ([Oracle Time](https://oracleoftime.com/nodus-x-oracle-time-sector-ii-sport-red-jasper/), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/nodus-x-oracle-time-sector-ii-sport-red-jasper-accessible-automatic-tool-watch-stone-dial-introducing-price/), [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/introducing-the-nodus-x-oracle-time-sector-ii-sport-red-jasper/))

![Nodus x Oracle Time Sector II Sport Red Jasper on a wrist with a red London bus blurred in the background]({{ "/assets/img/watches/nodus-oracle-time-sector-ii-sport-red-jasper.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Oracle Time (Nodus x Oracle Time Sector II Sport Red Jasper)*

Nodus was founded in 2017 in Los Angeles and has quietly become one of the most respected names in affordable tool watches. Oracle Time is the British watch publication behind the Hands On Horology show, and its earlier collabs include the Christopher Ward C65 Dune Shoreline and the Ball Engineer II Dazzle. Oracle Time says the idea grew out of the 15 piece Sector II Sport Tiger's Eye that Nodus made for its 2025 show. This time both sides shaped the design together, and the stone choice is the whole story.

Oracle Time ruled out tiger's eye, malachite, turquoise, onyx and lapis because everybody is doing them. They landed on red jasper, an iron rich quartz and chalcedony stone, picked to echo London's Routemaster buses, post boxes and phone booths. The stone is untreated and undyed, so no two dials are identical.

What the sources confirm:

* **Dial:** natural red jasper with gilt applied markers and Arabic numerals at 3, 6 and 9, plus hands and markers filled with Swiss Super-LumiNova BGW9 Grade A that glows blue. There is no Oracle Time logo on the dial at all.
* **Case:** 38 mm stainless steel, 11.7 mm thick, 47 mm lug to lug, mostly brushed with polished bevels, screw down hobnail crown and 100 m of water resistance. Fratello notes antireflective coating on the underside of the sapphire crystal.
* **Caseback:** solid and screwed, engraved with topographic relief lines around the OT monogram, plus a collaboration inscription and the individual limited edition number.
* **Movement:** Seiko group NH38 automatic, no date, 3 Hz with about 41 hours of power reserve. Final assembly and regulation happen in Los Angeles, per Monochrome.
* **Bracelet:** tapered three link steel with quick release spring bars and Nodus's NodeX clasp, which Fratello says gives up to 10 mm of tool free micro adjustment.

**Price and availability:** **$650 USD** (Oracle Time also lists £490), limited to **100 pieces**. Per Monochrome, general sale opened November 20 at 17:00 GMT, which is 12 PM ET, through noduswatches.com and oracleoftime.com, after a 24 hour early window for OT Club members. When we checked, the regular Sector II Sport lineup on Nodus's site ran $525 to $600 USD, so the collab carries a modest premium for the limited stone.

**My take:** this is exactly how a collab should be done. Same proven 38 mm case, same honest NH38, and all the energy goes into one bold idea. Red stone dials usually live on watches costing ten times this much. With only 100 made and a real OT Club head start, I would not sleep on it. If stone dials are your thing, revisit [AP's Code 11.59 obsidian and onyx dials]({% post_url 2026-11-04-audemars-piguet-code-11-59-stone-dials-farer-stratton-ii-news %}) for the other end of the price ladder.

## Alpina Startimer Pilot Solarmetre Galaga

**Released:** November 19, 2026 ([Alpina](https://alpinawatches.com/pages/startimer-pilot-solarmetre-galaga), [Watchonista](https://www.watchonista.com/editorial/novelties/insert-coin-alpinas-startimer-pilot-solarmetre-galaga-puts-a-1981-arcade-classic-on-your-wrist/), [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/alpina-startimer-pilot-solarmetre-galaga/))

![Alpina Startimer Pilot Solarmetre Galaga on a blue NATO strap displayed next to its miniature Galaga arcade cabinet box]({{ "/assets/img/watches/alpina-startimer-pilot-solarmetre-galaga.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Alpina (ref. AL-140NGA3S26 with its arcade cabinet box)*

Insert coin. Alpina partnered with Bandai Namco to celebrate Galaga, the 1981 arcade shooter, and went all in on the bit. The base is the brand's solar Startimer Pilot, and the details are pure arcade:

* **Dial:** blue with tone on tone Arabic numerals. The Boss Galaga sits at 12 in full color, firing its tractor beam toward the fighter ship, while the other invaders are laser engraved under the sapphire crystal so they seem to hover over the dial.
* **Aperture at 3:** instead of a date, a space window shows a different Galaga character in full color against a black background, rotating day after day according to Alpina. Gear Patrol also describes it as daily, while Watchonista says monthly, so we are going with the brand.
* **Seconds hand:** white with a pixel fighter ship counterweight.
* **Caseback:** solid, recreating the 100 yen coin you needed to play in Japan, ringed by the original Galaga logo and the engraving "ONE OF 1981".
* **Case and movement:** 38 mm sandblasted steel, 100 m water resistance, AL-140 solar quartz developed with La Joux-Perret, running up to 10 months in total darkness on a full charge.
* **Extras:** a removable blue rubber bumper with a red crown guard, a navy NATO style strap with "Insert Coin" and "Game Over" keepers plus a third with a famous cheat code, and a box shaped like a miniature 1981 arcade cabinet. Alpina says the first 30 orders also get a starship collector's case.

**Price and availability:** **CHF 1,295**, about **$1,543 USD**, on Alpina's Swiss online store, limited to **1,981 pieces**. Gear Patrol quotes $1,573, but we could not find the watch on Alpina's US store, so we are not treating that as an official US price. The UK price is £1,095.

**My take:** is it serious? Not even a little. Is it fun? Absolutely, and the solar movement means you can toss it in a drawer and it will still be ready to play. The coin caseback is my favorite touch. For another solar quartz special edition from this month, see the [TAG Heuer Formula 1 Solargraph x Red Bull]({% post_url 2026-11-15-tag-heuer-formula-1-solargraph-red-bull-vulcain-skindiver-mark-ii-citizen-news %}).

## Citizen Attesa Hazy White and Hazy Black

**Announced:** November 19, 2026 ([Citizen](https://www.citizenwatch-global.com/news/2026/20261119/index.html), [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/citizen-attesa-hazy-white-and-hazy-black/), [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Citizen-debuts-striking-limited-edition-Attesa-chronographs-with-mother-of-pearl-dials-and-sapphire-bezels.1454246.0.html))

![Citizen Attesa Hazy Black AT8404-52E in black DLC titanium and Hazy White AT8400-53L in silver titanium against a misty city skyline]({{ "/assets/img/watches/citizen-attesa-hazy-white-hazy-black.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Citizen (left: Hazy Black AT8404-52E, right: Hazy White AT8400-53L)*

Citizen's Attesa line is its techy titanium flagship, and fresh off the [Death Star II moonphase]({% post_url 2026-11-14-citizen-attesa-death-star-ii-by1004-news %}) it is back with something prettier. The trick on both Hazy models is a full sapphire bezel: the crystal runs all the way to the edge of the case, and a ring of mother of pearl sits underneath, throwing shifting color as the light moves. The world time cities are printed on that sapphire.

* **Hazy White, AT8400-53L:** light blue gradient dial inspired by morning mist, white mother of pearl ring, Super Titanium with a Duratect Platinum treatment. **$1,790 USD**, limited to **2,400 pieces**.
* **Hazy Black, AT8404-52E:** black dial inspired by night fog, black mother of pearl ring, Super Titanium with Duratect DLC. **$1,900 USD**, limited to **2,600 pieces**.
* **Shared specs:** mother of pearl subdials, a molded pearl effect pattern on the main dial, sapphire crystal with antireflective coating and 100 m of water resistance. Citizen's global release lists the case at 43.5 mm by 13.3 mm as a design specification, while Citizen Canada's product page says 43.1 mm, so expect something in that range.
* **Movement:** Eco-Drive Caliber H800 with radio sync in four regions (Japan, China, USA and Europe), Direct Flight time setting across 26 time zones, a 1/20 second chronograph up to 60 minutes, a perpetual calendar, a power reserve indicator, accuracy of ±15 seconds per month without signal and up to 10 months of running on a full charge.

**Price and availability:** **$1,790 USD** (Hazy White) and **$1,900 USD** (Hazy Black), per Citizen's global release, with launch scheduled for December 2026. Notebookcheck reports Japan sales start December 3. The US product pages were not live when we checked.

**My take:** Attesa specs are already absurd for the money. Radio sync, perpetual calendar and a 1/20 second chrono in titanium for under $2,000. The pearl under sapphire bezel finally gives the line some romance. The Hazy White is the one I would wear daily; the Black is the night out.
