Every Rolex conversation I have on Instagram eventually lands on the same question: "What year is it?" The answer lives in two engravings, and most buyers only half read them. Learn to read both properly and you will catch a surprising number of problems before a watchmaker ever opens a case.

## Where the numbers are

On a vintage or neo vintage Rolex, take the bracelet off (or have the seller do it on video) and look between the lugs.

* **At 12 o'clock:** the reference number, which tells you the model.
* **At 6 o'clock:** the serial number, which identifies that individual case and gives you an approximate production year.

Rolex later moved the serial. According to [Bob's Watches](https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-serial-numbers), the company began also engraving the serial on the rehaut (the inner ring between dial and crystal) in 2005, and from 2008 it was engraved only on the rehaut. If a listing for a 1990s watch shows a serial on the rehaut, something is off. If a listing for a 2015 watch shows a serial between the lugs at 6, something is very off.

## Dating a watch by serial

From the 1920s until 1987, Rolex serials were numeric and climbed over time. After hitting the high millions, Rolex switched to a letter prefix followed by six digits. Approximate letter years, from the [Bob's Watches chart](https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-serial-numbers):

| Prefix | Approximate year |
|---|---|
| R | 1987 to 1988 |
| L | 1989 |
| E | 1990 |
| X, N | 1991 |
| C | 1992 |
| S | 1993 to 1994 |
| W | 1995 |
| T | 1996 |
| U | 1997 to 1998 |
| A | 1999 |
| P, K | 2000 to 2001 |
| Y | 2001 to 2002 |
| F | 2003 to 2005 |
| D | 2005 to 2006 |
| Z | 2006 to 2007 |
| M | 2007 to 2008 |
| V | 2008 to 2009 |
| G | 2010 |

From about 2011, serials became random alphanumeric codes, so the serial no longer dates the watch ([Rolexopedia](https://rolexopedia.net/wiki/Reference:Serial-numbers)). For anything newer than that, the warranty card is your dating document.

Three cautions keep you honest here:

1. **These charts are compiled by collectors and dealers.** There is no public official Rolex list. Treat every year as plus or minus one.
2. **The serial dates the case, not the sale.** A watch could sit at a dealer for a year or two. That is why a 1999 serial with 2001 papers is normal.
3. **Serial and dial should agree.** A tritium dial on a 2004 case, or a Super LumiNova dial on a 1992 case, means a part was changed. Sometimes Rolex did it at service. Sometimes someone else did. Either way it changes value.

## Decoding the reference number

Rolex references carry structure. On five and six digit references, the opening digits identify the model family, and the final digits usually tell you the bezel and the metal. Bob's Watches uses the Datejust 16234 as the example: 162 is the Datejust family, 3 is a fluted bezel, and 4 is steel with an 18k white gold bezel.

The last digit is the one I use most. A few that hold up across modern references:

| Last digit | Typical meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Stainless steel | GMT Master II 16710, Sea Dweller 126600 |
| 3 | Steel and yellow gold (Rolesor) | Submariner 126613 |
| 4 | Steel with white gold bezel | Datejust 16234 |
| 5 | Everose gold | Yacht Master 37 268655 |
| 8 | 18k yellow gold | Day Date 18238 |
| 9 | 18k white gold | Submariner 126619 |

The bezel digit, second from the end, matters most on the Datejust and Day Date families: 0 for smooth, 1 or 2 for engine turned, 3 for fluted. Four digit vintage references, such as the 1601 or 5513, do not follow this scheme cleanly, so learn those as whole numbers.

Six digit references arrived in the 2000s, and Rolex added letter suffixes for bezel and dial colors, such as LN for a black bezel. Those suffixes are not engraved between the lugs. They are retail descriptors.

## Clasp codes: the cheap cross check

Many Rolex clasps carry a date code inside the hinge. The code is one or two letters for the year plus a number for the month. In the [Bob's Watches clasp table](https://www.bobswatches.com/rolex-serial-numbers), for instance, N means 1989 and U means 1998. Look up the letter and compare it to the serial. An S stamped with the code indicates a service replacement bracelet.

A clasp a year or two newer than the case is normal, because watches were assembled and sold over time. A clasp ten years newer means the bracelet was replaced. Not a problem on its own, but it tells you to look harder at everything else.

## Casebacks on older watches

Many vintage Rolex casebacks carry the reference inside and a quarter and year stamp, such as a Roman numeral quarter followed by a two digit year. That stamp dates the caseback, which should land close to the serial year. A caseback stamped a decade away from the serial is a swapped caseback. Not the end of the world, but a franken clue.

## What the numbers cannot tell you

This is where buyers get burned. Correct numbers prove only that the case has correct numbers.

* **Cases get re engraved and fake cases get real looking numbers.** Rolex engravings are fine, deep, and crisp with a slight sparkle from the cutting. Shallow, sandy, or acid etched looking numbers are a warning.
* **Numbers say nothing about the movement, dial, or hands.** A correct case can hold a replica movement.
* **Polishing can soften the lug engravings.** Heavily blurred numbers on a watch that is "unpolished" is a contradiction.

The final check is always the same: a qualified watchmaker opens the case. ## A buyer's sequence that works

1. Get photos of the reference at 12 and the serial at 6 (or rehaut for later watches).
2. Date the serial, then confirm dial text, lume, and features fit that era.
3. Check the clasp code against the serial.
4. Ask whether the papers match the serial, and compare the digits yourself.
5. Make the sale conditional on a watchmaker opening the case.

## Quick takeaways

* Reference at 12 identifies the model. Serial at 6 (or rehaut from 2005) dates the case.
* Letter serials ran from 1987 to 2010. Random serials since then cannot be dated.
* The last reference digit usually tells you the metal. The one before it tells you the bezel on Datejust style models.
* Serial, dial era, clasp code, and caseback stamp should tell one story.
* Matching numbers never replace opening the case.
