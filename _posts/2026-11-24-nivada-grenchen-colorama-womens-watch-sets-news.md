Every so often a brand digs into its archive and pulls out something nobody saw coming. Nivada Grenchen, the revived Swiss name best known to enthusiasts for the Antarctic and the Chronomaster, just brought back the Colorama: a tiny 1960s jewelry watch that changes its face in seconds and can even hang around your neck. It is the first piece of the brand's new women's line, it lands for Nivada's 100th anniversary, and it is priced like a gift you can actually buy this season.

The sets went live on Nivada Grenchen's store on November 19, WatchTime introduced the collection on November 18, and Worn & Wound put it back in the spotlight today. Here is everything that is confirmed.

### The original idea from 1965

Nivada first presented the Colorama in 1965 as "the watch with many faces." The concept, protected by a Swiss patent at the time according to the brand, was simple and kind of brilliant: one small, good quality ladies' watch, a handful of colored straps and clip on bezels, so the same watch could match an outfit, an occasion or a mood. Some of the vintage sets even turned the watch into a pendant. They came in jewelry box sets with names like Colorama VI and VII, Maharani and Fiesta.

The modern brand relaunched in 2020, and WatchTime reports the Colorama took more than a year of development, with most of the work going into proportions, colors and the click mechanism that holds the bezels in place. That last part is the whole product, so it is good to hear it got the attention.

### The watch at the center of every set

All four sets share the exact same watch. Per Nivada Grenchen's product pages:

* **Case:** 17 mm 316L stainless steel with gold tone IP plating, just 6.75 mm thick, flat crown, gold tone snap on caseback
* **Dial:** flat white enamel with gold tone applied indexes and two hands
* **Crystal:** flat sapphire
* **Movement:** Ronda 1042 Swiss quartz
* **Water resistance:** 5 ATM
* **Straps:** leather, 8 mm wide tapering to 5 mm, quick to swap
* **Made in:** Switzerland (Swiss Made)
* **Warranty:** 2 years, per the brand's store

Yes, 17 mm is small. That is the point. This is a jewelry watch in the classic sense, sized like the cocktail watches of the 1960s rather than a modern 34 or 36 mm "ladies' size" sport watch. Quartz makes sense here too: a watch you rotate in and out of the jewelry box should be ready to wear the moment you pick it up.

### Two looks: Golden Hour and Color Pop

The bezels are where the Colorama gets its personality, and they click off and on in seconds.

* **Golden Hour:** three polished gold tone bezels with three different silhouettes, called Sunburst, Heart and Faceted. Think evening, dinner, dressier outfits.
* **Color Pop:** three enamel bezels in orange, green and white, each with a raised floral pattern taken from the vintage pieces. Think daytime and bright colors.

Two details worth getting right, because the launch coverage reads a little differently from the brand's own pages:

1. **The heart necklace is in every set.** The Time Capsule is a gold tone steel heart with a hammered texture and a 1.5 mm prong set diamond. Nivada packs it in its own pouch and jewelry box, separate from the watch set, so it can be given together with the watch or saved for another occasion.
2. **Only the Collector sets turn the watch into a necklace.** The brand's product page is explicit: the standard sets are designed to be worn on the wrist, and the round 35 mm Golden Sun pendant, the piece that actually holds the Colorama at the neck, is reserved for the Collector sets.

**Price and availability:** available now on Nivada Grenchen's store at **$695 USD** for the Golden Hour Set or Color Pop Set and **$895 USD** for either Collector Set. US orders ship from the brand's US warehouse, with free tracked shipping from $200 USD and no extra charges on delivery, per the product page. The Colorama is not listed as a limited edition.

## My take: an enthusiast idea in a jewelry box

Here is what makes this more interesting than a typical "for her" launch. Customization is one of the oldest joys in this hobby. We swap straps, we hunt for the right bracelet, we argue about bezel inserts. The Colorama takes that exact instinct and builds a whole product around it, using a real piece of the brand's history instead of a marketing story. Worn & Wound's Zach Kazan made the same point today, calling it "the rare watch aimed squarely at women that is also pretty clearly an enthusiast focused product."

I do have one gripe. You cannot mix the two worlds. If you want one gold bezel and one enamel bezel, there is no set for that right now, so you have to pick a lane. My pick is the Color Pop Collector Set at $895 USD: the enamel bezels carry a raised floral pattern taken straight from the vintage pieces, and the Golden Sun pendant brings back the necklace trick from the original 1960s sets. If the budget stops at $695 USD, the standard Golden Hour Set is the easiest gift to get right.

For perspective on value, the brand's own store lists the 35 mm Antarctic Spider at $945 USD and its quartz F77 SST Chrono Mecaquartz from $540 USD, so the Colorama sits right in Nivada's everyday price zone while adding a diamond necklace and a box full of bezels.
