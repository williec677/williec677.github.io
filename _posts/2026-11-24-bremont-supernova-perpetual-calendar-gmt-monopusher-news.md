A perpetual calendar is usually a gold dress watch thing. Bremont just put one in a blacked out, ten sided sports watch, added a travel time you can jump forward with a single press of the crown, and capped the run at 20 pieces. The Supernova Perpetual Calendar GMT Monopusher is the most complicated watch in Bremont's space themed line so far, and the movement story behind it is the real hook. Let's get into it.

## Bremont Supernova Perpetual Calendar GMT Monopusher

**Announced:** November 24, 2026 ([Bremont](https://www.bremont.com/en-us/products/supernova-perpetual-calendar-gmt-monopusher-bracelet), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/bremont-supernova-perpetual-calendar-gmt-monopusher-black-dlc-introducing-price/), [Time+Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/bremont-adds-its-perpetual-calendar-gmt-monopusher-introducing/))

### What the dial is telling you

Bremont built the dial around a spacecraft solar array theme: a black galvanic metal base with a raised 3D grid, cut through by triangular pathways. Four displays sit on top of it, and each one does a single job.

* **12 o'clock, second time zone:** a domed globe display reads against a 24 hour scale split into day and night. Press the monopusher built into the crown and the GMT moves forward one hour at a time.
* **3 o'clock, month and leap year:** a four blade hand tracks the month and the four year leap cycle, with one blue lacquered blade marking the current year.
* **6 o'clock, date:** a domed disc with a textured moon at its center, read by a hand around it.
* **9 o'clock:** running seconds.

The applied triangular indexes and the black gold hands are filled with white Super-LumiNova that glows blue. Setup happens through two recessed correctors in the case side: the one at 2 o'clock adjusts the date and the one at 4 o'clock adjusts the month and leap year, per Monochrome and Time+Tide.

### The movement: Agenhor brains, British hands

This is where it gets interesting. The caliber **BHC9192MH** carries a perpetual calendar GMT module developed exclusively for Bremont by **Agenhor**, the Geneva workshop founded by Jean-Marc Wiederrecht in 1996 that has built mechanisms for MB&F, H. Moser & Cie. and Hermès. Bremont says the calendar hands work through 172 micro jumps over the four year cycle, and that its watchmakers at The Wing in Henley on Thames were trained by Agenhor's team so they could fully assemble the movement in Britain.

* **Winding:** manual
* **Jewels:** 19, with a Glucydur balance
* **Power reserve:** 50 hours
* **Regulation:** minus 6 to plus 6 seconds per day
* **Base:** a Sellita AMT6900 hand wound movement, per Monochrome (Bremont's own spec sheet does not name the base)

Monochrome and Time+Tide both note the same caliber debuted in Bremont's Altitude collection before landing here. You can see it through a decorated sapphire caseback.

### Case and specs

* **Case:** 904L stainless steel with "Ultra Black" DLC, screwless sandwich construction that echoes Bremont's Trip-Tick case
* **Size:** 41 mm wide, 13.9 mm thick, 49.7 mm lug to lug, 20 mm integrated lug width
* **Bezel:** ten sided black ceramic ring
* **Crystal:** domed sapphire with antireflective coating, plus a sapphire caseback
* **Crown:** push in DLC steel crown with the GMT monopusher built in
* **Water resistance:** 100 m (10 ATM)
* **Edition:** 20 pieces worldwide

### Price and availability

Bremont's US site lists both versions as available now:

* **Integrated DLC steel bracelet (SN41-PER-DLC-BK-B):** **$34,400 USD**
* **Black rubber strap with DLC steel folding clasp (SN41-PER-DLC-BK-R-S):** **$34,100 USD**

Both use a quick release system. For reference, Time+Tide lists £29,950 and £29,700 in the UK, and Monochrome lists EUR 35,950 and EUR 35,650 in Europe. Only 20 will be made across both configurations.

## My take

A Bremont with an Agenhor module is a statement, and it backs the statement up: a genuinely useful pair of complications for anyone who travels, a calendar that handles short months and leap years on its own as long as you keep it wound, and a case that does not need to be babied. The design is loud, and the black on black dial will not be for everyone, but the globe at 12 is one of the most charming GMT displays I have seen this year.

The price is the debate. At $34,400 USD you are in serious independent and big house perpetual calendar territory, so the question is whether British assembly and the Agenhor module carry that weight.
