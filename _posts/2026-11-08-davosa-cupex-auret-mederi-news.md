Davosa just walked into the most crowded fight in watchmaking under $1,500: the slim integrated sports watch. The new Cupex Automatic is 40 mm across, 10.2 mm thick, and rated to 100 m, with a Swiss automatic and a bracelet that grows out of the case. All five references are in stock on Davosa USA and on Davosa's own shop. I checked every size, price, and spec below against those product pages on November 8, 2026. Colombian newcomer Auret is also opening the shop tomorrow for its first watch, the 12 sided Mederi. [Time and Tide](https://www.timeandtidewatches.com/auret-mederi-micro-mondays/) published the full first look today.

## Davosa Cupex Automatic: a 10.2 mm sports watch from $1,150

Integrated sports watches usually ask you to pick a lane. Go thin and you often give up water resistance. Go tough and the case starts to feel like a hockey puck. Davosa is trying to have both. The [Cupex Automatic](https://www.davosa.com/collections/cupex-automatic) uses a stainless steel case that measures 40.0 mm wide, 45.6 mm lug to lug, and just 10.2 mm thick, with 100 m of water resistance. The official US and Swiss pages also list the watch at 132 g.

The name comes from the Latin *cuspis*, a point. You see that idea on the fixed bezel, whose kite shaped facets take their geometry from Davosa's compass rose emblem. Brushed and polished surfaces meet in sharp edges, and the bicolor versions add gold ion plating rather than solid gold. A fluted crown and a screw down exhibition caseback finish the package, under an anti reflective sapphire crystal.

The dial is Clous de Paris, that classic hobnail texture, with little fragments of the compass rose hiding in the pyramid shapes. Applied indices and the hands are filled with Super-LumiNova BGW9, which reads white in daylight and blue in the dark. Through the caseback you get another compass rose: the rotor that winds the movement.

That movement is Davosa's DAV 3021, the brand's name for a Sellita SW200-1 automatic. Davosa lists hours, minutes, seconds, a date with quick set, and a seconds stop (hacking). [WhoWatchWear](https://whowatchwear.com/davosa-cupex-automatic-brings-a-10-2mm-case-to-the-integrated-bracelet-field/) puts the beat at 28,800 vibrations per hour and the reserve at about 38 hours, which is the usual SW200-1 story. Davosa's own product pages I checked do not print those two figures, so I am repeating them as reported rather than as a number copied off the official spec sheet.

The bracelet is the whole point of this watch. It is integrated, 23.8 mm at the lugs, and it meets the case with no visible gap. The clasp is a folding design with a push button release. Because the bracelet is built in, this is not a watch you will casually hop onto a NATO. On [davosa.com](https://www.davosa.com/en/products/cupex-automatic-16159740) the steel models are €1,150 and the bicolor models are €1,200, tax included, with delivery listed at 3 to 5 days and a wooden box in the package. Davosa USA's prices above are the listed US figures as of this check, and those listings also showed the watches as available.

My take? At $1,150 you are buying Swiss assembly, a service friendly Sellita, 100 m of water resistance, and a genuinely thin sports case. That is a real answer to the PRX conversation if you want something less common on the wrist. The tradeoffs are honest too. The bracelet is a one way street, gold IP will wear like any plating, and a roughly 38 hour reserve (if WhoWatchWear has that right) means this watch wants wrist time. I would start with the steel blue or black before paying the extra $50 for two tone.

## Also new: Auret Mederi, a Colombian debut on sale November 9

[Auret](https://auret.co/) is a new Colombian brand, and its own site is counting down to a November 9, 2026 launch while inviting collectors to sign up among the first 100. There are no prices or spec sheets on that splash page yet. For the watch itself I am relying on [Time and Tide's Micro Mondays feature](https://www.timeandtidewatches.com/auret-mederi-micro-mondays/), published today, which walked through the debut collection in detail.

The watch is called the Mederi, from the Latin for "to heal." Time and Tide says the brand was founded by an oncologist who wanted the design to talk about how fragile time can feel. The collection has three dial colors and the same steel case: Origin in black, Horizon in blue, and Legacy in burgundy.

The case is 316L stainless steel with a 12 sided polished bezel. Time and Tide lists it at 40 mm across, 46 mm lug to lug, and 10.5 mm thick, with 20 mm lugs, sapphire front and back, a screw down crown and caseback, and 100 m of water resistance. The bezel is oriented so a point sits between the lugs rather than a flat, the opposite of the usual Royal Oak or Riviera setup.

The dial is the show. Time and Tide describes an undulating guilloché of concentric circles, 2 mm deep, with a fumé gradient, applied faceted indices, and a recessed small seconds subdial at 6 o'clock. The date that comes with the movement is left off the dial on purpose. The AURET logo turns its R into a stylized 12, which the brand treats as the starting point of every cycle.

Inside is a Swiss made Sellita SW260 automatic with small seconds, 31 jewels, 28,800 vibrations per hour, and a 41 hour reserve, specified at Sellita's D4 finishing level. Time and Tide notes perlage, nickel plated surfaces, snail finished barrels, and a custom brass open worked rotor shaped like a coffee flower, a nod to Colombia. Two quick release straps ship with the watch, a leather strap with rubber lining and a textile strap with leather lining, both color matched to the dial, with curved ends and a custom steel deployant clasp.

Time and Tide lists the Mederi at US$2,200 from the Auret boutique starting November 9. Earlier September writeups cited €1,750. Until Auret prints a number on [auret.co](https://auret.co/), treat both figures as reported prices, not a sticker copied off the official shop. At either number this is a confident ask for a first watch from a brand most readers have not heard of, and Time and Tide said as much, pointing out there is no bracelet yet and plenty of competition from houses with longer track records.
