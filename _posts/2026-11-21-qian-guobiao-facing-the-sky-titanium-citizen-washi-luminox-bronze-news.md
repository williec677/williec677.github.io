Saturday mornings are usually quiet in watch news, but one of the most interesting independents in the world picked today to drop something new. Qian GuoBiao, who builds and finishes every watch himself in Dongguan, China, has taken his signature Facing The Sky into titanium and given it two colors and his first bracelet. Joining it: a Citizen whose dial is literally handmade paper, and a Luminox that trades steel for bronze. Three very different price points, all worth your attention.

## Qian GuoBiao Facing The Sky 2.0 Titanium

**Released:** November 21, 2026 ([Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/qian-guobiao-introduces-the-facing-the-sky-2-0-titanium/), [Oracle of Time](https://oracleoftime.com/qian-guobiao-facing-the-sky-2-0-titanium/), [Independent Watcher](https://independentwatcher.com/collections/qian-guobiao))

![Qian GuoBiao Facing The Sky 2.0 Titanium with Lagoon dial on a dark alligator strap]({{ "/assets/img/watches/qian-guobiao-facing-the-sky-2-titanium-lagoon.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Qian GuoBiao via Oracle of Time (Facing The Sky 2.0 Titanium, Lagoon)*

If you have not followed Qian GuoBiao yet, here is the short version. The first Facing The Sky arrived in 2018 as a 43.5 mm piece built around one big idea: put the balance wheel on the dial, out in the open, "facing the sky." In 2025 he shrank it to a far more wearable 39 mm in steel as the Facing The Sky 2.0, which his own site lists as a 12 piece, subscription only series. The new Titanium edition (ref. AB-02Ti per Oracle of Time) keeps that architecture and changes almost everything you touch.

What is new and what carries over, per Fratello and Oracle of Time:

* **Case:** Grade 5 titanium, 39 mm wide, 46 mm lug to lug and 12.5 mm thick including the crystal, with alternating brushed and polished surfaces and a stepped bezel. Water resistance is 50 m and the lug width is a friendly 20 mm.
* **Two dials:** Lagoon, a soft greenish blue, and British Racing Green, the darker and more formal of the pair. Both carry an off center silver hour and minute subdial with heat blued hands.
* **The signature view:** the balance sits on the dial side under a multi layered, arrow shaped bridge, next to a swan neck regulator with a long arrow pointer that reads against a copper colored scale. Most watchmakers hide that regulator on the back. Qian puts it front and center.
* **Movement:** the in house hand wound caliber AB-02-2.0, 18,000 vph (2.5 Hz) with 40 hours of power reserve. Fratello says Qian makes everything except the jewels, balance wheel and hairspring, and finishes the bridges with mirror polished anglage. Through the sapphire caseback you get his bird shaped click and an individually numbered plate. Oracle of Time counts 16 jewels.
* **First bracelet:** a titanium bracelet designed and built in his own atelier, with arrow shaped links, end links engraved with the outline of the balance bridge, a push button folding clasp and quick release bars. A black JeaYou strap is included for a dressier look (Fratello calls it leather, Oracle of Time specifies alligator).

**Price and availability:** **$43,500 USD** excluding VAT, also listed at CHF 35,000 and HKD 340,000, per both outlets. Each dial is limited to **24 pieces**, so 48 in total, which is generous by Qian's single digit standards. Fratello reports the watch is on sale now with deliveries roughly 18 months after ordering. One note: Hong Kong retailer Independent Watcher lists both colors at HK$340,000 and already marks them sold out on its storefront, and the brand's own site had not added the titanium model when I checked this morning. If you want one, contact the brand directly and expect a waiting list.

**My take:** this is what makes the independent scene so exciting right now. One person, one atelier, a movement he largely makes himself, and a design that is instantly recognizable from across a room. $43,500 is serious money, and you are paying for hand finishing and rarity rather than brand power. But compare it with what the established houses charge for limited pieces, like the [Roger W. Smith Series Seven we covered this week]({% post_url 2026-11-19-roger-w-smith-series-seven-nomos-noir-oris-doraemon-news %}), and it starts to look like a collector's entry ticket to a name that is only going to get harder to buy. Lagoon on the bracelet is my pick.

## The Citizen AQ4100-65F with a Tosa washi dial

**Announced:** November 19, 2026 ([Citizen Japan](https://citizen.jp/news/2026/20261119_1.html), [The Citizen product page](https://citizen.jp/the-citizen/special/lineup/5sec/AQ4100-65F.html), [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/21/citizen-aq4100-65f-tosa-washi-dial/))

![The Citizen AQ4100-65F with a gray Tosa washi paper dial on a Super Titanium bracelet]({{ "/assets/img/watches/the-citizen-aq4100-65f-tosa-washi.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Citizen (The Citizen AQ4100-65F)*

The Citizen is the brand's flagship line, the one most Americans never see in a mall, and since 2017 it has been using Japanese washi paper for dials because the paper lets light pass through to the Eco-Drive solar cell underneath. This limited edition uses Tosa washi from Kochi Prefecture, specifically unryu paper, where long fibers are worked into the sheet to create drifting cloud and dragon like patterns. Citizen colors it a calm gray inspired by ink wash painting, and because the paper is handmade, no two dials will match.

Specs from Citizen's announcement:

* **Movement:** Eco-Drive caliber A060, rated to **±5 seconds per year**, with a perpetual calendar that needs no end of month correction until February 28, 2100. About 1.5 years of running time on a full charge with power save active.
* **Smart extras:** impact detection, automatic hand position correction, a date that changes right at midnight, a time difference setting and JIS class 1 magnetic resistance.
* **Case and bracelet:** Super Titanium with Citizen's Duratect Platinum hardening, 38.3 mm wide and 12.2 mm thick (design values), dual curved sapphire crystal with Clarity Coating, 10 bar water resistance.
* **Warranty:** three years, extendable to up to ten through the Citizen Owners Club.

**Price and availability:** ¥440,000 including tax (¥400,000 before tax), which works out to **about $2,774 USD (¥440,000)** at the ECB reference rate of November 20 (USD 1.0990 and JPY 174.35 per euro). Limited to **250 pieces worldwide**, with a Japan release planned for **December 17, 2026**. Citizen has not announced a US price or US release, so for now this is a Japan purchase or an import.

**My take:** a ±5 seconds per year solar watch with a handmade paper dial is about as Japanese as watchmaking gets: obsessive precision wrapped in quiet craft. It is a sharp contrast to the louder [Citizen Attesa Hazy limited editions from earlier this week]({% post_url 2026-11-20-nodus-oracle-time-sector-ii-red-jasper-alpina-galaga-citizen-attesa-hazy-news %}), and at 38.3 mm it is the one I would actually wear every day.

## Luminox Sport Timer Automatic Bronze XS.0921.BR

**Released:** November 19, 2026 ([Luminox](https://luminox.com/products/sport-timer-automatic-bronze), [Luminox Europe](https://de.luminox.com/en/blogs/news/luminox-automatic-sport-timer-bronze-xs-0921-br), [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/luminox-sport-timer-automatic-bronze/))

![Luminox Sport Timer Automatic Bronze XS.0921.BR with black dial, black ceramic bezel insert and black leather strap]({{ "/assets/img/watches/luminox-sport-timer-automatic-bronze-xs-0921-br.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Luminox (Sport Timer Automatic Bronze XS.0921.BR)*

Luminox is mostly known for tough quartz tool watches, so a bronze automatic is a fun left turn. The Sport Timer Automatic is an existing model; this edition swaps steel for bronze, which will build up its own patina with wear, so every piece ends up looking a little different. Per Luminox:

* **Case:** 42 mm bronze with a bronze unidirectional bezel and black ceramic insert, 13 mm tall, 24 mm lug width, about 95 g, screw down protected crown, 200 m water resistance. The screw in exhibition caseback is black IP coated 316L steel, so the part resting on your wrist is steel, not bronze.
* **Dial and lume:** black dial with bronze toned numerals and hands, day and date display, 15 Luminox Light Technology tritium elements that glow for up to 25 years, plus Super-LumiNova on the seconds hand tip.
* **Movement:** Swiss automatic Sellita with 38 hours of power reserve. Heads up on a conflict: the US product page says SW200-1, while the European page says SW 220-1. The day and date display points to the SW 220-1, but I am flagging it until Luminox confirms.
* **Strap:** black leather with off white stitching.

**Price and availability:** **$1,895 USD** on the Luminox US site (EUR 1,645 in Europe), available now, limited to **500 pieces worldwide** per Luminox Europe.

**My take:** bronze, a ceramic bezel, tritium lume and 200 m under $2,000 is a strong package, and the patina makes it personal.
