Most people shopping for a vintage Speedmaster are really shopping for a 145.022. It is the reference that introduced calibre 861, Omega made it for about two decades, and it comes in enough variants to keep you busy for years. The good news is that the variants follow a logic once you know where to look. The bad news is that every one of those details can be swapped.

## What the 145.022 is

Omega introduced the 145.022 in 1968 as the successor to the 145.012 and its column wheel calibre 321. The new calibre 861 was Lemania based, used a shuttle cam instead of a column wheel, and ran faster at 21,600 vibrations per hour. It is hand wound, robust, and cheaper to service than a 321. Fratello dates the run from 1968 to 1988 ([Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/buying-guide-omega-speedmaster-professional-145-022/)).

The case is 42mm wide with 20mm lugs and about 48mm lug to lug, under a Hesalite crystal ([Tempus Europa](https://tempuseuropa.com/en/reference/omega-speedmaster-145-022)). In 1983 the reference number changed to 145.0022, while the case back kept its 145.022 marking. From 1988 Omega used the product code 3590.50, and calibre 1861 did not arrive until 1996 or 1997.

## The suffix marks the case back, not the watch

Look inside the case back and you will find 145.022 followed by a two digit suffix. Fratello is clear that the suffix indicates the production year of the case back design, not the year the whole watch was made. The 145.022-69, for example, was produced from 1969 to 1971. To date a specific watch, use the movement serial number, and remember that movements could sit on a shelf before they were cased.

The steel variants are:

* **145.022-68 "Transitional."** The first 861 Speedmaster, still wearing the look of the 321 era, with an applied Omega logo on the dial ([Watchfid](https://www.watchfid.com/omega-speedmaster-145-022/)).
* **145.022-69.** Printed logo, step dial, and the widest range of case backs.
* **145.022-71.** The last 145.022 with a step dial.
* **145.022-74, -76, and -78.** Flat dials. These later watches are usually the most accessible way into the reference.

## Dials: step vs flat

A step dial has a raised flange that separates the center from the outer minute track, much like the 321 Speedmasters. Fratello places step dials on the -69 and -71, and flat dials from the -74 onward. The -76 even comes with small typography differences, such as a longer "S" in Speedmaster on later dials.

Neither style is better. Step dials carry a premium because they are earlier and closer to the 321 look. Some dials have also aged toward brown, which collectors call tropical, and those carry a premium too. Whatever you are looking at, check the tritium plots and the hands together. Original lume should have aged into matching tones. Fresh white hands on an aged dial usually mean a service replacement.

## Bezels: DON, DNN, and the 220

The tachymeter bezel is one of the most discussed parts of a vintage Speedmaster.

* **DON** means dot over ninety. On the 145.022 it belongs to the -68 and some, but not all, -69 watches. DON bezels also spell TACHYMÈTRE with an accented È.
* **DNN** means dot next to ninety, standard from the -71 onward.
* The **220 bezel** is a 1970 misprint where the scale reads 220 at the spot where it should read 200 ([Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/buying-guide-omega-speedmaster-professional-145-022/)).

DON bezels are now expensive parts, which is exactly why they get fitted to watches that never had one. A DON on a -71 or later case is a swap. A perfect insert on a heavily worn case deserves a question.

## Case backs

Fratello lists four regular case backs for the steel 145.022, plus one rarity:

1. **Seahorse with "Speedmaster" text** on the -68 and -69, considered the pre Moon case back and used on -69 cases until 1971.
2. **Straight "First Watch Worn On The Moon" writing** on the -69, from 1970 to 1973.
3. **Straight writing with "Apollo XI 1969"** added, on the -69, around 1971 and 1972.
4. **"Flight Qualified by NASA For All Manned Space Missions"** on the -71 through -78.
5. **"No NASA"** on a small number of -71 cases, rare enough to treat separately.

Straight writing backs, especially the Apollo XI version, are the most sought after, and several designs overlapped in time. Make sure the back fits the suffix and the movement serial.

A quick word on the legend. The Speedmasters that went to the Moon were 321 powered. The 145.022 came close, though. Fratello notes that Apollo 17 astronaut Ron Evans had one aboard the spacecraft for an experiment, while the watch on his wrist was a 321 model.

## Movement and bracelet details

Early 861 movements used a steel chronograph brake. In 1971 Omega switched to a Delrin brake, a nylon part chosen for wear resistance. Ask your watchmaker about the brake when you service the watch.

Period bracelets include the 1039 with flexible links (until 1971), the famous 1171, and the 1116 and 1175. In the 1980s Omega also used the Holzer made 1171/1, which uses pins rather than screws. A correct era bracelet adds value and comfort. A stretched bracelet is a negotiating point, not a deal breaker.

## Paperwork: Extract of the Archives

Omega historically offered an Extract of the Archives that confirms a watch's production date and delivery country. Fratello calls it the only way to be certain of the production year. Availability has changed, and collectors report that Omega stopped offering new extracts some years ago. Check Omega's own site for the current status before you make an extract a condition of sale, and treat a seller's promise to get one with caution.

## Quick takeaways

* The 145.022 is the first cal 861 Speedmaster, made from 1968 to 1988, renumbered 145.0022 in 1983.
* The case back suffix marks the case back design, not the build year. Date by movement serial.
* Step dials on the -69 and -71, flat dials from the -74.
* DON belongs on the -68 and some -69s. The 220 is a 1970 misprint.
* Seahorse, straight writing, and NASA backs should fit the suffix and serial.
