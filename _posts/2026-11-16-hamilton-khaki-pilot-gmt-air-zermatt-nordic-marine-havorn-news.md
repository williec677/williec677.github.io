Hamilton has been building watches with Air Zermatt, the Swiss alpine helicopter rescue outfit, since 2011. For the 15th anniversary it did not just swap a dial color. The new **Hamilton Khaki Pilot GMT Air Zermatt**, reference **H76505930**, puts a forged carbon case and a proper traveler's GMT into the Khaki Pilot family for the first time, and it costs $1,725 USD. That is a lot of watch for the money, and if you have been waiting for Hamilton to make a GMT that feels like a tool instead of a fashion piece, this is it. I checked the details below against [Hamilton's own Swiss product page](https://www.hamiltonwatch.com/en-ch/h76505930-khaki-aviation-gmt-air-zermatt-auto.html), [Monochrome's first look](https://monochrome-watches.com/hamilton-khaki-pilot-gmt-air-zermatt-accessible-true-gmt-watch-in-forged-carbon-review-price/) published this morning, and Hamilton's press release as carried by [Europa Star](https://www.europastar.com/time-keeper/1004116603-hamilton-celebrates-15-years-of-partnership-with.html). After that, a sharp little dress watch from a Nordic micro brand that I missed when it launched earlier this month.

A quick honesty note on thickness: Hamilton's own product page says 11.1 mm, while Monochrome's spec list says 13.38 mm. Those can't both be right, and I could not get Hamilton's US page to load to settle it, so treat the case height as unconfirmed until you see one on the wrist. Everything else lines up across all three sources.

## Why the movement is the headline

Plenty of watches under $2,000 call themselves a GMT. Most of them are "caller" or "office" GMTs, where the crown jumps the 24 hour hand and you leave your main hour hand alone. That is great for tracking a colleague in another time zone, and annoying when you land in Tokyo and want your main time to change.

The H-14 works the other way. As Monochrome explains, the local hour hand jumps in one hour steps on its own, without stopping the minutes and without disturbing the 24 hour hand that keeps your home time. That is the traveler setup you get on a Rolex GMT Master II or a Tudor Black Bay Pro, and it is still rare at this price. The H-14 itself isn't brand new: it is the Swatch Group's Powermatic 80 adapted for a GMT display, first seen in 2020 in the now discontinued Khaki Aviation Converter Auto GMT and since used across Hamilton, Certina and Mido. What's new is getting it into a Khaki Pilot, and getting 80 hours of reserve with it. Take it off Friday night and it will still be running Monday morning.

## The case and dial: rescue helicopter energy

Forged carbon is the fun part. Every case comes out with its own marbled grain, it is light, and it gives the watch a stealthy, technical look that suits a pilot's tool. The red details aren't random either. Hamilton ties the red GMT hand, the red seconds tip and the bright red strap to Air Zermatt's red rescue helicopters, and Zermatt sits in red on the city ring to mark Central European Time.

That inner city ring, turned by the crown at 2 o'clock, gives the dial a world timer vibe without being a true world timer. Line up the city you want against the 24 hour scale and you can read a third zone at a glance. For travelers that is genuinely handy, and it makes the dial far busier than a classic Khaki Pilot, so this is a watch for people who like instruments, not minimalists.

## My take

At $1,725 USD, this is one of the most interesting GMTs you can buy new from a major Swiss brand under $2,000. A flyer style GMT, 80 hours of reserve, 100 m and a forged carbon case is a spec sheet that would have cost a lot more not long ago. The price is high for Hamilton, and Monochrome says as much, but I think the package earns it. The trade offs are the busy dial and a 42 mm case that some wrists will find big, though carbon keeps the weight down.

## Also new this month: Nordic Marine Instruments Havørn, a $349 USD rectangular dress watch

Micro brand Nordic Marine Instruments usually makes tough, sea ready watches. On November 4 it [announced](https://nordicmarineinstruments.com/pages/havorn-launch) its first dress watch, the **Havørn**, named for the white tailed sea eagle, and [aBlogtoWatch reviewed it today](https://www.ablogtowatch.com/actually-affordable-nordic-marine-instruments-havorn-dress-watch/). I missed it at launch, and it deserves a mention because it is a fresh take on the rectangular dress watch at a price almost anyone can reach.

* Case: 316L steel, 38 x 29 mm rectangular case with faceted lugs and twin crown guards, curved sapphire crystal, screwed solid caseback
* Thickness: 7.7 mm per the brand's spec sheet (aBlogtoWatch measured about 7.6 mm at the thickest point)
* Water resistance: 30 m, push pull crown
* Movement: Swiss Made Ronda 1062 quartz, rated at minus 10 to plus 20 seconds per month, battery life up to about six years
* Dials: Arctic Silver, Sky Blue and Ember Red, all with a textured wave pattern and sunburst finish, lumed hands and markers
* Strap: sailcloth with leather lining, 20 mm tapering to 18 mm
* Price: $349 USD on the brand's site, available to order now, with shipping listed as mid November 2026

aBlogtoWatch points out that the case shape nods to the Cartier Tank, but the crown guards and faceted lugs give it a sportier, modern edge. aBlogtoWatch's reviewer said he wished it had more than 30 m given the brand's marine roots, and I agree, but for a dress watch that's plenty. If you want a rectangular watch for weddings and the office without spending Tank money, this is a smart one to look at.
