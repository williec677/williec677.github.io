Put a Sea Dweller and a Submariner side by side and most people could not tell you which is which. Black dial, black ceramic bezel, Mercedes hands, Oyster bracelet. But these two watches were built for very different jobs, and in 2026 the price gap between them tells a fascinating story.

If you are deciding between the two, here is everything that separates them, with every spec and US list price checked on rolex.com on November 13, 2026.

## Two watches, two missions

### The Submariner: the original

[Rolex's newsroom](https://newsroom.rolex.com/watches/oyster-collection/submariner) calls the Submariner the first Rolex divers' wristwatch. It launched in 1953, waterproof to 100m. A year later, that rating went to 200m. The Submariner Date arrived in 1969, the date model went to 300m in 1979, and the no date model followed to 300m in 1989.

The Sub became the template for the modern dive watch, and then it became something bigger: the watch people wear everywhere, from boardrooms to beaches. If you want the history of the vintage side, my [Submariner 5513 vs 1680 guide](/rolex-submariner-5513-vs-1680/) covers two of the greats.

### The Sea Dweller: built for saturation diving

The [Sea Dweller](https://newsroom.rolex.com/watches/oyster-collection/sea-dweller) came in 1967, designed for and with the pioneers of professional deep sea diving. Rolex says it was initially waterproof to 610m, and from 1978 to 1,220m.

Its signature feature is the helium escape valve, which Rolex patented in 1967. Saturation divers spend long periods under pressure, and Rolex explains that the valve lets excess pressure built up inside the case escape during decompression in a hyperbaric chamber, without compromising the watch's waterproofness. That is the kind of problem almost none of us will ever have, and that is exactly what makes it cool.

## The details that set them apart

### Size

The Sea Dweller grew from 40mm to 43mm for its 50th anniversary in 2017, according to Rolex. At 43mm, it has real wrist presence. The Submariner at 41mm is the more balanced, versatile size for most wrists. If you are on the fence, this is usually the deciding factor.

### The dial

The Sea Dweller 126600 has a red "Sea Dweller" line on the dial. Rolex says it is a reference to the first model. It is a subtle detail, but to collectors it instantly separates the Sea Dweller from a Sub at a glance.

### The Cyclops

Earlier Sea Dweller references had a plain crystal over the date. That changed with the 2017 update, when Rolex added a Cyclops lens to magnify the date. Some purists still miss the clean crystal. Others love the readability. Either way, the current Sea Dweller and the Submariner Date now share that feature.

### The movements

The Sea Dweller and Submariner Date both use calibre 3235. The no date Submariner uses calibre 3230. Both are Rolex manufacture movements with the Chronergy escapement, a blue Parachrom hairspring, approximately 70 hours of reserve, and Superlative Chronometer certification, precise to within two seconds a day either way after casing, according to Rolex.

### The bracelet

All three ship on the Oyster bracelet with an Oysterlock safety clasp and the Glidelock extension system. Rolex says Glidelock offers 10 notches of about 2mm, so you can adjust the length by about 20mm without tools. It is one of the most practical features on any Rolex. For more on how the Oyster bracelet compares with the Jubilee, see yesterday's [Jubilee vs Oyster guide](/rolex-jubilee-vs-oyster-bracelet/).

## Here is where it gets interesting: the market

At Rolex, the Sea Dweller lists for about $4,500 USD more than the no date Submariner. On the pre owned market, that order flips.

* **Sea Dweller 126600:** WatchCharts estimates a private sale price of about **$11,560 USD** as of November 12, 2026, below its about $14,550 USD list price. Expect about $12,864 USD from a pre owned dealer ([WatchCharts](https://watchcharts.com/watch_model/1300-rolex-sea-dweller-126600/overview)).
* **Submariner 124060:** WatchCharts estimates about **$12,263 USD** as of November 12, 2026, above its about $10,050 USD list price. Expect about $13,534 USD from a dealer ([WatchCharts](https://watchcharts.com/watch_model/21813-rolex-submariner-124060/overview)).

Read that again. On WatchCharts' numbers, a pre owned Sea Dweller is estimated to cost less than a pre owned no date Submariner. You get the bigger case, the date, the helium valve, and 1,220m of depth rating for less money. ## Who should buy which

**Buy the Submariner if:**

* You want the most versatile Rolex dive watch for a wide range of wrists.
* You prefer the cleaner no date look of the 124060, or the classic Submariner Date.
* Resale strength matters to you. On WatchCharts' November numbers, the 124060 trades above list.

**Buy the Sea Dweller if:**

* You like a bigger, more substantial watch and your wrist suits 43mm.
* You love tool watch history and the helium escape valve story.
* You are buying pre owned and want the most watch for the money, based on WatchCharts' current estimates.

## My verdict

If I were buying new at list price, I would take the Submariner. The 41mm case suits more wrists, and the 124060 is the purest expression of the Rolex diver. But if I were shopping pre owned this month, the Sea Dweller is incredibly hard to ignore. More watch, more history, and on WatchCharts' numbers, less money. That is a rare thing in the Rolex world.
