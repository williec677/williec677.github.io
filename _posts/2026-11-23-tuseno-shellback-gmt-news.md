The red and blue GMT bezel is having a moment, and the reason is no secret: Rolex no longer sells one in steel, as we laid out in our [affordable Rolex GMT alternatives guide]({% post_url 2026-11-05-affordable-rolex-gmt-alternatives %}). Into that gap walks Tusenö, the Gothenburg microbrand that has been making Swedish automatic watches since 2015. Today it took the wraps off the Shellback GMT, a travel version of its best known diver, and it did more than drop a new movement into an old case. The case is new, thinner and a touch smaller, the bracelet tapers harder, and the price stays just over a thousand dollars. Let's get into it.

## Tusenö Shellback GMT

**Announced:** November 23, 2026 ([Tusenö](https://tuseno.com/collections/shellback-gmt), [Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/tuseno-introduces-the-shellback-gmt/))

![Tusenö Shellback GMT Blue Linen with red and blue sapphire bezel on the wrist]({{ "/assets/img/watches/tuseno-shellback-gmt-blue-linen-wrist.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Tusenö (Shellback GMT Blue Linen)*

If you have not met the Shellback before, it is Tusenö's sandwich dial diver: big round lume plots, bold batons and the brand's signature hands. The GMT keeps that face almost untouched, with a double baton at 12 and a date window at 6 in the press images, so it still reads instantly as a Shellback. Underneath, Tusenö says the case is an entirely new construction, and the numbers back it up.

### Case, bracelet and specs

Here is what Tusenö's product pages list for all three versions:

* **Case:** 316L stainless steel, 39 mm case with a 40 mm bezel, 47 mm lug to lug, 10.85 mm thick excluding the sapphire
* **Hardening:** a 1200 HV surface treatment on every stainless steel component, bracelet included
* **Movement:** Swiss Sellita SW330-2 in Elaboré grade, about 56 hours of power reserve, with a custom rotor etched with the brand's Guiding Light symbol and logo
* **Bezel:** ball bearing, 24 click bidirectional bezel with a sapphire 24 hour insert
* **Lume:** Super-LumiNova C1 Blue X1 on the dial, hands and bezel
* **Crystal and caseback:** box sapphire crystal and a sapphire display caseback, both with seven layers of inner antireflective coating
* **Water resistance:** 200 m (20 ATM)
* **Bracelet:** steel, tapering from 20 mm at the lugs to 16 mm at the clasp, half links included, with a compact diver clasp that hides a 9 mm on the fly extension
* **Origin:** Swiss made

For context, the current Shellback diver is listed by Tusenö at 40 mm wide, 47 mm lug to lug and 11.9 mm thick, with a bracelet that tapers from 20 to 18 mm. So the GMT trims about a millimeter of height and pulls the bracelet in another 2 mm at the clasp while keeping the same lug to lug length. Worn & Wound's Zach Kazan called it "an objectively better watch than any previous release in the Shellback family," and on paper those changes are exactly the ones that make a sport watch wear smaller.

### Three dials

* **Blue Linen:** deep blue dial with a heavy linen texture, red GMT hand and accents, blue and red sapphire bezel. This is the Pepsi.
* **Green Linen:** deep green linen dial, orange GMT hand and accents, two tone green sapphire bezel.
* **Frozen White:** silver white dial with a frozen texture that Tusenö says shifts from crisp silver white to warm cream as the light changes, red GMT details, gunmetal plated hands and the same blue and red bezel.

### Caller GMT, not flyer

One thing to know before you fall for the bezel. The SW330-2 is a caller GMT: the 24 hour hand is the one you set independently, while the local hour hand moves with the minutes. That is great for tracking a second time zone from home, less convenient when you land somewhere new and want to jump the local hour. Worn & Wound's review is openly skeptical of the caller layout, so if you travel a lot, weigh that. My own 1989 GMT Master II 16710 works the other way, and if you want to see how that system sets, our [how to set a Rolex GMT Master II]({% post_url 2026-11-18-how-to-set-rolex-gmt-master-ii %}) walkthrough shows the difference.

**Price and availability:** Tusenö lists all three versions at **$1,045 USD** (12,495 SEK, €1,095), with preorders opening **December 3** and deliveries listed for **July and August 2027** in a limited production run. The preorder terms add $150 off plus a free rubber strap, and Tusenö says its launch price applies to the first 50 pieces of each color, with regular preorder pricing after that. Worn & Wound also reports the $1,045 USD retail price and the December 3 sale date.

## My take: where it fits

At $1,045 USD you are getting a Swiss Sellita GMT, a sapphire bezel insert, 200 m and a 10.85 mm case. The closest Swiss comparison from our GMT alternatives guide is the Christopher Ward C63 Sealander GMT, which also runs the SW330-2. Christopher Ward's US site lists the 39 mm black dial version at $1,395 USD in its strap configuration tonight, with a fixed steel bezel and 150 m of water resistance ([Christopher Ward](https://www.christopherward.com/c63-sealander-gmt/C63-39AGM4-S00K0-VC.html)). So the Tusenö comes in $350 USD lower while adding a steel bracelet, a rotating sapphire 24 hour bezel and an extra 50 m of water resistance. If you want a flyer GMT for less, that guide also covers the Miyota 9075 options like the Lorier Hyperion and Vaer G5, and Direnzo's [DRZ 08 Cosmonaire GMT]({% post_url 2026-11-21-direnzo-drz-08-cosmonaire-gmt-isotope-on-sale-news %}) is another recent microbrand travel watch worth a look.

The tradeoff is time. You are paying in December for a watch slated for summer 2027, which is normal for a small brand but worth planning around. My pick is the Blue Linen: it is the most honest answer to the steel Pepsi Rolex stopped making, and the linen texture keeps it from looking like a straight copy.
