Here is the kind of watch drop I love: no hype machine, no 40 piece allocation drama, just a genuinely well designed automatic at a price a real person can pay. Merci, the Paris concept store that has quietly built one of the most tasteful little watch lines out there, launched its third collection today, the Super. Then we jump to the other end of the spectrum with a brand new independent called Algorithme du Temps, whose seconds hand slows down, stops, runs backwards and sprints, and still lands on twelve at every single minute. Let's go.

## Merci Super

**Announced:** November 18, 2026 ([Merci](https://merci-merci.com/en/pages/montres-super), [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/merci-super))

### Merci's third act

According to Hodinkee, Merci's original LMM-01 line takes its cues from the 1930s and is still in production, and this spring's Beaumarchais moved a decade forward with a more complex octagonal case. The Super jumps ahead again to the architecture and industrial design of the 1960s and 70s, and each dial carries a Plan name tied to a real building. The numbers are a neat easter egg too: they match the years those buildings were completed or opened, 1972 for the Nakagin Capsule Tower, 1974 for Charles de Gaulle Terminal 1, 1970 for the Brasília Cathedral and 1963 for the Maison de la Radio.

### Four dials, four buildings

* **Plan 19.72:** black dial with openwork style Arabic numerals and round markers, a nod to the stacked capsule modules and porthole windows of Tokyo's Nakagin Capsule Tower. Brushed silvered hands, black cordovan strap.
* **Plan 19.74:** the bright one. An opaline center with a brushed outer ring, black stick markers, only the 12 and 6 numerals, and a blue tip on the central seconds hand. Merci says it is inspired by the radial shapes of Terminal 1 at Paris Charles de Gaulle airport. Dark brown cordovan strap.
* **Plan 19.70:** a black take on that same layout with white stick markers and the 12 and 6 numerals, inspired by the radiating lines of Oscar Niemeyer's Brasília Cathedral seen from above. Black cordovan strap.
* **Plan 19.63:** the minimalist. Concentric circles inspired by the Maison de la Radio in Paris, a black dial with fine markers and no numerals (Hodinkee describes a gray outer ring shifting to a black center), and big white hands. Hodinkee notes the hands are solid and fully coated in Super-LumiNova, and its writer picked this one as the standout. Dark brown cordovan strap.

### Case and movement

The case is where the Super really wins on paper. It is 36 mm across and only 9.9 mm thick, with a compact 42.8 mm lug to lug, so it should wear beautifully on smaller and average wrists. Hodinkee describes the shape as broad and brushed with smooth curves and an angular feel, and says it is delightful in the metal. Merci lists a sapphire crystal, a screwed steel caseback, a smooth bezel, mixed brushed and polished surfaces, and 5 bar of water resistance. Inside is the Miyota 9039 automatic, a no date three hander running at 28,800 vph with about 42 hours of power reserve. Merci says the watches are assembled in France.

### Strap and lug width

Every Super ships on a cordovan leather strap, and Merci's spec sheet lists a 19 mm lug width. ### My take

This is a lot of watch design for about six hundred bucks. A 36 mm by 9.9 mm automatic with a sapphire crystal, a proper cordovan strap and four dials that each have a real story behind them is exactly what the affordable category needs more of. If I am picking one, it is the Plan 19.74, because that opaline and brushed sector dial with the blue tipped seconds hand is pure charm. The fair criticisms: 5 bar is fine for daily life but not for swimming, the Miyota 9039 is reliable but not exciting, and 19 mm lugs make strap shopping slightly harder. ## Also new: Algorithme du Temps Rêveries des Secondes

**Announced:** November 18, 2026 ([Algorithme du Temps](https://www.algorithmedutemps.com/), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/algorithme-du-temps-reveries-des-secondes-watch-indie-watchmaking-seconds-hand-non-linear-motion-introducing-price/))

![Algorithme du Temps Rêveries des Secondes in polished 904L steel with grained silver dial, hour and minute sub dials, blued seconds hand and the cam aperture at six o'clock, on a black strap]({{ "/assets/img/watches/algorithme-du-temps-reveries-des-secondes.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Algorithme du Temps*

Ever stared at a clock while waiting for someone and felt like the seconds were crawling? Founder Nadim Elgarhy, who has a degree in computer engineering and algorithms, built a whole watch around that feeling. The Rêveries des Secondes, the first piece from his new brand and labeled Étude Nº1, has a central seconds hand that never moves evenly. It can drop well below normal speed, stop completely, run backwards at normal speed or race up to three times faster. Per the brand, it runs backwards 19% of the time and changes direction 46 times in every five minute cycle. And yet at every full minute it crosses twelve exactly, after running forward for at least two seconds.

### How it works

The escapement and going train of the Sellita SW200-1 base are left alone. A module on the dial side adds a small differential to the seconds, so the hand shows the movement's real seconds plus an offset. That offset is steered by an anchor resting on two shaped cams, one turning in 50 seconds and the other in 42.86 seconds. They only line up again every five minutes, so that is how often the pattern repeats, and the cam profiles are computed so the offset cancels out at every minute. The coolest part: each watch gets its own pair of cams, so no two pieces share the same seconds pattern. You can watch the cams and anchor work through a 10 mm aperture at six o'clock, while hours and minutes run normally on recessed sub dials at 10:30 and 1:30.

### Case and price

The case is cushion shaped, mirror polished 904L steel at 38.0 mm by 42.1 mm and 10.53 mm thick, with a domed sapphire crystal and a closed, acid etched caseback. The base movement gives about 38 hours of power reserve at 28,800 vph. Monochrome lists no water resistance rating and a black alligator strap. It will be a numbered series of 40 pieces.

**Important:** this is not a finished watch you can order today. Monochrome reports the price is expected at around CHF 16,800 excluding taxes, which is about $19,935 USD at today's ECB rates (1.0968 USD and 0.9243 CHF per euro), and says the final price will be confirmed once the prototype is ready and the production partners are chosen. Right now the brand's site only takes registrations of interest, and no delivery date has been announced.

### My take

Watchmakers have played with seconds before, from the Swiss railway clock that pauses at twelve to John C. Taylor's Corpus Clock in Cambridge, both mentioned by Monochrome. But a fully mechanical wristwatch where every single piece has its own computed pattern is a seriously fun idea, and the dial, with the cams on display, looks fantastic. It is still a prototype with an unconfirmed price and an SW200 base, so I would wait for the final specs before getting too excited.
