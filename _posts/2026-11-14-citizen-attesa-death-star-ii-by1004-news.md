That's no moon. Citizen has taken the most famous line in Star Wars and built a watch around it. The new **Citizen Attesa Death Star II, reference BY1004-50E**, replaces the moon in its moonphase display with the Galactic Empire's battle station, and it is now on sale on Citizen's US site for **$1,500 USD**. It landed on [citizenwatch.com](https://www.citizenwatch.com/us/en/product/BY1004-50E) this week, with [Gizmochina](https://www.gizmochina.com/2026/11/12/citizen-attesa-death-star-ii-watch-launched-specs-price/) and [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/12/this-moonphase-tracks-the-death-star-instead-of-the-moon/) both reporting the US launch on November 12. Every spec below comes from Citizen's own product page, checked on November 14, 2026, with outside reporting linked wherever I lean on it.

## What Citizen actually built

Strip away the license and this is a serious piece of Japanese tech. Attesa is Citizen's top tier travel line, the one that usually stays home in Japan, and this watch carries the full toolkit: light powered Eco-Drive, atomic radio timekeeping, a perpetual calendar, world time and a power reserve display, all in a black coated Super Titanium case and bracelet.

| | Citizen Attesa Death Star II BY1004-50E |
|---|---|
| Case | 41.5 mm gray Super Titanium with Duratect DLC coating (Citizen's marketing copy rounds it to 42 mm) |
| Thickness | 10.8 mm (The Gadgeteer and Notebookcheck) |
| Crystal | Anti reflective sapphire |
| Water resistance | 100 m (10 bar) |
| Movement | Eco-Drive caliber H874, solar powered, radio controlled |
| Functions | Moonphase with the Death Star as the moon, perpetual calendar, day and date, world time, power reserve display, atomic timekeeping |
| Dial | Black, with a 3D inkjet printed image of the second Death Star under construction |
| Bracelet | Super Titanium with DLC coating, fold over clasp with push buttons |
| Price | **$1,500 USD**, Citizen US retail |

Citizen says the DLC treated titanium is 40 percent lighter and five times harder than stainless steel. On a full black bracelet watch, that hardness is the difference between a coating that looks fresh in two years and one that is covered in desk scuffs.

### The Death Star moonphase

This is the part people will screenshot. At six o'clock, where you would normally see a little golden moon drift across a starry disc, Citizen put the Death Star. As the lunar cycle moves on, the station rotates through the aperture. According to [The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/11/12/this-moonphase-tracks-the-death-star-instead-of-the-moon/) and [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Citizen-s-new-Star-Wars-watch-replaces-the-Moon-with-the-Death-Star-limited-to-1-500-units.1448587.0.html), the H874 uses Citizen's Luna Program to work out the moon's age from the calendar on its own, and you can flip the display between Northern and Southern Hemisphere views. Citizen's page adds that the phase is kept on track through radio reception. No crown fiddling, no app, and no setting the moonphase by hand after it runs down, because thanks to Eco-Drive it basically never runs down. Both outlets put a full charge at up to about two and a half years in power save mode.

The rest of the dial tells the Return of the Jedi story. That half built second Death Star spreads across the right side, printed in 3D so the plating catches light. A 24 hour subdial sits at nine, the day of the week sits up top, the date window tucks in at about half past four, and a thin red seconds hand sweeps over everything like a turbolaser shot.

### How many will there be?

Here is the one spec you should know is still fuzzy. **Citizen's US product page says 400 pieces worldwide.** Gizmochina repeats that 400 figure, but Notebookcheck and The Gadgeteer both report 1,500 pieces worldwide. I am going with the brand's own number on the US page while flagging that outlets disagree, so treat the exact run size as unconfirmed until Citizen clarifies. Either way, this is a limited edition, not a permanent catalog piece.

Notebookcheck also reports, citing a Japanese retailer listing, that the watch went on sale in Japan on November 5 at ¥162,800, which it converts to about $1,019 USD. That gap versus the $1,500 US price is typical for Attesa models sold outside Japan, so if you see Japanese listings pop up on eBay, expect import costs on top.

## My take

I love when a brand does a licensed watch like it means it. Most movie tie ins are a logo slapped on a basic quartz. This one is a proper Attesa with a perpetual calendar, sapphire, titanium and radio sync, and the Star Wars piece is built into a real complication instead of just printed on the caseback. A rotating Death Star moonphase is genuinely clever, and the all black DLC look is exactly what the Empire would wear.

Now the honest part. Fifteen hundred dollars is real money for a Citizen, and the dial is busy, so if you want a clean daily watch this is not it. But if you are a Star Wars fan who wants one watch that is both a collectible and something you can wear to the office, the BY1004-50E is a far better buy than most franchise merch. And Citizen is not done: Notebookcheck notes the brand already released a Death Star themed Imperial Battle AW1857-57W earlier this year, so this is the second Death Star Citizen has sold in 2026. With the 50th anniversary of the original film coming in 2027, expect more trips to a galaxy far, far away.
