The last two days were stacked. Christopher Ward handed its chiming Bel Canto to the jokers at **Mr Jones Watches** and got back the most colorful watch it has ever sold. **Zenith** brought an American guilloche artist into its chronometer world with a 25 piece G.F.J. **Petermann Bédat** made one of the best chronographs in independent watchmaking even smaller. And artist **Takashi Murakami** stopped collaborating and started his own brand. Specs and prices come from the brands and from Worn & Wound, Monochrome, aBlogtoWatch, Fratello, Revolution and Hodinkee, all checked on November 13, 2026.

## Christopher Ward x Mr Jones C1 Bel Canto Lucia: the hour rings from a bird's tail

The Bel Canto launched in 2022 as a chiming watch that nobody saw coming from Christopher Ward: a hammer strikes a gong once at the top of every hour, a sonnerie au passage, at a price that made the Swiss blink. Four years later, [Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/christopher-ward-and-mr-jones-launch-the-new-c1-bel-canto-lucia/) calls the Lucia the first time Christopher Ward has really given up creative control of the watch to someone else. The partner is Mr Jones, the London brand famous for turning ideas about time into visual jokes, and the two worked together before on the long sold out C1 Celestial Moonphase.

The theme comes from Donizetti's opera *Lucia di Lammermoor*, where the heroine sings about a bird only she can see. Here that bird lives on the dial, and it is not just decoration. Its beak is the chime on and off indicator, opening and closing when you press the pusher at 4 o'clock. Its tail is the hammer, so you can watch it creep back into position all hour long before it snaps forward and hits the gong. Worn & Wound counts 19 colors on the dial, each one with its own pad printing plate, done in house by Mr Jones artisans.

| | Christopher Ward C1 Bel Canto Lucia |
|---|---|
| Case | Grade 5 titanium Light catcher case, 41 mm, 48 mm lug to lug, 14.31 mm tall, case weight 54 g |
| Crystal | New box sapphire |
| Water resistance | 30 m |
| Movement | Calibre FS01 chiming module on a Sellita SW200-2 base, automatic, 28,800 vph, 32 jewels, 60 to 65 hours (Christopher Ward quotes the range) |
| Complication | Sonnerie au passage, one strike on the hour, on and off via the pusher at 4 o'clock |
| Strap options | White or blue Italian leather $5,550, Bader bracelet $5,895, Consort bracelet $5,995 |
| Availability | Not a limited edition |

All three USD prices are Christopher Ward's own, from its online store on November 13, 2026.

### My take

This is the most fun watch of the week, period. The Bel Canto was already the best party trick under $10,000, and Mr Jones just made the trick part of the story instead of something you have to explain. The fact that it is not limited is huge: no raffle, no flipper markup, you just buy it. If you want the cleanest version, go white leather; if you want maximum wrist presence, the Bader bracelet. I have a soft spot here because Will wears a Christopher Ward GMT, and this brand keeps proving it is way more than a homage label. ## Zenith G.F.J. Calibre 135 Double Signed J.N. Shapiro: Los Angeles guilloche, Le Locle chronometry

Zenith's G.F.J. line is built around the Calibre 135, the hand wound movement Ephrem Jobin designed in 1948 for observatory competitions. [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/zenith-gfj-calibre-135-double-signed-with-jn-shapiro-guilloche-dial-by-american-independent-watchmaker-review-price/) notes the competition version won five straight first places at the Neuchâtel Observatory from 1950 to 1954, and Zenith re engineered it for the modern G.F.J. in 2025. After a first Double Signed edition with Japan's Naoya Hida, this second one goes to J.N. Shapiro, the Los Angeles watchmaker Joshua Shapiro started in a home workshop in 2018 after a career as a school principal and US history teacher.

Shapiro cut the solid silver dial himself on rose engine and straight line machines, with three patterns: barleycorn on the outside, basketweave toward the center, and his signature Infinity Weave in the small seconds at 6. Applied 18k rose gold rings split the zones, and an applied gold plate carries both names plus the G.F.J. initials. Flip it over and the bridges wear damaskeening, the wavy decoration from old American pocket watches, over rhodium plating.

### My take

This is the kind of collab that makes sense: an American indie who taught himself guilloche, working on one of the greatest chronometer calibers ever made. Three textures on one dial sounds busy, but the gold rings keep it orderly. It is a 25 piece trophy for serious collectors, not a buy now watch for most of us, but it is a sign Zenith wants the G.F.J. to be a stage for independent craft.

## Petermann Bédat 2914: the monopusher gets smaller

Petermann Bédat's founders came from A. Lange & Söhne, and their earlier reference 2941 split seconds chronograph is one of the most loved independent chronographs around. According to [Revolution](https://revolutionwatch.com/petermann-bedat-2914-chronograph-monopusher/), with [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/introducing-petermann-bedat-2914-chronograph) also covering the launch, the new 2914 drops the split seconds module, keeps the monopusher and the jumping minute counter, and shrinks the case from 38.5 mm to 36.5 mm. The dial is now brushed steel with smaller, darker sub dials instead of frosted platinum.

| | Petermann Bédat 2914 Chronograph Monopusher |
|---|---|
| Case | Platinum, 36.5 mm x 9.9 mm |
| Water resistance | 30 m |
| Movement | Hand wound calibre 234, 18,000 vph (2.5 Hz), 56 hours, jumping chronograph minutes |
| Production | 20 pieces in platinum |
| Price | **About $216,000 USD (CHF 180,000 excluding taxes)** |

### My take

A 36.5 mm platinum chronograph under 10 mm thick is pure collector catnip. Revolution points out the slow 2.5 Hz beat suits the old school character, and that the movement still looks almost the same from the back as the 2941. It is priced for the top of the indie world, but the small size is the headline: proof that the downsizing trend has reached haute horlogerie chronographs.

## Takashi Murakami Time Objects: a skull that tells time

After several Hublot collaborations that started with the Classic Fusion Takashi Murakami All Black in 2021, the Japanese artist has launched his own brand, [Takashi Murakami Time Objects](https://monochrome-watches.com/japanese-artist-takashi-murakami-launches-own-watch-brand-time-objects-skull-flowers-cylindrical-jumping-hours-tourbillon-introducing-price/), per Monochrome. The first watch is the Skull Flowers Cylindrical Jumping Hours Tourbillon. The case is a sculpted Grade 5 titanium skull made of 33 parts, about 44 mm wide and 18.4 mm tall, with his smiling flowers spinning on ball bearings in the eye sockets. The time sits in a side window at 6 o'clock: hours on a cylinder that jumps each hour, minutes on a trailing disc.

The movement is the hand wound KM 7012 TM, developed by Marco Tedeschi, the former Hublot movement and case developer who later ran Romain Jérôme and founded Kross Studio. Monochrome lists 409 parts, 81 jewels, 21,600 vph, 125 hours of reserve and a tourbillon at 12 o'clock visible through the back. Water resistance is 30 m.
