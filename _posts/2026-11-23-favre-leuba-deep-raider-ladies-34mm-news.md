Here is a sentence I did not expect to write on a Monday: one of the most interesting dive watches of the relaunch era just got smaller, slimmer and a lot more colorful. Favre Leuba, the Le Locle house founded in 1737, announced the Deep Raider Ladies today, its first collection designed specifically for women since the brand came back in 2024. That is 11 references at once, all built around the same chunky, twisted lug Deep Raider DNA, now in a 34 mm case that sits under 10 mm thick. And unlike most "for her" launches, these keep a working dive bezel and 100 meters of water resistance. Let's get into it.

## Favre Leuba Deep Raider Ladies

**Announced:** November 23, 2026 ([Favre Leuba](https://www.favreleuba.com/deep-raider-renaissance-00-20309-106-57-211), [WatchTime](https://www.watchtime.com/brands/womens-watches/favre-leuba-deep-raider-ladies-collection), [The Hour Markers](https://www.thehourmarkers.com/articles/favre-leuba-deep-raider-ladies), [titanium.lv](https://www.titanium.lv/favre-leuba-predstavila-pervuyu-zhenskuyu-kollektsiyu-chasov-deep-raider-ladies/))

Quick context if you have not followed the revival. Favre Leuba's dive story goes back to the 1964 Deep Blue, and in 2024 the brand rebooted it as the Deep Raider Revival and the more modern Deep Raider Renaissance. Since then the family has picked up malachite and meteorite dials and a mechanical power reserve model. What it never had was a smaller watch built from scratch for a different wrist. That is the gap this collection fills.

### The case and bracelet

Every reference shares the same steel package. Here is what Favre Leuba's product pages and the launch coverage confirm:

* **Case:** 34 mm stainless steel, 9.89 mm thick, 18 mm lug width, with the signature twisted lugs carried over from the bigger Deep Raider
* **Water resistance:** 100 m (10 ATM)
* **Crystal:** double domed sapphire with antireflective coating
* **Caseback:** closed, engraved with a pattern built around Favre Leuba's hourglass monogram
* **Bezel:** unidirectional rotating. Nine references get a sculpted 60 minute dive scale in steel, 4N rose gold PVD or yellow gold PVD. Two references swap the scale for 48 full cut round white diamonds, each 1.8 mm and VVS graded
* **Bracelet:** integrated three link steel bracelet with brushed and polished surfaces and a butterfly clasp. The links are slightly wider than on earlier Deep Raider bracelets, and on the rose gold and yellow gold PVD versions the polished center links get the matching treatment while the outer links stay brushed steel

That 9.89 mm number matters. A 34 mm watch with real lugs and a bezel that still slides under a cuff is exactly what this size category needs, and the twisted lugs keep it from looking like a generic shrunken diver.

The four steel wave dials are the ones I keep coming back to. Favre Leuba lays a vertical, three dimensional wave pattern under an enamel like layer, and on the two gradient versions the color fades from light to deep across the dial. It reads like water moving across the face of a dive watch, which is a smarter idea than just painting a dial pink and calling it a ladies' model. The blue aventurine is the showstopper for me: tiny shimmering flecks scattered across a deep blue dial like a night sky, framed by a rose gold PVD bezel. If you want sparkle without diamonds on the bezel, that is the one.

### The movement

Inside every reference is Favre Leuba's Swiss quartz calibre FLQ01, based on the Ronda 783, showing hours, minutes and seconds. No date. Purists will grumble about quartz in a Deep Raider, but this is the right call. It is how the case stays under 10 mm, it means the watch is always ready to wear when it comes out of the drawer, and nobody buying a 34 mm mother of pearl diver is shopping for a chronometer certificate.

**Price and availability:** the collection is open for pre booking on Favre Leuba's site, which lists delivery in November 2027, at **$1,750 to $3,750 USD**, per Favre Leuba's US product pages and WatchTime. The steel wave dials sit at the entry point, the PVD mother of pearl models at $2,100 USD, the aventurine at $2,250 USD, and the two diamond bezel models at $3,400 USD (steel) and $3,750 USD (rose gold PVD). Every reference is listed with a USD price on Favre Leuba's own site, which is the cleanest place to pre book.

## My take: who this is for

This is for the person who loves the look of a proper sport watch but finds 40 mm divers wear like hockey pucks. It is also a seriously good gift play heading into the holidays, because the price ladder is easy to read: pick a dial, pick a budget.

How does it stack up? At $1,750 USD you are paying well above an entry Swiss integrated bracelet watch. Our [Tissot PRX Powermatic 80 buyer's guide]({% post_url 2026-11-04-tissot-prx-powermatic-80-buyers-guide %}) has the 35 mm automatic at $795 USD, and that one has a mechanical movement. What the Favre Leuba gives you instead is a real 100 m dive bezel, twisted lugs that set it apart from every other small diver, diamond markers on every model and a 1737 founding date that predates almost every name in your watch box. On the other end, a small sport watch like the all silver [Nomos Club Sport neomatik 34]({% post_url 2026-11-05-piaget-warhol-camouflage-nomos-club-sport-34-dwc-terra-news %}) starts at $3,490 USD with an in house automatic, which is roughly where the diamond bezel Deep Raiders land.

So my honest read: the steel wave dials at $1,750 USD are the value sweet spot and the most original designs in the collection. The diamond bezel models are priced like jewelry, and at $3,400 to $3,750 USD you are paying for stones on quartz, so buy those because you love them, not as a smart money move.
