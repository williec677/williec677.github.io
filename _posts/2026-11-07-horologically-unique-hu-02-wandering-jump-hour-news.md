Here is a weekend story for the people who love weird, beautiful displays. Horologically Unique, a young independent brand out of Singapore, just introduced the HU-02, a wristwatch built around the wandering jumping hour that watchmaker Robert Cart patented and sold to houses like Breguet in the Art Deco era. Two versions, a 38mm lugless case, and prices that start at USD 3,850. Every number below comes from [Monochrome's Introducing piece and spec table](https://monochrome-watches.com/horologically-unique-hu-02-inspired-robert-cart-chronoscope-wandering-jump-hour-in-wristwatch-disco-volante-format-introducing-price/), checked on November 7, 2026.

## Who is Horologically Unique?

The brand started as an Instagram account, @horologically_unique, run by a collector who first planned to sell part of his collection and then decided to make the watches he actually wanted instead ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/horologically-unique-hu-02-inspired-robert-cart-chronoscope-wandering-jump-hour-in-wristwatch-disco-volante-format-introducing-price/)). Its first project, the HU-01, was an accessible take on the Patek Philippe Calatrava reference 96. The HU-02 is a much bigger swing, both mechanically and in design.

## The Robert Cart Chronoscope, explained

Robert Cart, born in 1871, set up his own manufacture in Le Locle around 1919 and 1920 and built a reputation for thin, complicated movements. His signature idea, which he called the Chronoscope, pairs a jumping hour with a wandering minute indication. The hour shows in a small window on a rotating disc that travels across a minute scale, so the window itself acts as the minute pointer. When the minute reaches 60, the hour disc jumps to the next number and the trip starts over ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/horologically-unique-hu-02-inspired-robert-cart-chronoscope-wandering-jump-hour-in-wristwatch-disco-volante-format-introducing-price/)).

Breguet, Gübelin and Vacheron Constantin all commissioned pieces using this display, usually in sharp geometric cases. Originals are rare and expensive, which is exactly the gap the HU-02 is trying to fill. The specific inspiration here is a Robert Cart for Breguet pocket watch with an ultra thin case and an arrow motif on the dial.

## Design: a tiny Art Deco object

The case is a lugless "Disco Volante" shape with the strap integrated through the caseback, a stepped bezel, and a concealed crown. Pull the strap and it almost reads as a little desk clock. Monochrome lists 38mm in diameter, 38mm lug to lug, and 11.5mm thick including the domed sapphire crystal (10.1mm without it).

The dial carries the arrow shaped guilloché style motif in the center, a geometric hour window, a sharp minute track font, and a fixed minute scale around the edge. Pure Chrysler Building energy.

## Two versions

**HU-02 Classic:** polished naval brass case with a protective coating, smooth matte cream dial, sand colored grained calf strap. Monochrome notes the brass shows a slight patina in its photos, so expect it to age. USD 3,850.

**HU-02 Silver Speciale:** polished 316L stainless steel case, two tone grained silver dial with a brushed minute track, taupe grained calf strap. USD 3,980.

## Movement and specs

* **Movement:** ETA 2824-2 automatic base, heavily modified with a custom wandering jump hour module on top
* **Jewels and frequency:** 25 jewels, 28,800 vph
* **Power reserve:** 32 hours (shorter than a stock 2824 because of the module)
* **Case:** 38mm x 38mm lug to lug x 11.5mm, sapphire display back secured by 4 screws, concealed crown
* **Water resistance:** 30m
* **Strap:** 18mm lug width, curved quick release spring bars, 16mm pin buckle
* **Price:** USD 3,850 (Classic), USD 3,980 (Silver Speciale)

Monochrome reports a limited production run sold only during a defined allocation window on the brand's website, with the details still to be confirmed. I could not find the allocation dates or the exact run size on [the brand site](https://www.horologicallyunique.com/) when I checked, so I am not quoting a number. Watch that page if you want in.

## My take

This is the kind of watch that makes independent watchmaking fun. You get a historically legit, genuinely uncommon display that used to live in pocket watches and auction catalogs, wrapped in a case with real design attitude, for under four grand. The tradeoffs are honest ones: 32 hours of reserve, 30m of water resistance, and 11.5mm of height for a dress watch. Monochrome's Brice Goulard says he would have loved a sub 9mm case but admits that would likely need a far pricier base movement. I agree. At this price the ETA plus module route is the smart call.

If the steel Silver Speciale is your pick, it is the one I would wear more. If you love patina and want the most vintage vibe, the brass Classic will tell its own story over time.
