Every big house has a tourbillon. Blancpain has something rarer, and today it made it a lot more interesting. The Carrousel Incliné takes the brand's signature one minute flying carrousel, tilts the whole cage 30 degrees, wraps it in a case machined entirely from sapphire and then backs the whole show with a chronometry promise most tourbillons never make. It is one of the most technical watches Blancpain has launched in years, and it lands in a slim 40.3 mm package. Let's break it down.

## Blancpain Carrousel Incliné

**Announced:** November 24, 2026 ([Revolution](https://revolutionwatch.com/blancpain-introduces-the-worlds-first-carrousel-incline/), [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/the-blancpain-carrousel-returnson-tilt-immaculately-finished-and-increasingly-accurate), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/blancpain-carrousel-incline-inclined-carrousel-regulating-organ-sapphire-case-video-review-price/), [SJX](https://watchesbysjx.com/2026/11/blancpain-carrousel-incline.html), [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/hands-on-with-the-all-new-blancpain-carrousel-incline-a-new-angle-on-precision/))

### Carrousel vs tourbillon, in 30 seconds

Both devices rotate the balance and escapement to average out the rate errors gravity causes when a watch sits in different vertical positions. A tourbillon drives its cage directly from the gear train. A carrousel, patented by Danish watchmaker Bahne Bonniksen in 1892, drives the cage through a separate train, so the escapement and the rotation are powered on two paths. Blancpain brought the carrousel back in 2008 with the Carrousel Volant Une Minute and its caliber 225, which Hodinkee credits as the first flying carrousel in any wristwatch, and Blancpain has been the carrousel's biggest champion ever since.

### Why tilt it?

A flat rotating cage does nothing for the watch when it is lying face up or face down. Tilt the cage and the balance keeps changing its orientation even when the watch is resting flat on a nightstand, so the averaging works in all six test positions instead of four. Greubel Forsey has proved the idea with inclined tourbillons for years. According to Monochrome, Blancpain tested inclines from 0 to 35 degrees in 5 degree steps and found 30 degrees worked best. Revolution calls the result the world's first inclined carrousel, and SJX says that to its knowledge no one has built one before.

Getting there was not simple. Because the cage sits at an angle to the mainplate, the gear train has to bend, so Blancpain splits the change of plane into two meshes of 22 and then 8 degrees. The 9 mm cage turns once a minute on a double ball bearing system that Monochrome reports is patent pending, and the cage is 8.5% lighter with 30% less rotational inertia than Blancpain's previous construction. A beak shaped pointer on the cage doubles as the seconds indicator.

### The caliber 222ISQ

* **Type:** manual winding, one minute flying carrousel inclined at 30 degrees
* **Frequency:** 28,800 vph (4 Hz), silicon hairspring, 8 mm balance; SJX describes it as a free sprung titanium balance with gold timing screws
* **Power reserve:** 100 hours from two barrels in series, with a small E/F (empty/full) wind indication on one barrel visible from the back
* **Build:** 224 components, 37 jewels, 35.2 mm wide and 5.3 mm thick, rising to 6.98 mm at the cage
* **Material:** mainplate and bridges in solid 18k 5N gold, either rhodium plated or left in red gold
* **Rate:** each watch gets an individual internal chronometry certificate with a mean daily rate of 0 to +2 seconds; Revolution reports the rate is logged in all six positions across the full 100 hour power reserve

That last number is the headline for nerds. SJX points out that 0 to +2 seconds a day puts it in the same bracket as Omega's Spirate equipped watches. Plenty of tourbillons are beautiful. Very few come with a rate certificate that tight.

### Finishing you can see from every side

Blancpain says each movement takes about 210 hours of hand finishing, with 253 sharp inward angles (not counting the stamped corners of the lower cage wheel) and more than 60 custom tools built just for this caliber. Because the case is transparent, the bridges are beveled on both faces: 30 degree bevels on top, 45 degree bevels underneath. Revolution and SJX both describe the anglage being shaped by hand with a burin and polished with gentian wood and diamond paste, which is the old school way and the only way to get a truly sharp inward corner.

### A case made of sapphire

This is Blancpain's first watch with a complete sapphire case. SJX reports it is made by Comadur, with the middle and back machined as one piece and the bezel and crystal as a second piece, no screws, and the movement going in from the front. Fratello says each of those sapphire parts takes around 100 hours to machine and polish.

* **Size:** 40.3 mm wide, 11.3 mm thick including sapphire, 46.45 mm lug to lug (Fratello)
* **Water resistance:** 30 m
* **Dial:** sapphire with applied gold Roman numerals and luminescent hands
* **Crown:** 18k gold
* **Strap:** blue grey leather with an 18k gold folding clasp; Fratello lists a 22 mm strap with quick release spring bars

### References, price and availability

* **Ref. 66222S 0000 55B:** rhodium plated 5N gold movement
* **Ref. 66222S A 0000 55B:** 5N red gold movement

**Price:** **$502,600 USD** per Hodinkee and Revolution, or CHF 380,000 before tax per SJX, Monochrome and Fratello. It is **not a limited edition**, but no annual production number has been published, and SJX lists it as available through Blancpain boutiques and retailers. Given the finishing hours alone, expect very few.

## My take

At half a million dollars this is not a watch most of us will ever strap on, so judge it on what it does for the industry. Big groups usually chase complication counts. Blancpain went the other way: one complication, executed for accuracy, with a certified rate and finishing that genuinely competes with the independents. That is the same energy that drove this year's [GPHG winners]({% post_url 2026-11-07-gphg-2026-winners-rexhep-rexhepi-aiguille-dor-news %}) and Roger W. Smith's [Series Seven]({% post_url 2026-11-19-roger-w-smith-series-seven-nomos-noir-oris-doraemon-news %}). And fitting a tilted cage into an 11.3 mm case is a real engineering flex.

My pick is the rhodium version. Inside a clear case, the cooler finish lets the bevels and the tilted cage do the talking.
