Some watch news is about what just got announced. Tonight's is about timing. Two micro brand releases that were announced earlier this month hit their make or break moments in the next 18 hours: one opens its doors at 7 PM ET tonight, and the other shuts them for good at noon ET tomorrow. If either one has been sitting in a browser tab, this is your nudge.

## Kiwame Tokyo Kubo Momiji: preorders open tonight

**When:** November 16 at 7:00 PM ET, which is 9:00 AM on November 17 in Japan ([Kiwame Tokyo product page](https://kiwametokyo.com/en/products/kubo%e7%aa%aa-momiji), [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/kiwame-tokyo-kubo-momiji/))

Kiwame Tokyo Asakusa builds its watches around the architecture and culture of its home neighborhood in Tokyo, and the Kubo collection is its smallest and most dressy line. The name comes from the Japanese character 窪, meaning a hollow or recess, and the whole design is built around that idea: a sunken small seconds display sitting between four and five o'clock, cut down into the dial instead of printed on top of it.

The Momiji, reference **KT304**, is the autumn follow up to the pink Kubo Sakura that Gear Patrol notes arrived in June. This time the dial is a deep red lacquer inspired by the traditional Japanese color Koimomiji, the shade of maple leaves late in the season. The brand says it reads warmer red in bright light and darker, almost burgundy, in shadow. Here is the detail that sold me: inside that recessed seconds well sits a raised maple leaf, painted the same red as the dial around it, so it only shows itself when light rakes across it. Kiwame says the motif is registered as a design (No. 2026-014046). The seconds hand counterweight is shaped like the roofline of Asakusa's Kaminarimon gate, the brand's signature touch.

**On the price:** Kiwame lists the Momiji at ¥84,700 before tax and VAT. At the ECB reference rate on November 16 (¥159.60 to the dollar) that works out to about **$531 USD**. Gear Patrol's launch story lists it at **$630**, and I could not confirm where that figure comes from, so check the final USD amount at checkout before you pay. Import taxes on delivery are on top either way.

**The fine print matters here.** Kiwame says preorders cannot be canceled for any reason, and there is a limit of one watch per person across the whole Kubo collection, with extra orders subject to cancellation. The brand also warns that high traffic can sell watches out during checkout and recommends setting up an express payment method like Shop Pay, Apple Pay or PayPal ahead of time. In other words, be ready at 7:00 sharp.

## Straum x TRTS Jan Mayen Titanium Stormy Seas: last call

**Window closes:** November 17 at 18:00 CET, which is **12:00 PM ET** ([Straum press release](https://straum.co/pages/newsroom/straum-trts-stormy-seas-sets-sail-one-last-time))

Norway's Straum and The Real Time Show podcast brought this one back on November 3 for a final two week run, and when the window closes tomorrow, Straum says the model will be discontinued. It first launched in November 2025, also for just two weeks, and Straum says requests to bring it back never stopped.

The story behind it is the reason it hits. During Straum's 2022 Explorers Club expedition to the Arctic island of Jan Mayen, the boat carrying co host Rob Nudds sailed into a gale that tore the mainsail in two, some 300 km from land. What stuck with him was the fluorescent orange immersion suits hanging ready against the grey blue sea. That became the watch: a textured, stamped and fumé painted grey blue dial with a Fluor Orange tipped seconds hand. "Having this watch on my wrist keeps those memories present," Nudds says in Straum's release.

New for this final run, the Stormy Seas is offered for the first time on Straum's Grade 5 titanium bracelet with micro adjust and quick release, alongside the FKM rubber strap.

Two things to know before you jump. First, this watch uses Straum's integrated coupling system with a 24.8 mm attachment, so it is not a standard lug width watch; you are buying into Straum's own straps and bracelet. Second, those US prices include taxes and duties, which makes the true landed cost easier to judge than most European micro brands.
