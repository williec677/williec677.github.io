Here is the number that should make every watch buyer sit up: according to WatchCharts, in production IWC and Panerai models now change hands for about 40% less than their sticker prices. That is not a clearance rack. That is the Portugieser Chronograph, the Top Gun and the Luminor, all sitting near half of retail on the secondary market while the rest of the industry has been climbing for almost two years. WatchCharts published its [November 2026 Watch Market Update](https://watchcharts.com/articles/p/9545/november-2026-watch-market-update) today, November 9, covering October, and it is one of the most useful buyer reports of the year.

**My take:** If you have been circling a big name Swiss watch and you do not care about waitlists or bragging rights, October's data points straight at IWC and Panerai. The brands with hype are where prices are firm. The brands without it are where your money stretches furthest.

## October in one paragraph

The WatchCharts Overall Market Index fell 0.8% in October. Rolex dropped 1.1%, Patek Philippe gave back 0.4%, and Audemars Piguet was the only one of the big three in the green, up 0.2%. Zoom out to twelve months, though, and the picture flips: Patek Philippe is the standout, up 15.6% over the past year, and the overall index is up 7.5%. So October was a small breather inside a recovery that WatchCharts dates back to January 2025, not a crash.

## Who won and who lost

Roughly half of the brands WatchCharts tracks with average secondary prices above $3,000 rose in October. The two leaders were both independents: Ming climbed 3.1% and Parmigiani Fleurier rose 1.7%. Omega was the weakest performer at a 1.7% decline, and Rolex finished fifth from the bottom after ranking third from the bottom in September.

The collection level moves tell a sharper story:

Two things jump out. First, the old Tudor Submariner climbing while the current Black Bay softens is a classic collector pattern: discontinued and vintage pieces tend to hold attention better than whatever is in the window today. Second, a 1.8% slide on the GMT Master is a small move, but if you have been shopping for one, every soft month is leverage. ## The real story: the brands the recovery skipped

WatchCharts' focus section this month is about two brands that have missed the rebound almost entirely. Its IWC Market Index is down 0.1% over the past year, and its Panerai index is up only 0.4%, against 7.5% for the overall market. For context, Cartier is up 8.3% and Omega is up 5.0% over the same stretch, and those are the brands WatchCharts treats as IWC and Panerai's closest peers on price and reach.

The interesting part is that this is not a demand problem. By WatchCharts' estimates, IWC sales grew 18% year over year and Panerai grew 9%. People are buying. They are just refusing to pay more. Meanwhile, WatchCharts says IWC has raised US retail prices by 10.8% over the past year, so the gap between the boutique and the secondary market has grown: in production IWC models now trade 41% below retail, compared with 39% a year earlier. WatchCharts also reports that more dealers are cutting their asking prices on IWC and Panerai, while the opposite is happening with Patek Philippe and Rolex.

It is also not every model. WatchCharts points out that the IWC Ingenieur is up 5.6% over the past year, while Patek's Complications collection is down 0.3% and the Rolex Sea Dweller is down 0.8%. The weakness at IWC sits in its two most traded lines, the Pilot's Watch and the Portugieser, each down 0.5%.

## The four watches WatchCharts called out

These are WatchCharts' own figures, which are market prices from its marketplace tracking compared with retail, not asking prices I picked off a listing:

| Watch | Secondary market (about) | Retail |
|---|---|---|
| IWC Portugieser Chronograph IW371605 | $5,100 | $9,200 |
| IWC Pilot's Watch Chronograph Top Gun IW389101 | $5,900 | $10,200 |
| Panerai Luminor PAM422 | $5,500 | $13,200 |
| Panerai Luminor Chrono PAM01109 | $6,100 | $10,800 |

Do the math on the Luminor PAM422 and you are looking at roughly 58% off retail for a watch from a brand with real Italian navy history. The Portugieser Chronograph is one of the most recognizable dress chronographs on the planet, and it is trading around $4,100 under its boutique price. WatchCharts' own conclusion is that as long as preowned supply keeps pace with demand, those discounts are unlikely to close quickly. That is an opinion about the future, not a guarantee, so buy because you love the watch, not as an investment.

## How to shop the discount without getting burned

A big gap to retail only helps you if the watch is right. Before you pull the trigger:

* **Check sold prices, not asking prices.** Asking prices on any marketplace run high. Filter eBay to sold listings and compare them with the WatchCharts figures above before you make an offer.
* **Confirm the exact reference.** IWC and Panerai both run many close variants, and a different dial, case material or generation can change the value a lot. Match the reference number on the paperwork to the caseback and the listing.
* **Ask for box, papers and service history.** A watch with its warranty card and a recent service is worth more than the same piece without, and it is easier to resell later.
* **Budget for the strap.** Panerai's big lug widths and IWC's pilot straps are part of the look. The [Watch Strap Finder](https://mrtictoctalk.com/strap-finder/) has fit pages for the [Panerai Luminor 44 mm](https://mrtictoctalk.com/strap-finder/panerai-luminor-44/) and the [IWC Pilot's Watch Mark XX](https://mrtictoctalk.com/strap-finder/iwc-pilot-mark-xx/), so you know the width before you buy a spare.

## The bottom line

October was a soft month, but not a scary one. The hype brands are holding, the independents are hot, and the best value in Swiss watches right now is hiding at IWC and Panerai, where buyers are getting near half price on watches with real heritage. If one of those four has been on your list, this report is your green light to start comparing sold prices.
