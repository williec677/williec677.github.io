Most skeleton watches ask you to choose: either you get color, or you get to see the movement. Arnold & Son just found a way to give you both, and it did it with a rock. This morning the brand unveiled two new Time Pyramid 42.5 editions that slice natural agate so thin that light passes right through it. Only 16 watches total will exist. Here is everything you need to know, plus a genuinely fun curveball from Jaeger-LeCoultre.

## Arnold & Son Time Pyramid 42.5 Agate Editions

**Announced:** November 17, 2026 ([Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/arnold-and-son-time-pyramid-agate), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/arnold-son-time-pyramid-42-5-agate-editions-skeleton-independent-watcmaking-introducing-price/))

Quick refresher on why the Time Pyramid is such a cult watch. Arnold & Son takes its name from John Arnold, the 18th century British watchmaker who pioneered marine chronometers. The brand was revived in 1995 and has belonged to movement specialist La Joux Perret since 2010. The Time Pyramid arrived in 2013 with a movement laid out like the old British skeleton and regulator clocks: wide at the bottom where the two barrels live, narrowing as the gear train climbs straight up the middle, and topped by the balance wheel at 12 o'clock. It literally looks like a pyramid of gears. For the model's 10th birthday in 2023, the brand shrank the case from 44.6 mm to today's 42.5 mm.

### What's new: agate you can see through

In earlier versions, the hour ring was done in materials like white opal, and recent pieces leaned into solid backdrops like aventurine and malachite. This time the suspended chapter ring with its Roman numerals is cut from agate just 0.4 mm thick, red agate for the red gold watch and blue agate for the platinum one. At that thickness the stone glows with color but still lets you see the small seconds sitting underneath it.

The same trick happens behind the movement. A 0.8 mm slice of matching agate sits under the sapphire caseback, so on the wrist it acts as a rich colored backdrop for the two power reserve indicators and the balance up top, and when you hold the watch up to the light it turns into a hazy window onto the movement. Monochrome described the effect as "like a ship in foggy weather," and honestly, that nails it.

Finishing is classic Arnold & Son: NAC treated bridges, hand applied anglage, heat blued hour and minute hands, a blued small seconds hand and blued screws. Hodinkee notes a neat detail on the double barrel setup: when you wind it through the crown at 6 o'clock, the left power reserve indicator fills first, then the right.

**On the price:** the USD figures are the US prices Hodinkee published, and the Swiss prices (including tax) come from Monochrome. Worth noticing: the US price sits well above a straight conversion of the Swiss price, which would land around $59,200 USD and $72,100 USD at the ECB rate of November 16 (0.8393 CHF to the dollar). If you have a reason to buy in Switzerland, that gap is real money. Availability is through Arnold & Son retailers.

### My take

Let's be honest, $63,400 USD is a lot of watch money, and Hodinkee itself admits eight pieces per color is a blip on the radar. But this is exactly the kind of release that keeps independent minded watchmaking interesting. The original Time Pyramids were fully see through, the aventurine and malachite ones were fully opaque, and agate lands right in the sweet spot: color on the wrist, transparency in the hand. The blue agate in platinum is the one I'd chase. Cold metal, icy stone, blued hands. That is a mood.

What I really like is that the architecture is the hero, not the stone. A 4.4 mm thick manual movement with 90 hours and a layout you can read like a diagram is a true horology lesson on your wrist. If skeleton dials are your thing, previous Time Pyramid editions do surface on the secondary market for a lot less than a brand new limited piece.

## Jaeger-LeCoultre made a video game, and it's actually hard

**Announced:** November 17, 2026 ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/jaeger-lecoultre-video-game-with-zain-memon-the-valley-of-inventions-news/), [The Valley of Inventions](https://valleyofinventions.jaeger-lecoultre.com))

Jaeger-LeCoultre, through its Made of Makers program, teamed up with game designer Zain Memon, whom Monochrome calls India's most influential game designer, on a game called **The Valley of Inventions**. You play as Elie LeCoultre, son of the founder, starting in the Manufacture in winter 1881, and work your way through almost 200 years of the brand's history.

It is built as an escape game: solve one enigma and you unlock the next. According to Monochrome, it packs 9 classic complications, 10 enigmas, 7 stories and 7 puzzles, all turning the mechanics of watchmaking into gameplay. Monochrome's team played it and says it takes real mental effort. You can play it now in your browser at the official site, and there is an app, though only on Android for now.

### My take

This is a smart move. The hardest part of getting someone into mechanical watches is explaining why any of it matters, and a puzzle game that makes you think like a watchmaker does that better than any spec sheet.
