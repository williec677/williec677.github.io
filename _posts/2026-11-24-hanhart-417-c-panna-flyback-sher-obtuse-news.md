Two very different watches, one shared idea: design you can spot from across the room. Hanhart took its most famous pilot's chronograph, poured cream all over the dial and dipped the touch points in bronze with help from a podcast that knows exactly what collectors want. And a British collector turned founder built a slim quartz rectangle whose dial looks like a sheet of folded paper. Let's get into it.

## Hanhart 417 C Panna Flyback x The Real Time Show

**Announced:** November 24, 2026 ([Hanhart](https://www.hanhart.com/en/product/417-c-panna-flyback-39-mm/), [Hodinkee](https://www.hodinkee.com/articles/hanhart-417-c-panna-flyback), [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/two-tone-hanhart-417-c-panna-flyback-chronograph-the-real-time-show-introducing-price/))

![Hanhart 417 C Panna Flyback on the wrist with a black bund strap]({{ "/assets/img/watches/hanhart-417-c-panna-flyback-wrist.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Hanhart (417 C Panna Flyback, ref. H701L.950)*

The 417 is the chronograph Hanhart built for military pilots in the 1950s, and the one Steve McQueen made famous. This version was developed with Rob Nudds and Alon Ben Joseph, hosts of The Real Time Show podcast, who helped pick the color scheme. "Panna" is Italian for cream, and Hanhart says the "C" stands for Copperhead, a nod to the copper colored bronze parts.

What makes it different from a regular 417:

* **Cream everything:** a cream dial with the historic Hanhart logo, plus Super-LumiNova Light Old Radium on the numerals, hands and bezel insert. The usual red bezel marker gives way to a cream one, which Hodinkee says is a first as far as it knows.
* **Bronze touch points:** the fluted bidirectional bezel, crown and pushers are aluminum bronze, which Hanhart says will develop its own patina over time, set against a satin and polished steel case.
* **Numbered back:** a screw down sapphire caseback engraved with the edition number out of 100.

### Specs

* **Reference:** H701L.950
* **Case:** 39 mm, 13.3 mm thick with the high domed crystal (11.55 mm without), 46 mm lug to lug, 20 mm lugs
* **Water resistance:** 10 bar (100 m)
* **Movement:** Sellita AMT5100 M, hand wound flyback chronograph with column wheel (per Monochrome), 23 jewels, 4 Hz, 58 hours, hacking seconds, 30 minute counter and small seconds
* **Accuracy:** regulated by Hanhart to 0 to +8 seconds per day across six positions
* **Strap:** calfskin bund strap in black, dark brown or light brown (S, M or L) with a pin buckle, or a stainless steel bracelet
* **Edition:** 100 pieces

![Hanhart 417 C Panna Flyback caseback showing the AMT5100 M flyback chronograph movement]({{ "/assets/img/watches/hanhart-417-c-panna-flyback-caseback.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Hanhart (sapphire caseback)*

**Price and availability:** **EUR 2,740 on the strap, about $2,997 USD**, or **EUR 2,940 on the bracelet, about $3,216 USD**, at the ECB reference rate of November 23, 2026 (EUR 1 = $1.0939). Those euro prices include 19% German VAT, so as Hodinkee notes, buyers outside the EU may pay less. Hanhart's own shop also has a currency switcher that showed higher dollar figures when we checked this morning, so confirm the final amount at checkout. The shop lists delivery for early February 2027.

**My take:** compared with the busy dials on most pilot chronographs, this is warm, legible and genuinely different, and 39 mm with a 46 mm lug to lug is the sweet spot for a flyback. ## SHER Obtuse Numeric and Pure

**Announced:** November 24, 2026 ([SHER](https://www.sherwatch.com/collections/sher-obtuse), [Time+Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/sher-obtuse-numeric-pure-introducing/))

![SHER Obtuse Pure with black dial and raised triangle markers on its integrated steel bracelet]({{ "/assets/img/watches/sher-obtuse-pure-black.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: SHER (Obtuse Pure, pre production prototype)*

SHER is the independent British brand of collector Darren Sher. His first watch was the SHER Kids Watch, with a segmented dial designed to help children read the time (Time+Tide says it came from his own experience growing up with dyslexia), followed by a dive watch made with That Watch Podcast. Time+Tide frames the Obtuse as the first SHER where he followed his own tastes all the way through, and it is a wild one.

The dial is built in three layers: a grained base, a middle layer of shaped Super-LumiNova and a top layer of raised triangles. Each triangle marks one hour and its point marks five minute intervals, so you read the time from the geometry instead of from markers. The **Numeric** uses a blue fumé dial with luminous cut out numerals. The **Pure** drops the numerals for a black textured dial that looks like origami under glass.

### Specs (from SHER's product pages)

* **Case:** 316L steel, 34 x 27 mm, 7.4 mm thick, 34 mm lug to lug (40 mm including the bracelet end links), concealed brushed crown
* **Crystal:** trapezoid sapphire with antireflective coating
* **Movement:** Swiss Ronda 762 quartz, two hands, about 10 years nominal battery life
* **Water resistance:** 50 m (5 ATM), though SHER says swimming is not recommended
* **Bracelet:** integrated 316L steel, tapering from 27 to 16 mm, with 4 mm links for fine sizing and a butterfly clasp
* **Assembly:** designed in Britain, final assembly in the United Kingdom

**Price and availability:** **$759 USD** for the first 50 watches sold across both dials, which get a Founders Edition caseback and warranty card, then **$899 USD** regular price. Preorders open on SHER's site on **Wednesday, November 25 at 8:00 AM ET**. Time+Tide reports a first run of 300 watches with first deliveries expected in April 2027; SHER's own pages only name the 50 piece Founders Edition. SHER also notes the photos show pre production prototypes and that the final bracelet will get refinements to articulation and fit.

**My take:** at 7.4 mm thick this is a true cuff slider, and the triangle dial is the most original quartz design I have seen this year. The price is in mechanical territory, so you are paying for design rather than the movement.
