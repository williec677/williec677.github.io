Albishorn built its whole reputation on chronographs that never existed: watches that look like they came out of a 1950s regatta or a Himalayan expedition, but were dreamed up today by founder Sébastien Chaulmontet. Now the Swiss independent has done something new. The **Albishorn Voisin Aérosport**, revealed on November 10, 2026, is the brand's first time only watch, and [SJX](https://watchesbysjx.com/2026/11/albishorn-voisin-aerosport.html) also notes it is the first Albishorn that lets you see the movement. It is a slim, layered sector dial chronometer that goes on sale tomorrow, and it is exactly the kind of watch that sells out quietly while everyone is busy arguing about steel sports models. I checked every number below against SJX and [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/albishorn-voisin-aerosport-retro-futuristic-chronometer-dress-watch-sector-dial-review-price/), both published today, with [Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/review/review-the-albishorn-voisin-aerosport-imagines-an-art-deco-watch-that-never-was/) running a full review the same morning. After that, two more stories from today: Jaeger-LeCoultre's eighth vintage capsule and a very affordable dress watch on Kickstarter.

Those are the brand's retail prices as reported by both outlets. There is no US dollar price in either report, so I am not going to guess one, and there is obviously no secondary market yet.

## Why a car designer's watch looks like this

The name comes from Gabriel Voisin, the French aviation pioneer who turned to building cars after the First World War under the name Avions Voisin. His most famous design is the C28 Aérosport, which Monochrome dates to the early 1930s and SJX to 1935. Albishorn's pitch is the same "imaginary vintage" idea behind its chronographs: this is the watch Voisin might have commissioned for himself back then. The brand is clear, as Monochrome quotes, that it is "not a tribute or a commemorative object," and the design does not try to copy the car. It borrows from the wristwatches of that era instead.

That shows up most in the case. The bezel is unusually wide and split into two levels, a circular grained top step and a polished, inclined lower step. The caseband and lugs repeat that stepped construction with an inserted grained ring. SJX calls the case "sumptuously sculpted," and from the photos I believe it. The crown at 10 o'clock is an Albishorn signature carried over from the chronographs. Monochrome admits it may not be the easiest for winding, but it should keep the crown off the back of your hand.

## The dial is the reason to buy it

Sector dials are everywhere right now, but most are flat prints. This one is built on three levels. At the bottom sits a 180 degree running seconds gauge near 4 o'clock. The middle layer carries the colored plates and blued steel hands. On top, a raised, satin brushed chapter ring with polished edges holds the hours and minutes in blue print. SJX explains the trick in the seconds display: a double ended seconds hand, where each end sweeps half of every minute in turn. SJX compares it to the format on the Daniel Roth Tourbillon, and it is still a rare sight.

Classic or Ice Blue? SJX prefers the Classic with its pad printed middle layer because it feels more like a real artifact from the early twentieth century, while the Ice Blue gets an embossed middle layer with raised markings. Personally, the Classic is the one I would put my name down for. It looks like the watch was sitting in a drawer since 1935.

## The movement: AMT based, and finally visible

The ALB04 M is built from an ébauche by Manufacture AMT, the arm of Sellita that makes bespoke calibers for small independents. Worth knowing for full transparency: Monochrome points out that Chaulmontet is also Director of Innovation and Marketing at Sellita and AMT. The same large base turns up in Massena LAB watches, and SJX notes a similar layout to the AMT6600 in the Havid Nagan Classic One, rotated here to put the crown at 10. It is COSC certified, runs at 4 Hz with 60 hours of reserve, and Monochrome lists radiating Geneva stripes, double snailing on the winding wheels and perlage on the main plate. SJX calls the finishing "industrial but appealing," which is a fair and honest read at this price.

## My take

At about $4,209 USD (MSRP CHF 3,450) for a 38 mm, under 10 mm, COSC certified hand wound watch with this much dial depth, the Voisin Aérosport is a strong value. SJX points out it costs almost the same as Albishorn's chronographs, so you are trading a complication for refinement, and in a dress watch that trade makes sense. The limits are real: 50 m of water resistance, only 99 per color spread over three years, and a crown position some people will not love. If sector dials are your thing, though, this is one of the most interesting ones of the year. ## Also new: Jaeger-LeCoultre The Collectibles VIII, America's vintage JLC

Jaeger-LeCoultre's The Collectibles program sells restored vintage pieces straight from the brand, and the eighth capsule marks the 250th anniversary of the United States by focusing on watches made for, or because of, the American market. [Time and Tide](https://timeandtidewatches.com/jaeger-lecoultres-the-collectibles-capsule-news/) walked through it with Matthieu Sauret, Jaeger-LeCoultre's Product and Heritage director, and the highlights are a lot of fun:

* A 1930s steel Reverso signed LeCoultre only, which Sauret calls very rare, with a dial that "was born chocolate brown," not aged that way. It is included even though it was not made or sold in America.
* A 1947 Triple Calendar with moon phase, the VXN code tied to the Vacheron relationship that handled US distribution, and rare English day and month discs.
* An 18k gold LeCoultre Rectangular Triple Calendar, one of only two the brand found made in gold.
* A Futurematic in an American made case nicknamed the Nautilus, which Sauret calls "the Nautilus before the Nautilus."
* A 14k gold Beetle case, the Deep Sea Alarm pair (the Barracuda and the Shark chronograph, the Shark with its original strap and buckle), a 1970s American made Memovox with a Bakelite bezel, and a coin watch built from a $10 gold coin cut in half.

The best detail is the tariff story: Sauret explained that to get around US tariffs, "the case, the dial, the hands and the calibre travelled separately," which is why so many of these watches wear American cases. The capsule is on view only at the Jaeger-LeCoultre Madison Avenue boutique in New York City until November 20, but Time and Tide says clients worldwide can buy the pieces unless they have already sold. No prices were published, so I am not listing any. ## Also new: Angelier's $537 (EUR 479) Calatrava style dress watch

Dutch independent Angelier, whose first watch was the 100 piece Classique with a La Joux Perret movement, is back with a cheaper, smaller model on Kickstarter, as [Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/angelier-on-kickstarter-accessible-37mm-vintage-calatrava-automatic-dress-watch-review-price/) reports. The design leans on 1930s and 1940s dress watches, with Monochrome naming the Patek Philippe Calatrava 96 as the clear reference point.

* Case: 37.2 mm 316L steel, 8.7 mm thick with a box sapphire, 45.9 mm lug to lug, solid screwed back, 50 m
* Dials: Frosted Black, Frosted Ivory, Tuxedo and Sunburst Blue, all no date, with applied Breguet style numerals drawn by the founder
* Movement: Miyota 9039, automatic, 28,800 vph, 42 hours
* Strap: 20 mm calfskin tapering to 16 mm, quick release
* Price: about $537 USD (EUR 479) standard pledge, about $784 USD (EUR 699) planned retail, shipping and taxes extra, delivery estimated for April 2027

Monochrome says the campaign has already passed its EUR 50,000 goal (about $56,070 USD) and is nearly over, so if a 37 mm no date dress watch for under about $560 USD (EUR 500) sounds right, move fast. The usual crowdfunding caution applies: you are pledging, not buying from stock, and delivery is months away.
