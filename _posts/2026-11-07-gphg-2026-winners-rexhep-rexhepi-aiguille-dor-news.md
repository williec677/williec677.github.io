The biggest night in watchmaking just crowned an independent. At the 26th Grand Prix d'Horlogerie de Genève on November 7, 2026, Rexhep Rexhepi's RRCHF Chronograph Flyback won the Aiguille d'Or, the GPHG's top prize for the best watch of the year. It was a huge evening for the little guys, too: Cleguer, Dominique Renaud, Ferdinand Berthoud and Otsuka Lotec all walked away with trophies. The winners list below comes from [Revolution's full prize list](https://revolutionwatch.com/the-20-winners-of-the-2026-gphg-awards/) and [Monochrome's breaking report](https://monochrome-watches.com/rexhep-rexhepi-rrchf-chronograph-flyback-wins-gphg-2026-aiguille-dor-plus-all-prize-winners/), both checked the night of the ceremony.

## The Aiguille d'Or: Rexhep Rexhepi RRCHF Chronograph Flyback

If you follow independent watchmaking, you already know the name. Rexhepi's Akrivia and RRCC watches are some of the hardest pieces in the world to get, and now his very first flyback chronograph has taken the trophy every brand wants.

Here is what makes it special, per Revolution. The RRCHF runs an all new, manually wound, integrated flyback chronograph caliber with a column wheel, a horizontal clutch and an instantaneous jumping minute counter. That movement holds 320 components and 72 hours of power reserve, and Rexhepi fits it into a case that is 38.8mm wide and only 9.7mm thick. The platinum version wears a storm blue Grand Feu enamel dial made in his own workshop with smoked sapphire subdials, and the time display is set off center so the chronograph owns the stage.

Monochrome's Brice Goulard points to the finishing as the knockout: deep Geneva stripes, symmetrical bridge layout, and sharp inward angles on the bevels that only top hand finishers can pull off. He also compares the win to 2022, when another indie chronograph, the MB&F Legacy Machine Sequential EVO, took the top prize. Last year's Aiguille d'Or went to the Breguet Classique Souscription 2025.

My take: this was the right call. A compact, wearable chronograph built from scratch by a small Geneva workshop beat the most complicated watches in the room. That tells you where the collector world's heart is right now.

## Every GPHG 2026 winner

This year's competition had 84 nominated watches across 14 categories, with 20 prizes handed out. The format changed in a big way, too: new Essential, Complication and Gemset categories replaced the old men's and ladies' divisions. Revolution founder Wei Koh served as jury president for the first time, leading a 24 person jury, with the wider GPHG Academy vote counting for a third of the result.

## The winners worth knowing about

**Grand Seiko Ushio 300 Diver (Sports Watch).** This is the one most of us could actually buy. It runs Caliber 9RB1, a Spring Drive with Grand Seiko's Ultra Fine Accuracy rating of plus or minus 20 seconds a year, in a 40.8mm High Intensity Titanium case with 300m of water resistance. Revolution says it is Grand Seiko's smallest diver yet, with a new clasp that has both micro adjustment and a diving extension. A properly sized, crazy accurate titanium diver winning a sports prize is a big deal for Grand Seiko fans.

**Parmigiani Fleurier Tonda PF Chronographe Mystérieux (Chronograph).** Five coaxial hands stack on top of each other at rest, so it looks like a clean three hand Tonda PF. Hit the monopusher and they split apart to time things, then disappear back into one stack. The Caliber PF053 uses a triple clutch setup with one vertical and two horizontal clutches. Genuinely clever.

**Chopard L.U.C 1860 (Time Only).** A 36.5mm Lucent Steel dress watch with a hand guilloché blue dial and the micro rotor L.U.C 96.40 L inside, around 65 hours of reserve, COSC certified and stamped with the Geneva Seal, which is rare on a steel watch.

**Audemars Piguet Royal Oak RD#5 (Iconic Watch).** A 39mm titanium Jumbo hiding a selfwinding flying tourbillon chronograph. AP also won Calendar and Astronomy with the 150 Heritage, a 50mm platinum pocket watch with 47 functions and the 1,099 part Caliber 1150.

**Cleguer Inspiration One (Complication).** A debut watch from founder Mathieu Cleguer built around his own Innate escapement, a self starting take on the natural escapement, under a 12.9mm balance in a 38.5mm case. Winning a GPHG with your first watch is wild.

**Otsuka Lotec No.8 (Petite Aiguille).** My favorite story of the night. This Japanese indie shows the hours through a jumping window and the minutes with a retrograde "fader" inspired by analog mixing consoles. The 62 part module sits on a Miyota base, which kept it inside the category's about $5,971 USD (CHF 4,900) ceiling.

**SpaceOne X Baltic Seconde Majeure (Challenge).** A jumping hour collaboration with the movement architecture out in the open, built around a mechanism by independent watchmaker Théo Auffret. The Challenge prize rewards strong ideas at accessible prices, and this one fits that bill.

**Blancpain Grande Double Sonnerie (Mechanical Exception).** Eight years of work, 1,053 components, grande and petite sonnerie, minute repeater, retrograde perpetual calendar and flying tourbillon. Pure flex.

## Also new today: Archi Works X AVW The Diplomat 1.87

Not every great watch needs a trophy. Paris micro brand Archi Works and Amsterdam Vintage Watches, the shop run by Jasper Lijfering, dropped The Diplomat ref. 1.87 today, per [Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/archi-works-x-avw-the-diplomat-1-87/). It is a 35mm steel dress watch, 10.05mm thick with the crystal, with 19mm lugs and 50m of water resistance. Inside is a hand wound La Joux Perret D100 Soigné based on the Peseux 7001, at 21,600 vph with 50 hours of reserve. Two dials, Aurora with lab grown emerald baguettes and Azure with lab grown sapphire baguettes, each limited to 100 pieces at €2,250 before VAT. The reference number nods to AVW's founding year, 1987. Fratello says it is sold only through the Archi Works website and ships next week, not a preorder.
