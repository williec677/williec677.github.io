Friday night and Omega just did the most Omega thing possible. No launch event, no press blast, just a row of "new" tags appearing next to some of the oldest Aqua Terras in the catalog. Look closer and the classic teak dial models are wearing a completely different bracelet. Then, from the far end of the design spectrum, ATOWAK sent its spaceship inspired Cosmofleet back into orbit with a white composite case and the first movement it has developed entirely in house. One is the ultimate stealth upgrade, the other is pure science fiction. Let's get into both.

## Omega Seamaster Aqua Terra 150M teak dial: new bracelet and clasp

**Reported:** November 20, 2026 ([Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/omega-aqua-terra-new-bracelet/), [Omega](https://www.omegawatches.com/en-us/watches/seamaster/aqua-terra-150m/gents-collection/product), [r/OmegaWatches](https://www.reddit.com/r/OmegaWatches/comments/1wjsroe/teak_dial_aqua_terra_with_the_new_bracelet/))

![Omega Seamaster Aqua Terra 41 mm with black teak dial on the new steel bracelet]({{ "/assets/img/watches/omega-aqua-terra-teak-new-bracelet.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: Omega via Gear Patrol (Aqua Terra 150M 41 mm, black teak dial)*

Collectors on r/OmegaWatches spotted teak dial Aqua Terras on the newer bracelet showing up on Omega's website, and today Gear Patrol got the story straight from Omega. Gear Patrol counts eight stainless steel references now marked "new": the blue, black, green and white teak dials in both 38 mm and 41 mm. These are the Aqua Terras with the horizontal stripes inspired by the wooden decks of sailing yachts, the ones that made the line famous.

At first glance it looks like a clasp swap. Omega told Gear Patrol it goes further than that. Here is what changed, per Gear Patrol:

* **The whole bracelet is new.** It integrates more closely with the case and tapers by 2 mm from the end links to the clasp.
* **Rounder links, tighter tolerances.** The links were redesigned to be rounder and fit closer together, so the bracelet drapes more fluidly. Outer links stay brushed and center links stay polished.
* **Micro adjustable clasp.** The butterfly clasp now offers 2 mm of on the fly adjustment, so you can loosen it on a hot afternoon without reaching for a tool.
* **Not brand new tech.** It is the same bracelet already used on newer non teak Aqua Terras, such as the black lacquer and turquoise dial models from 2024.
* **Not the whole family.** Gear Patrol notes the Summer Blue and the Small Seconds models still use the older bracelet and clasp.

On Omega's US site, the references I could confirm with the new label include the 41 mm black teak **220.10.41.21.01.003**, blue teak **220.10.41.21.03.007** and green teak **220.10.41.21.10.002**, plus the 38 mm blue teak **220.10.38.20.03.006**. Nothing else changes under the hood. Omega's spec sheet for the 41 mm lists:

* **Case:** 41 mm steel, 13.26 mm thick, 47.91 mm lug to lug, 20 mm between the lugs, about 150 g on the bracelet
* **Dial:** sun brushed horizontal teak pattern, date at 6, rhodium plated hands and indexes with white Super-LumiNova
* **Crystal:** domed sapphire with antireflective treatment on both sides, plus a sapphire caseback
* **Movement:** Omega Co-Axial Master Chronometer caliber 8900, self winding, 60 hours of power reserve, METAS certified
* **Water resistance:** 150 m

The 38 mm runs the Master Chronometer caliber 8800 instead, and it takes a 19 mm strap. Our Strap Finder has both sizes mapped out: [Aqua Terra 41 mm]({{ "/strap-finder/omega-aqua-terra-41/" | relative_url }}) and [Aqua Terra 38 mm]({{ "/strap-finder/omega-aqua-terra-38/" | relative_url }}).

**Price and availability:** available now at **$7,400 USD** on the steel bracelet for all eight references, per Gear Patrol and Omega's US product pages. The 41 mm teak dial on a leather strap is listed at $6,700 USD. For context, WatchCharts last recorded a $7,100 retail price in September 2026 for the outgoing white dial 41 mm, ref. 220.10.41.21.02.001, and its WatchCharts market price estimate was $4,784 as of November 19. That is an estimate, not a verified sale, but it tells you the old bracelet versions trade well under retail.

**My take:** this is the update Aqua Terra fans have been asking for since the 2024 models got the good bracelet first. The polished center links will still divide people, but the rounder links tame the shine, and a micro adjust clasp on a daily watch is a genuine quality of life upgrade. If you want one at sticker, buy new. If you are value hunting, the outgoing references on the older bracelet are where the deals live. Thinking about the rest of the Omega lineup? ## ATOWAK Cosmofleet Ascent

**Released:** November 20, 2026 ([ATOWAK](https://atowak.com/products/cosmofleet-ascent-green), [WatchPro](https://www.watchpro.com/atowak-cosmofleet-ascent-wandering-hours-watch/), [Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/quick-release/atowak-launches-the-second-generation-cosmofleet-ascent/), [aBlogtoWatch](https://www.ablogtowatch.com/atowak-returns-to-the-stars-with-an-updated-cosmofleet-ascent-watch-featuring-an-in-house-movement-and-host-of-updates/))

![ATOWAK Cosmofleet Ascent green with white composite case and three green wandering hour satellites on a green leather strap]({{ "/assets/img/watches/atowak-cosmofleet-ascent-green.webp" | relative_url }})
*Image: ATOWAK (Cosmofleet Ascent Green)*

ATOWAK builds watches that look like they were pulled off a starship's control panel, and the Cosmofleet is its take on the wandering hours complication. Two years after the original, the second generation Ascent keeps the signature display and reworks almost everything around it. Quick disclosure on sourcing: Worn & Wound and aBlogtoWatch both ran this launch as sponsored posts, so I cross checked the numbers against ATOWAK's own store and WatchPro's editorial coverage.

How the display works: three hour satellites orbit the dial and rotate on their own axes, and the satellite carrying the current hour points at a 120 degree minute track along the bottom of the dial. Here is what changed:

* **Movement:** the new caliber AK-A01 automatic, the first movement ATOWAK has developed fully in house, three years in the making. Power reserve jumps from about 38 hours on the original to **65 hours**, with a stated accuracy of ±5 seconds per day. aBlogtoWatch credits a new ATOFLEX mainspring and ATOCHRON hairspring, plus a ceramic ball bearing rotor.
* **Star wheel module:** 37 components with tolerances down to 0.005 mm, built mainly from Grade 5 titanium, which aBlogtoWatch says cuts the module's weight by about 65 percent. Instead of the usual Maltese cross, ATOWAK uses a cylindrical drive inspired by traditional Chinese irrigation waterwheels.
* **Case:** white composite fiber replaces the original dark titanium. It measures 39 mm by 46.5 mm and 15.7 mm thick, and weighs just 84.9 g on leather. aBlogtoWatch reports 135 separate processes and nearly 4.5 hours of machining per case.
* **Crystal and caseback:** a sculpted sapphire crystal with 11 shaped edges, meant to look like a spacecraft window, and a steel caseback with a sapphire display window.
* **Dial:** multi layered copper with a fish scale texture and sandblasted finish, per Worn & Wound, with hand applied Super-LumiNova on the numerals and minute markers.
* **Straps:** color matched leather with a squared profile, secured by screw bars, plus a spare white leather strap and a tool for swapping.

**Price and availability:** **$3,799 USD** for either the green or purple version on ATOWAK's store, available now. Worn & Wound reports each color is limited to **150 pieces**. ATOWAK lists water resistance of **3 ATM / 30 m**, so treat this as a desk watch, not a swimmer.

**My take:** an in house movement with 65 hours of reserve and a genuinely unusual complication for under $4,000 is a bold flex for a young brand. The white composite case is love it or hate it, but it makes the green and purple satellites pop like cockpit lights.
