If you have ever asked a watch person what your first automatic should be, there is a good chance you heard one word: Seiko. And for the last several years, the specific Seiko they meant was very often the 5 Sports SRPD, the watch the community nicknamed the 5KX.

It is easy to see why. You get a real in house automatic movement, a dive style bezel, a 100m rating, and a look that borrows heavily from the legendary SKX007, all for an MSRP of $350 USD at Seiko's own US store. Let me break down exactly what you are getting, which version to buy, and where the SKX comparison holds up and where it does not.

## Where the SRPD came from

The Seiko 5 story goes back further than most people realize. According to [Seiko's 2019 launch announcement](https://www.seikowatches.com/uk-en/news/20190807-50002270216), the first Seiko 5, the Sportmatic 5, was born in 1963 as Japan's first automatic day date watch. The "5" stood for five promises: an automatic movement, a day date display at 3 o'clock, water resistance, a recessed crown at 4 o'clock, and a case and bracelet built for durability. Seiko 5 Sports followed in 1968.

On August 7, 2019, Seiko announced a completely redesigned Seiko 5 Sports collection, with the watches available from September 2019. The SRPD models were the heart of that launch. Seiko's own US site still files them under "5 Sports SKX series," so the family resemblance is official.

Two things jump out. First, 46mm lug to lug on a 42.5mm watch is compact. The SRPD wears smaller than the diameter suggests, which is a big part of why it works on so many wrists. Second, the 4R36 gives you hacking and hand winding, two features that matter more than you think when you are setting the time or picking the watch up after a weekend off.

## Which SRPD should you buy?

Seiko made the SRPD in a big range of colors. Here are the core models I checked on Seiko's US site on November 13, 2026, with their MSRPs.

* **[SRPD55](https://www.seikowatches.com/us-en/products/5sports/srpd55), black dial and black bezel on steel, MSRP $350 USD.** The classic. This is the one that looks the most like an SKX007, and it goes with everything.
* **[SRPD51](https://www.seikowatches.com/us-en/products/5sports/srpd51), blue dial and blue bezel on steel, MSRP $350 USD.** A little more color without getting loud.
* **[SRPD53](https://www.seikowatches.com/us-en/products/5sports/srpd53), blue dial with a blue and red bezel on steel, MSRP $350 USD.** The Pepsi look for a tiny fraction of what a Rolex Pepsi costs.
* **[SRPD63](https://www.seikowatches.com/us-en/products/5sports/srpd63), green dial and green bezel on steel, MSRP $350 USD.** My favorite of the bunch. The green is rich and it has a little vintage warmth.
* **[SRPD65](https://www.seikowatches.com/us-en/products/5sports/srpd65), black dial with a hard coated case and a dark bracelet, MSRP $395 USD.** The stealth option.
* **[SRPD79](https://www.seikowatches.com/us-en/products/5sports/srpd79), black with a hard coated case on a nylon strap, MSRP $375 USD.** At 94g, it is much lighter than the bracelet models.

If you are buying one SRPD and only one, start with the SRPD55 or the SRPD63. Both are timeless, both are steel, and both sit at the base price.

## SRPD vs SKX007: the honest comparison

The SRPD is often called the SKX007's replacement. That is half true. It replaced the SKX in Seiko's affordable dive style lineup and kept the general look, but it is not the same kind of watch.

Here is what changed, based on [namokiMODS' side by side comparison](https://www.namokimods.com/blogs/namokitimes/head-to-head-skx-vs-5kx-aka-srpd-seiko-5-sports):

* **Movement: SRPD wins.** The SKX007 used the 7S26, which could not hack or hand wind. The SRPD's 4R36 does both.
* **Water resistance: SKX wins.** The SKX007 was rated to 200m and carried ISO dive watch certification. The SRPD is rated to 100m with no ISO certification.
* **Crown: SKX wins.** The SKX007 had a screw down crown. The SRPD does not.
* **Case back: SRPD wins for looks.** The SRPD has a display case back so you can watch the 4R36 work.

So what does that mean for you? For swimming, showering, and daily wear, 100m is plenty. If you are a serious diver who wants an ISO rated tool, look at Seiko's Prospex line instead. For everyone else, the SRPD is the better daily watch thanks to the movement upgrade.

I already wrote about Seiko's most recent twist on the SKX formula, the SKX GMT, in my [HDB016 news post](/seiko-5-sports-skx-gmt-hdb016-dr-pepper-news/). If you want a second time zone, that is worth a look.

## The 4R36 in real life

The 4R36 is not about chronometer bragging rights. It is a simple, proven Seiko workhorse, and it powers every SRPD in this guide.

Two practical tips:

1. **Wind it when you pick it up.** Approximately 41 hours of reserve means a watch left off over a weekend will stop. A dozen or so turns of the crown in the winding position gets it going before you set it.
2. **Use the hacking seconds.** Pull the crown to the time setting position and the seconds hand stops, so you can set the watch to a reference clock.

## Straps and mods

The SRPD is one of the most customizable watches in the world. The 22mm lug width opens up a huge range of rubber, NATO, and canvas straps, and the case shape has become a favorite of the modding community.

Before you order a strap, confirm the size on my [Seiko 5 Sports SRPD Strap Finder page](/strap-finder/seiko-5-sports-srpd/) or the full list of [22mm compatible watches](/lug-width/22mm/). ## Final verdict

The Seiko 5 Sports SRPD is not an SKX007 clone, and that is fine. It is a modern, compact, hacking and hand winding automatic with a great case and endless options, for an MSRP of $350 USD. If this is your first automatic, it is one of the smartest places to start. If you already own a few watches, the SRPD63 in green is the perfect weekend beater.
